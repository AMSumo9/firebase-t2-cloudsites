Material Decisions After the Engineered Stone Ban

Benchtop selection now involves compliance and design. Engineered stone with 1% or more crystalline silica has been banned for manufacture, supply, processing, and installation across Australia since 1 July 2024. Low-silica alternatives include porcelain, sintered stone, natural granite or marble, solid timber, stainless steel, laminate, or low-silica engineered stone (under 1% crystalline silica). The choice depends on how the room is used: busy family cooking, entertaining, rental durability, visual match with cabinetry, edge profile, splashback choice, and cleaning expectations. Inquire about proposed products, templating, and whether the quote separates costs for cabinetry, benchtop, splashback, appliances, and trade allowances.

Every project starts with a measure-up and an itemised, fixed-scope quote, with design, materials, and lighting planned before cabinetry is made. Check if demolition, rough-in, plumbing, electrical, tiling, appliances, and clean-up are included. Ask what drawings will be supplied before cabinetry production, confirm the proposed benchtop material and its compliance with silica rules, and inquire about warranties and compliance paperwork.

Common questions

What is the timeline for a typical kitchen renovation in Eastern Melbourne? A full kitchen renovation usually takes between 6 to 12 weeks from contract signing to handover. This includes approximately 4 to 6 weeks for cabinetry and stone fabrication, followed by 2 to 4 weeks of on-site work for installation, plumbing, electrical, tiling, and finishing.

What are the alternatives to engineered stone for kitchen benchtops? Following the ban on high-silica engineered stone, popular alternatives include porcelain, sintered stone, natural granite, natural marble, solid timber, stainless steel, laminate, and engineered stone with less than 1% crystalline silica. Each offers different aesthetics, durability, and maintenance requirements.

What should be included in a kitchen renovation quote? A comprehensive quote should be itemised and clearly state all inclusions and exclusions. It should detail costs for design, materials, cabinetry, benchtops, splashbacks, appliances, and trade labour (plumbing, electrical, tiling). It should also outline the process for variations and the handover documentation.
