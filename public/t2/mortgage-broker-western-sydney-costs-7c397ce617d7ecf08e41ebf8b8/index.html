Deposit size and lenders mortgage insurance

Your deposit size directly impacts the cost of your loan. Most lenders require at least a 5 percent genuine savings deposit to approve a home loan. If you borrow more than 80 percent of the property value, lenders usually require you to pay lenders mortgage insurance. This is a one-off premium that protects the lender if you default on the loan. It is typically capitalised into the loan amount rather than paid upfront. You can often avoid LMI by saving a 20 percent deposit or using a guarantor. Government schemes like the First Home Guarantee may also allow eligible buyers to purchase with a 5 percent deposit and avoid this cost, subject to lender approval. Knowing your deposit position early helps you plan for these additional expenses.

Serviceability and the APRA buffer

Borrowing power is determined by your income, existing debts, and living expenses. Lenders use the higher of your declared expenses or the Household Expenditure Measure to assess your budget. They then test your repayments at the actual interest rate plus the APRA 3 percent prudential buffer. This buffer ensures you can still meet repayments if rates rise. For borrowers in Western Sydney, understanding this calculation is crucial for estimating how much you can borrow. A broker can review your full situation to ensure your numbers are realistic before you apply. This review helps prevent rejection due to serviceability issues. Common questions

Do mortgage brokers charge a fee in Australia? For most standard home loans, mortgage brokers do not charge you a direct fee. They are typically paid a commission by the lender once your loan settles. For complex lending scenarios, such as some commercial or low-doc loans, a fee may apply, and that should be disclosed in writing up front.

What is Lenders Mortgage Insurance and when does it apply? Lenders Mortgage Insurance is a one-off insurance premium that protects the lender if you default on a loan where you borrow more than 80 percent of the property value. It is usually capitalised into the loan and is not a personal protection product for the borrower.
