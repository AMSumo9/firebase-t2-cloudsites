Questions to ask before choosing a broker

A broker can compare useful options only when the borrower gives clean information and asks direct questions. Before submitting an enquiry, write down the amount needed, the purpose, preferred repayment comfort, existing debts, income position and any timing pressure.

Use questions that reveal process, not just confidence:

  • Which lenders are being considered and why?
  • Does the broker deal with the specific loan type I need?
  • What documents should I prepare before an application?
  • How are broker commissions or fees disclosed?
  • What happens if the first lender does not suit my circumstances?
  • Will I receive a comparison of options, or one recommendation only?

For investment loans, ask how interest-only structures, loan-to-value ratio and lenders mortgage insurance may affect the discussion, without assuming any fixed threshold applies. For business or equipment finance, ask what trading records, invoices, asset details or security questions the broker expects to review.

Reading lender access claims carefully

The published page states that partner brokers work with major banks including CBA, ANZ, Westpac, NAB and BankSA, as well as specialist lenders. A broad lender panel can be useful context, but the borrower still needs to know which lenders are actually relevant to the application in front of them.

Do not treat lender count as an outcome by itself. A borrower with stable income, an investor with existing debts, a self-employed applicant and a business seeking equipment finance may all need different documentation and different product comparisons. The value comes from matching the lender shortlist to the borrower profile.

ASIC Moneysmart publishes consumer guidance on home loans and property investment. In a broker conversation, use that as a reason to ask for repayment examples, comparison reasons, fee disclosure and a clear explanation of why a recommended option is suitable for the stated purpose.

  1. Define the loan purpose. Choose the closest category from home loan, investment loan, car finance, business loan, commercial finance or equipment finance before making an enquiry.
  2. Prepare borrower details. Collect income, debt, asset, property or business information so the broker can assess options against real circumstances.
  3. Check broker fit. Ask whether the broker regularly handles the exact type of finance you need in Adelaide or South Australia.
  4. Request lender reasoning. Ask which lenders are being compared and why those options suit the application.
  5. Confirm disclosure. Ask how fees, commissions, obligations and next steps will be explained before any application proceeds.
