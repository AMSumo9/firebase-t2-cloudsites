Where the Scope Usually Splits

The published service mix includes local SEO, eCommerce SEO, technical SEO, Google Ads, content marketing, link building, SEO audit, keyword research and small business SEO. A suburb based service business trying to appear in Maps needs a different first conversation from an online store with weak category pages or a professional firm with slow, hard to crawl service pages.

For a service area business, useful early questions concern the Google Business Profile, local directory listings, suburb level landing pages and review strategy. For an eCommerce business, the page points to category, product and intent matched content strategies. For an established site that has stalled, the better first step may be a technical and content audit rather than another batch of blog posts.

If the quote treats every business the same, press for the order of work. Keyword research, on page tune ups, link earning and content writing can sit inside one retainer, but the first month should still identify which constraint is costing the most visibility.

Local Search Without Suburb Stuffing

Melbourne locality is most useful when it changes the search task. The published material gives examples such as improving Google Maps and local pack visibility for a suburb or service area, and it names Box Hill as a Maps example. That separates local search from a broad informational keyword or a national online retail term.

A grounded local plan should decide which pages deserve suburb intent, which services need broader Melbourne intent, and how the Google Business Profile, reviews and directory consistency support those pages. A related Melbourne SEO audit guide can also help buyers frame diagnostic work before they compare longer retainers.

Avoid asking for a long list of location pages without a reason. Better questions are whether each page has a distinct service area purpose, whether it answers the buyer's local question, and whether it is supported by internal links from relevant service pages.

Cost, Timing and Guarantees

The published page gives clear price and timing signals. It says Melbourne SEO retainers typically run from $1,200 to $4,000+GST per month depending on competition, scope and the number of target keywords. It also says project based audits start from around $1,500. Those numbers are starting points for a scope conversation, not proof that the lowest or highest quote is automatically right.

Timing should be judged by competition. The page says most Melbourne businesses see measurable ranking movement in 3 to 6 months for mid competition keywords, while tougher law, finance and national online retail keywords can take 9 to 12 months. Early reports should show completed technical fixes, content improvements, links earned or keyword mapping, even before revenue changes are clear.

The page also says it will not guarantee first page rankings because Google's algorithm is the ranking authority. A stronger promise is a documented method, monthly reporting tied to completed work, and a realistic timeline before work starts.

Who This Applies To

This guide is for Melbourne businesses that already have a website and need a clearer way to compare SEO proposals. It applies especially to local service firms, small businesses, eCommerce stores, and organisations deciding whether to start with an audit or a monthly retainer.

It is less useful for a brand that wants paid search only, because Google Ads is presented as a way to capture high intent buyers while organic SEO builds momentum. If immediate paid traffic is the only goal, the scope should be separated from the organic work so reporting stays clean.

The best fit is a buyer willing to share the site, target services and suburb or service area, then judge the written audit on specificity. If the audit names crawl, indexation, page experience, internal links, backlinks and competitor gaps in plain English, it gives the business a firmer basis for the next conversation.

  1. Start with the audit. Use the written audit to identify crawl, indexation, content, link and competitor gaps before discussing a retainer.
  2. Match scope to the business model. Separate local, eCommerce, technical, content and paid search needs so the proposal solves the first constraint.
  3. Check the timeline. Compare the promised timing with the published 3 to 6 month and 9 to 12 month guidance.
  4. Review reporting language. Ask for monthly reporting tied to completed work, not only traffic graphs or broad ranking claims.
