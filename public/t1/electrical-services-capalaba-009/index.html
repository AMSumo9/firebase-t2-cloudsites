That structure separates work that begins with product choice, such as a ceiling fan or fixed light, from work that begins with diagnosis, such as repeated tripping or power loss.

  • For a new or moved power point, record the room, wall location and nearby fixtures.
  • For lighting or ceiling fans, note whether existing wiring is present and whether the fitting is fixed.
  • For tripping or power loss, describe the affected area and the action that caused the fault.
  • For a switchboard question, record labels, affected circuits and unclear protection devices without opening the board.

Safety Boundaries Before An Enquiry

The source page is careful about immediate hazards. It says a tripping switch, hot outlet or unexpected power loss deserves a safe assessment, and that people should not open a board or work on fixed wiring. If the situation is an immediate hazard, it directs readers to emergency services or the electricity distributor.

That boundary shapes the enquiry. The note should describe the symptom, not attempt to diagnose behind the faceplate or inside the switchboard. Useful wording might say that a switch will not reset, that one area has lost power, or that a particular appliance change happened shortly before the issue began. It should not include attempted wiring work.

For smoke alarm work, the source gives one concrete compliance point: compliant alarms are photoelectric, interconnected and meet AS 3786-2014. It also says the required power supply and replacement choice depend on the dwelling and the existing alarm arrangement, so a good enquiry should describe what is already installed rather than assume the replacement type.

What To Ask Before Work Is Accepted

An enquiry form is not the same as a booking. The source page states that submitting details does not confirm availability, timing or acceptance of the work. Readers should treat the first response as a scoping conversation, especially where the job may involve fixed wiring, switchboard assessment or fault investigation.

Good questions keep the conversation grounded. Ask whether the person assessing the job is appropriately licensed for the work. Ask what information is needed before attendance. Ask whether photos will help, since the source page notes that photos can be provided later if asked for them. Ask what access is needed around the room, board, ceiling space or external area, without attempting to prepare wiring yourself.

  • Confirm whether the job is installation, assessment or fault investigation.
  • Ask what must be switched off or left untouched before attendance.
  • Clarify whether the issue affects people, appliances, one room or the whole premises.
  • Record any recent appliance, renovation or building change that may be relevant.
