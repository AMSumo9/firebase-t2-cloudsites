Identifying whether a fence sits on a common boundary, agreeing on its purpose, accounting for site-specific factors, and knowing which official guidelines apply are critical initial steps. A Simple Fencing Planning Checklist

Use this sequence to transform a broad idea into a well-defined scope ready for discussion and action:

  1. Clarify the job: Determine if the project involves a boundary fence, an internal fence, a repair, or a complete replacement. The nature of the job will influence the conversations you need to have and the approvals you might need to check.
  2. Check the line and constraints: Before finalising fence type or layout, confirm the intended location, identify potential gate placements, consider site access, note any slopes, and be aware of drainage and existing services.
  3. Talk early where a boundary is involved: For any dividing fence, it is essential to discuss the proposal with the adjoining owner before commencing work. Ensure the scope, materials, and timeline are clear enough for both parties to consider and agree upon.
  4. Put the practical details together: A comprehensive scope typically includes the fence run, desired height, material choices, gate specifications, plans for removal or disposal of existing structures, site access arrangements, and the designated work area.
  5. Use the right local and official checks: Planning rules and necessary approvals can vary significantly based on the property's location and the nature of the work. Always consult the official resources provided for the most current information, rather than assuming existing regulations still apply.

Dividing Fences: A Shared Responsibility

A boundary fence represents a shared planning challenge between neighbours. Queensland's legislative framework for dividing fences outlines the responsibilities of adjoining property owners. Engaging in a constructive dialogue about the proposed fence, its associated costs, and the planned execution of the work can prevent a simple project from escalating into a complex dispute. It is advisable to maintain a clear record of key details, including:

  • The proposed fence location and its intended purpose.
  • The materials and dimensions under consideration.
  • Requirements for access, removal of existing structures, and site preparation.
  • Any quotes or written proposals that have been shared between parties.

For guidance on navigating these situations, refer to QCAT's dividing-fence guidance.

The first useful question is to identify whether the fence is on a common boundary, agree on its purpose, consider site constraints, and understand which official guidance is relevant. What are the key considerations for dividing fences in Queensland? For dividing fences in Queensland, it's crucial to discuss the proposal, costs, and work plan with your adjoining neighbour before commencing.
