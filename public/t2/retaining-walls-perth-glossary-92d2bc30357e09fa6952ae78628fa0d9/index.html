Retaining Walls Perth Glossary Explained

A useful brief starts with the job the wall must do, then names the measurements that change price and approvals. Retained height means the ground held back, not only the face you can see. Wall face is the visible surface used for the indicative square metre estimate. Slope matters because a block may fall away above or below the line, while boundary position matters where fences, neighbouring land or a dividing structure are close.

  • Record length, retained height and the reason the wall is needed.
  • Note winter water movement before choosing a material.
  • Keep appearance separate from the structural role.

Materials named for Perth conditions

The local material list is narrow enough to compare sensibly. Limestone is locally quarried and is described as the material most Perth walls are built from. Concrete post and panel systems suit taller or longer runs where a continuous face is wanted. Timber sleeper is presented as a low garden terracing option. Besser block and rock or boulder work round out masonry and natural-looking choices. Precast concrete and panel and post systems belong in the same longer-line conversation.

  • Small garden wall: compare timber sleeper with limestone.
  • Long retaining line: ask about panel and post or precast concrete.
  • Boundary, adjoining land or linked building work: raise engineered design before material preference.

Ground profile and water words

Perth planning language often splits blocks into two ground profiles. Near the coast, sandy ground can ravel and may need a wall to hold a pad or steady a face. Inland, toward the Darling Scarp, steeper grades, shallow soil over rock and winter runoff change the questions. The key terms are subsoil drainage, the collection and removal of water behind the wall, and discharge point, where that water is allowed to go. Backfill is the material placed behind the structure, spoil is excavated material removed from site, and footing or post assumptions describe the buried support a quote has priced.

Permit, design and compliance terms

In Western Australia, a building permit is generally needed once a wall retains more than 0.5 m of ground. Height is not the only trigger: a wall of any height can still need approval where it connects to other building work, affects or protects adjoining land, or sits on a boundary wall or substantial dividing fence. AS 4678 is the design standard to ask about when engineering input may be required. A Certificate of Design Compliance, called BA3, may be part of that pathway. Confirm the position with local government before work starts, clarify who arranges paperwork, and keep the answer with the quote file.

Quote terms that change the total

The indicative range is a wall-face figure, so the quote language matters more than the first number. Ask each contractor to state supply and installation, excavation, drainage, GST and difficult access in the same way. Length, retained height, material, footing or post assumptions, backfill and spoil handling should be written down. On a tight or sloping block, ask what machinery access is assumed. If permits or engineering are likely, ask whether that work is included, provisional or separate. Cost-effective choices reduce unknowns; the cheapest opening figure is weak when scope is unclear.

  1. Define the wall. Write down purpose, length, retained height, slope, boundary relationship and the ground profile.
  2. Clear approvals. Check the 0.5 m trigger and other permit triggers, then confirm with local government before work starts.
  3. Price the same scope. Ask every contractor to state material, excavation, drainage, GST, access and spoil handling in writing.

Common questions

Is 0.5 m a strict rule for every Perth wall? It is the general Western Australia permit trigger for retained ground, but a wall of any height can still need approval if it links to building work, affects adjoining land, or sits on a boundary wall or substantial dividing fence.

Does $250-$600 per square metre include all costs? No. The range is an indicative installed wall-face estimate and excludes GST, excavation, drainage and difficult access, so exclusions can change the real comparison.

When should engineered design be raised? Raise AS 4678 and BA3 early where height, location, boundaries, adjoining land or connected building work make the approval path less straightforward.
