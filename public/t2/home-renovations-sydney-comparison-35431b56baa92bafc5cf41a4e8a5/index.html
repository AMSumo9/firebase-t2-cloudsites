Home Renovations Sydney Comparison Explained

Before comparing home renovations sydney options, it helps to label the project class. A Paddington terrace under a conservation overlay is not the same project as a Castle Hill second-storey addition or a Cronulla beachside kitchen renovation. The main renovation paths include whole-home renovations, kitchen renovations, bathroom renovations, extensions, granny flats and heritage work. Each path brings different constraints regarding approvals, access and buildability. For example, a harbourourside home may raise access questions early, while a terrace may push heritage or conservation issues to the front. Identifying the real renovation path early helps you explain the outcome you want and the constraints you already know to a builder. If the problem sits across layout, services and finishes, a room by room request can understate the job. If the issue is mainly tired cabinetry, poor flow or one awkward wet area, a narrower scope may produce a more reliable written quote.

Cost ranges by scope

Costs vary widely based on scope, size and finish level. A cosmetic refresh of a few rooms can sit in the tens of thousands, while a full whole-home renovation with structural change commonly runs from around $150,000 into the several hundred thousands. Materials are usually the largest single cost, with labour a significant share on top. For kitchens, a budget refresh with new doors, benchtop and splashback, with no layout change, often starts in the low to mid tens of thousands. A mid-range full kitchen with new cabinetry, stone-look benchtops and appliances typically lands higher. Premium custom joinery with a butler pantry and integrated appliances costs even more. Those figures are better used to test scope than to predict a final contract sum. If your brief mixes finish upgrades with layout change, ask for those components to be separated in writing.

Bathroom specifics and compliance

Bathroom cost is driven by size, tiling extent, whether the layout moves and the quality of fixtures. A straightforward same-layout renovation is usually the most affordable path; moving plumbing, enlarging the room or using premium tiling and tapware increases the figure. Waterproofing to Australian Standard AS 3740 is a non-negotiable part of any compliant bathroom. If the layout changes, ask for waterproofing and service work to be described clearly, not folded into a vague allowance. Licensed renovators will price the work in writing after a site visit, but the scope must be clear to avoid budget blowouts. The source notes that bathroom cost usually rises when layout, plumbing, tiling extent and fixture quality all change together. This means a simple repaint and vanity swap is cheaper than a complete reconfiguration with new tiling and tapware.

Planning and approvals

Approvals, structural work and finish quality should be considered together before pricing. Council pathways, waterproofing and structural work should be handled early. The quoting contractor should set out scope, price, programme and exclusions in writing, and builder credentials, insurance details and written scope should be clear before work starts. Written contracts and builder credentials

Builder-led project delivery is essential for a smooth renovation. Builder credentials, insurance details and written scope should be clear before work starts. Written contracts before work begins are non-negotiable. The quoting contractor sets out the scope, price, programme and exclusions in writing so you can decide with full information. This documentation protects you if the project scope expands or if unexpected issues arise during construction. It also ensures that the builder is licensed and insured for the specific work being undertaken. Without a clear written scope, it is difficult to compare quotes accurately or hold a builder accountable for the agreed work. Always review the contract carefully before signing and ensure all verbal promises are included in writing.

  1. Define the scope. Decide whether the work is whole home, kitchen, bathroom, extension, granny flat or heritage related before requesting prices.
  2. List the constraints. Write down any likely approval, access, structural or waterproofing issue so it can be raised early.
  3. Set a rough budget. Establish a budget range that matches the likely scope and finish level.
  4. Request a written quote. Ask for a fixed-price quote that separates scope, price, programme and exclusions.
