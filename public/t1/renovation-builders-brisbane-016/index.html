Renovation Builders Brisbane Explained

Renovation Builders Brisbane describes several renovation paths: kitchens, bathrooms, Queenslander work, house extensions, full home renovations and heritage projects. That spread matters because each path asks different questions before price. A kitchen brief can focus on layout, finishes and cabinetry. A bathroom brief needs fixture choices and layout decisions. A full home renovation may bring structure, flooring, electrical, plumbing, painting and fixtures into one sequence.

Use the published categories as a scoping tool rather than a shopping list. Before requesting quotes, write down the rooms affected, what stays, what changes, and whether the project alters the footprint or structure. Brisbane homes can include classic Queenslanders and modern designs, so the same budget label may hide very different site conditions.

  • Kitchen: decide whether the aim is a targeted upgrade or a full redesign.
  • Bathroom: separate cosmetic refreshes from layout changes.
  • Queenslander or heritage: ask how original character, overlays and modern comfort will be handled.
  • Extension or full home: ask who manages approvals, design documentation and construction sequencing.

Cost Signals To Check Before A Quote

The source gives useful starting ranges: kitchens typically $25,000 to $60,000+, bathrooms typically $22,000 to $35,000+, house extensions from $150,000, and full home renovations from $150,000+. It also states a standard 3-4 bedroom home renovation ranges from $250,000 to $450,000, while raise and build-under projects start from $650,000.

Those figures are not a substitute for a measured quote. They help you test whether the brief is in the right cost band before time is spent on detailed pricing. A quote request should state the rooms involved, preferred finish level, whether layout changes are expected, and any known structural work. ASIC Moneysmart's renovating guidance is a useful reminder to treat renovation spending as a budget decision, not just a design decision.

Approvals, Timing And Documentation

The published guidance says council approval is required when renovations alter the building footprint or structure, and that a building permit is needed for structural alterations, extensions and significant layout changes. It also says internal-only renovations typically do not require planning approval, while properties in a heritage overlay need additional approval.

Timing should be discussed before design choices harden. The source states council approval can take 2-8 weeks depending on complexity. It also gives broad renovation timelines: full home renovations typically take 5-12 months including approvals, kitchen renovations 4-8 weeks, and bathroom renovations 2-4 weeks.

  1. Ask whether the project changes structure, footprint or layout.
  2. Ask whether a certifier, town planner or council pathway is needed.
  3. Ask which drawings, specifications and selections must be complete before pricing.
  4. Ask how approval timing affects ordering, demolition and the start date.

Builder Checks That Belong In The Brief

The page states that participating builders hold current QBCC licences, are verified before joining, and that QBCC licensing is required for all work over $3,300 in Queensland. It also says the builders are members of HIA and Master Builders Queensland.

For a homeowner, the practical check is simple: ask for the licence details, the project experience that matches your type of work, and a quote that separates scope, exclusions and assumptions. Housing Industry Association and Master Builders Australia are relevant industry bodies to understand professional context, while the QBCC licence claim should be checked directly against the builder proposed for the job.

Good quote comparison is not just the bottom line. Compare whether each builder priced the same rooms, the same level of finish, the same approval pathway and the same site assumptions. If one quote leaves out approvals or structural uncertainty, it is not directly comparable with one that includes them.

  1. Define the scope. List the rooms, structural changes, preferred finishes and any heritage or Queenslander features before requesting quotes.
  2. Check the approval path. Ask whether the work changes the structure, layout or footprint and whether a certifier or town planner should review it.
  3. Request comparable quotes. Give each builder the same brief so exclusions, allowances and assumptions can be compared clearly.
  4. Verify credentials. Check the builder's QBCC licence and ask for relevant experience with the same renovation type.
  5. Choose on fit. Compare price, scope clarity, communication and experience rather than choosing only the lowest number.

Common questions

How much should I budget for a Brisbane kitchen renovation? The source states kitchen renovations are typically $25,000 to $60,000+. Your quote should still depend on layout, finishes, cabinetry and whether services move.

Do internal renovations always need council approval? The published guidance says internal-only renovations typically do not require planning approval. It also says structural alterations, extensions, significant layout changes and heritage overlay work can require approval, so project-specific advice matters.

How long can a full home renovation take? The source gives a broad 5-12 month timeframe including approvals. It breaks that into feasibility, design and documentation, pre-construction and construction time.

What should I compare across builder quotes? Compare the same scope, finish level, approval assumptions, structural inclusions and exclusions. Also verify the QBCC licence for the builder proposed for the work.
