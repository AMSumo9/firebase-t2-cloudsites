Broker Versus Bank Direct

A bank can only offer its own loans, while a broker can compare loans from multiple lenders and help identify products that suit the borrower situation. That does not mean every borrower needs a broker. Some people prefer dealing with their existing bank directly, especially when the file is simple and they value one familiar contact.

The more complex the file, the more valuable the comparison step becomes. Self-employed income, investment servicing, equity-funded deposits, bridging needs or a tight settlement can all create questions that should be tested before lodging. The borrower should ask how the broker is paid, whether any fee applies, and which lenders were considered.

ASIC Moneysmart guidance on home loans is also useful here: comparison should look beyond the advertised rate and include fees, features, repayment type and whether the loan still suits the borrower's circumstances.

That makes document readiness a practical risk control. Before an inspection weekend, refinance review or build discussion, prepare the evidence a lender will ask for and check whether anything is missing. A broker can then test the likely questions before the application goes in.

  1. Confirm the goal: purchase, refinance, investment, construction, bridging or review.
  2. List income, dependants, existing commitments and deposit or equity position.
  3. Identify the property type and any timing pressure, such as an offer deadline or settlement.
  4. Ask whether the lender will need extra verification before approval.

Questions That Improve The Broker Conversation

Good borrower questions are specific enough to expose trade-offs. Ask how the recommended lender handles your income evidence, what happens if valuation, documents or settlement timing changes, and whether the repayment setup still works if rates or expenses move.

First home buyers should ask about the Victorian First Home Owner Grant, First Home Guarantee, LMI, deposit gaps and lender choice, because those items are named on the source page. Refinancers should ask whether switching is likely to leave them better off after current rate, fees, offset setup, equity and repayment pressure are reviewed. Investors should ask about interest-only structure, offset use, equity-funded deposit and portfolio servicing.

A construction borrower should ask when funds are released, what evidence the lender needs at each stage, and what happens if timing shifts. Do not rely on a general answer when the real file depends on lender policy and documents.

  1. Define the pressure point. State whether the issue is buying, refinancing, building, investing, bridging, deposit size, repayments, documents or a deadline.
  2. Prepare the evidence. Gather income details, commitments, dependants, deposit or equity information and property details before asking for borrowing guidance.
  3. Compare lender fit. Ask which lender policies may suit the file and which questions could slow approval.
  4. Decide the next move. Use the broker conversation to apply now, adjust the brief or wait until the file is stronger.

Common questions

Do Geelong borrowers usually pay a broker fee? The source page says most residential mortgage brokers in Australia are paid by lender commission and typically do not charge a direct fee for standard home or investment loans. It also says some may charge in complex situations and should disclose this upfront.

How long can pre-approval take? The source page says straightforward files with ready documents can sometimes receive pre-approval within a few days, while some lenders may take a week or more. Extra verification, including self-employed income checks, can affect timing.

Is a broker always better than going to a bank? No. A broker can compare multiple lenders, while a bank offers its own loans. The better path depends on how simple the file is, how much comparison the borrower wants and who explains the trade-offs clearly.

What should a first home buyer ask before inspecting properties? Ask about deposit gaps, LMI, lender choice and whether any first home buyer support mentioned by the broker applies to the file.
