The numbers that decide most shortlists

The published FAQs set clear baselines: most lenders require at least a 5% genuine savings deposit, lenders mortgage insurance usually applies when you borrow more than 80% of the property value, and repayments are commonly tested at the actual rate plus the APRA 3% prudential buffer. Lenders also commonly use the higher of your declared expenses or the Household Expenditure Measure, so a clean file starts with accurate spending and debt details rather than broad estimates.

  • Deposit strength: how much genuine savings you can show against the purchase price.
  • LMI exposure: the likely premium if you borrow above 80% of property value.
  • Buffer-tested repayments: whether repayments still work after the 3% buffer is applied.
  • Expense accuracy: declared living costs and existing debts documented properly.

That means a low deposit question is only half the task. A file can still tighten once expenses, debts and the buffer are applied, so comparing a buy-now path with a larger-deposit path on these numbers is more useful than comparing headline rates alone.

Common questions

Should I compare mortgage brokers on rate alone? No. The published guidance points to deposit size, serviceability and loan purpose as the first filters, with repayments tested at the actual rate plus the APRA 3% buffer. A slightly lower rate can come with a structure that suits your file less well, so compare the shortlist, the features and the disclosure alongside the rate.

Do mortgage brokers charge a fee in Australia? For most standard home loans, mortgage brokers do not charge you a direct fee. They are typically paid a commission by the lender once the loan settles. For complex lending scenarios a fee may apply, and that should be disclosed in writing up front, so ask for a clear statement of how the broker is remunerated before proceeding.

What deposit do I need before comparing loan options? Most lenders require at least 5% of the purchase price as a genuine savings deposit. If you borrow more than 80% of the property value, lenders usually require lenders mortgage insurance. Government schemes such as the First Home Guarantee may allow eligible buyers to purchase with a 5% deposit and avoid LMI, subject to lender approval and property price caps.
