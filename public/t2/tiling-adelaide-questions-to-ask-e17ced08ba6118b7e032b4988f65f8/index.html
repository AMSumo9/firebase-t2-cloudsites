Tiling Adelaide Questions to Ask Explained

Adelaide Tiling & Waterproofing views tiling not just as a surface finish, but as a project where the visible tile and grout performance relies heavily on the unseen layers. This includes the substrate condition, floor falls, movement joints, and the detailing of wet areas. For homeowners preparing a brief, this perspective is crucial because a visually appealing tile job can mask underlying issues with the base layers or waterproofing.

A specialist tiler needs specific information to provide an accurate scope. This includes details about the existing surface, the degree of water exposure the area will face, the type of fixtures involved, drainage systems, and the chosen tile format. Gathering these details helps distinguish between what can be estimated from initial information and what requires an on-site inspection.

When describing your project, specify the room type, such as a bathroom, ensuite, laundry, kitchen, living area, entry, feature wall, outdoor area, or alfresco zone. If repairs are needed, note symptoms like leaking, cracking, loose tiles, or deteriorated shower finishes. It is also important to request that waterproofing, screeding, and general preparation are itemised separately where applicable.

Questions to Ask Before Comparing Quotes

A quality quote should clearly outline all preparation work involved. Key inclusions to ask about are preparation methods, tile format, layout plans, trims, grout types, movement joints, site access, and waste removal. It is also vital that waterproofing or screeding is described separately if required. This ensures that quotes are compared on a like-for-like basis, rather than just the final price.

Ask each provider to explain the assumptions underpinning their price. For wet areas, this includes the substrate condition, floor falls, waste points, penetrations, thresholds, and the membrane system. For tiled floors or walls outside wet areas, it involves substrate preparation, transitions to adjoining surfaces, and the necessity of movement joints in the layout.

Crucial questions to pose include:

  • What specific aspects require on-site inspection before a fixed scope can be confirmed?
  • How are preparation, waterproofing, and tiling elements itemised within the quote?
  • What potential exclusions might alter the scope after demolition or surface removal?
  • Who is responsible for confirming licensed work details and product specifications in writing?

Repair vs. Renovation: Understanding the Difference

The distinction between maintenance and renovation is significant in tiling work. Moisture-related symptoms can stem from sealant issues, plumbing problems, substrate movement, or membrane failure. While regrouting might address minor maintenance concerns, it is not a substitute for diagnosing and fixing leaks.

Renovations follow a broader sequence. Before demolition, document the room's condition, identify suspected moisture sources, note access limitations, consider hazardous materials, and list fixtures that might need relocation. Before waterproofing, confirm the substrate's condition, floor falls, waste locations, penetrations, thresholds, and the chosen membrane system. Before tiling commences, finalise tile sizes, niche placements, trims, movement joints, cabinetry lines, and transitions to adjoining floors.

Adhering to this order is essential, as earlier work directly impacts the final tile layout. Changes to waste positions, cabinet lines, or threshold details can affect tile cuts, floor falls, and transitions, making it imperative to address these interfaces before ordering materials.

Local Conditions Affecting Tiling

Local conditions can significantly influence practical tiling considerations. For example, apartment access in the city might require specific planning for waste disposal and equipment movement. Coastal areas like Glenelg and Henley Beach may necessitate materials and detailing that account for salt exposure and increased moisture. Older villa substrates found in suburbs like Norwood and Unley might require more thorough investigation before pricing, while newer slabs around Mawson Lakes or Mount Barker still need checks on surface and movement assumptions.

These examples serve as prompts for discussions during the briefing process. An apartment job might need detailed access and waste management plans. Coastal or outdoor finishes require careful attention to exposure, drainage, and slip resistance. Older substrates may demand more in-depth assessment before a price can be set, and even new slabs need their surface and movement characteristics verified.

It is important to use the suburb or property type to highlight potential site conditions, but this should not replace a professional inspection. The tiler ultimately remains responsible for confirming the actual scope, licensing, products, and trade coordination in writing.

  1. Define the Room and Symptoms. Specify the exact room (bathroom, kitchen, etc.) and describe any visible issues like leaks, cracks, or loose tiles.
  2. Detail Substrate and Water Exposure. Explain your assumptions about the existing substrate and the level of water exposure the area will experience.
  3. List Inclusions and Exclusions. Clearly state what is included in the quote (preparation, tiling) and any potential exclusions that could affect the scope.
  4. Request Separate Waterproofing/Screeding. Ensure that any waterproofing or screeding work is itemised separately from the main tiling cost.
  5. Confirm Local Condition Impact. Discuss how local factors like coastal exposure or older substrates might affect preparation and sequencing.
