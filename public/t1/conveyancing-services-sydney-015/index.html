Conveyancing Services Sydney Explained

The right questions change depending on whether you are buying, selling, transferring a title, purchasing before construction or dealing with commercial property. Sydney Conveyancers describes assistance with contract review, searches, transfer duty calculations and settlement, while the professional you engage remains responsible for the legal work and advice.

For a buyer, ask who will examine the contract, identify conditions and organise searches before settlement. A seller should confirm who prepares the contract, attaches the prescribed disclosure documents and coordinates the sale. A family title transfer may require a different review from an ordinary purchase, and commercial property can involve leases, GST and due diligence.

  • Buying: check contract terms, searches and settlement tasks.
  • Selling: confirm disclosure documents and the settlement process.
  • Off the plan: ask about sunset clauses and disclosure rules before signing.
  • Commercial property: clarify how leases, GST and due diligence will be handled.

Auction and off-the-plan decisions

Auction purchases need preparation before bidding. The supplied guidance states that residential property bought at auction in NSW does not have a cooling-off period, so contract review before the bid is an important timing question. Ask when the review can be completed and what documents the professional needs before auction day.

Off-the-plan contracts create a different document trail because the property may not yet be built. Ask specifically how sunset clauses, disclosure rules and changes between the contract and the completed property will be addressed. These questions are more useful than relying on a standard purchase checklist.

Understand the quoted cost

The published FAQ describes routine professional fees as typically ranging from a few hundred to around $1,500. That is not the complete transaction cost. Government charges and disbursements, including title searches and registration, are additional and vary by property. Transfer duty is also identified as a separate cost to calculate.

A fixed professional fee can make the quote easier to compare, provided the written document separates professional work from searches, government charges, transfer duty and other disbursements. Before committing, ask whether the quoted fee covers the full transaction type, what happens if the matter becomes more complex and which charges will be passed through separately.

  • Request the professional fee in writing.
  • Ask for government charges and searches to be listed separately.
  • Confirm whether transfer duty is included or estimated separately.
  • Check what changes could create an additional professional charge.

Who this applies to

This guide is relevant to first home buyers, sellers of investment property, people transferring a title within the family, off-the-plan purchasers, auction bidders and commercial property buyers or sellers. Each group has a different document or timing issue to clarify.

First home buyers can ask what guidance is available about the First Home Buyers Assistance Scheme and the First Home Owner (New Homes) Grant, then confirm current eligibility and application requirements with the engaged professional or the relevant authority. A person selling an investment property should ask how the sale contract and disclosure documents will be prepared. Commercial parties should provide lease and due diligence material early.

Locality matters when the transaction involves a Sydney property, but the suburb alone does not determine the work. The property type, contract, title documents and transaction timing are more useful starting information than a general location label.

Settlement and the final checklist

The final stage involves more than a date on the calendar. The published process describes electronic settlement through PEXA, a digital platform used to lodge documents and transfer ownership. Ask who is responsible for outstanding searches, adjustments, document signing and confirming that settlement has completed.

Before instructing a professional, prepare the contract or sale documents, property details, the transaction type and any known timing pressure. Ask for the next action after the quote, the documents required from you and the point at which the settlement date becomes fixed.

  1. Identify the transaction. State whether you are buying, selling, transferring a title, bidding at auction, purchasing off the plan or dealing with commercial property.
  2. Send the core documents. Provide the contract or sale documents, property details and any known settlement timing requirements.
  3. Compare the written quote. Separate the fixed professional fee from searches, registration, transfer duty and other government charges.
  4. Confirm the process. Ask who handles review, searches, signing, PEXA settlement and communication about outstanding items.

Common questions

How much does a routine Sydney conveyance cost? The supplied FAQ describes professional fees as typically a few hundred to around $1,500. Government charges and disbursements, such as title searches and registration, are additional and vary by property.

Can a licensed conveyancer or solicitor handle the matter in NSW? The supplied page states that either may handle a conveyancing matter in NSW. A licensed conveyancer specialises in property transfers, while a solicitor can also advise on related legal issues.

Is there a cooling-off period after a NSW auction purchase? The supplied guidance states that the cooling-off period does not apply to property bought at auction. For most residential private treaty contracts, it describes a five business day period from exchange, ending at 5pm on the fifth business day.

What should an auction buyer do before bidding? Arrange contract review before bidding, because the supplied guidance says there is no cooling-off period at auction in NSW.

What does electronic settlement involve? The page describes settlement through PEXA, a digital platform used to lodge documents and transfer ownership. Ask the engaged professional which documents and approvals remain outstanding before settlement.
