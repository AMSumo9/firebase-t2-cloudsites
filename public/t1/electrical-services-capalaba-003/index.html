Safety Switches, Breakers And Smoke Alarms

The published FAQ gives three useful distinctions. A safety switch monitors for leakage of electrical current and cuts power when that fault is detected. It only protects the circuits connected to it and must work correctly to help protect people. A circuit breaker is described separately, as a device that primarily protects wiring and appliances.

The guide also notes that some devices combine both functions, so unclear board labels should be checked by a licensed electrician. That is a practical way to frame a board question without assuming what is installed. If a reader is unsure whether a device is a safety switch, circuit breaker or combined unit, the enquiry should say exactly that.

For smoke alarms, the source page says compliant alarms are photoelectric, interconnected and meet AS 3786-2014. It also says the required power supply and replacement choice depend on the dwelling and existing alarm arrangement. A good enquiry therefore describes the current alarm arrangement, whether the work is replacement or new work, and whether the question is about compliance, faults or installation.
