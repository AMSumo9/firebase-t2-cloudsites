Web Design Perth Costs Explained

Website costs vary widely depending on scope, design complexity and content needs. A safer quote is often the one that explains the work more clearly, not the one with the lowest figure on the front page. Read each proposal as a scope document. Who writes the copy, who uploads it, who sources or places images and who checks every page before launch? If the answer is left to assumptions, the final job can expand well beyond the original figure. Content drafting, editing and page upload are key inclusions to check. Image sourcing, resizing and placement should also sit inside scope. For a redesign, ask what happens to old URLs, downloads and contact forms during the rebuild. Revisions and post-launch support are also critical. Web design perth agencies often include hosting, maintenance and backups in their packages, so confirm these details before signing. A clear scope prevents vague quotes and helps you compare providers fairly.

Matching the Platform to Your Needs

The source guide separates common build paths clearly enough to turn them into a buyer decision. A small business build suits an owner who wants a practical site on a sensible budget. This type of site focuses on enquiries and clear calls to action rather than complex functionality. A WordPress build suits a team that expects to manage routine text, image and page changes without a developer on call. WordPress offers flexibility and a large ecosystem of plugins that can extend functionality over time. A Shopify or WooCommerce store needs a tighter brief around products, trust and the buying path, not just presentation. These platforms handle payments, inventory and shipping automatically, which saves time but requires more detailed product data. This is also where redesign projects need more discipline than a fresh build. If the current site still brings enquiries or ranks for useful searches, the discussion cannot stop at a new look. Ask what will happen to page structure, existing content, forms and any URLs that already matter to customers. A practical way to compare proposals is to ask each provider to explain the platform choice in plain language. If the answer is vague, or if every business is being pushed into the same stack regardless of goals, that is a warning that the build path has not been sized to the job.

Timeline and Handover

The published FAQ says a standard website usually takes about 2 to 3 months, including discovery, design, development, testing and content creation. It also says timing depends on project size and on how quickly content and approvals are provided. That makes the schedule a shared responsibility, not just a developer promise. Ask for milestone dates tied to real inputs. When is copy due, when are design comments due, who signs off page layouts and what happens if feedback lands late? These checks matter because the timeline can look reasonable on paper while the approval process is still too loose to hold it together. Control after launch needs the same level of detail. The source page says clients keep their domain, content and finished site, with logins handed over and help available on updates. Before signing, ask what that handover actually includes, who holds the registrar and hosting access, whether simple update training is part of the project and what support looks like once the site is live. A solid handover plan ensures you can maintain the site long after the project team has moved on.

Maintenance and Ongoing Support

Once a site is live, it needs regular attention to stay secure and perform well. Website care and maintenance plans cover ongoing updates, backups and security checks. These services help prevent downtime and protect your business from cyber threats. A good maintenance package should include monthly backups, software updates and security patches. It may also cover performance monitoring and minor content tweaks. If you plan to sell products online, maintenance is even more critical to ensure your store remains functional and secure. Ask your provider what is included in their standard care plan and whether it covers emergency support. Understanding these ongoing costs helps you budget for the full lifecycle of your website rather than just the initial build. Regular maintenance also ensures your site remains compatible with new devices and browsers, providing a consistent experience for all visitors.

SEO and Performance

Fast, well-structured websites built on solid technical foundations are essential for ranking in Google from day one. A site that loads slowly or is not mobile-friendly will lose visitors before they even engage with your content. Technical search work is part of the build or quoted on its own, so clarify this before you commit. Ask whether the provider handles technical SEO, such as page speed optimisation, structured data and XML sitemaps. These elements help search engines understand your content and index it correctly. A mobile-first design is also non-negotiable in 2026, as most users browse on smartphones. Ensure the design looks and functions well on all screen sizes. If your business relies on local customers, check that the site is set up for local SEO, including Google Business Profile integration and location-based keywords. Investing in solid SEO foundations from the start can save time and money later by avoiding the need for expensive rework.

  1. Define your goals and budget. Identify what your website must do, such as generate enquiries or sell products, and set a realistic budget range before contacting providers.
  2. Request detailed written quotes. Ask for a written proposal that lists every service, from design and development to content creation and ongoing maintenance.
  3. Review inclusions line by line. Check that the quote covers all necessary tasks, including image sourcing, copywriting, revisions and post-launch support.
  4. Confirm ownership and handover details. Ensure you will receive the domain, hosting access and login credentials, and ask about training for routine updates.
  5. Schedule regular maintenance and backups. Arrange a care plan that includes security updates, backups and performance monitoring to keep your site running smoothly.

Common questions

Can I update my website myself? Many modern websites, especially WordPress sites, are built so you can edit basic text, images and pages yourself. Some agencies also offer training or handover support so owners can manage routine updates without needing a developer on call.

Do web designers provide SEO as part of the website build? Some Perth web design agencies include SEO or basic search optimisation, but it is not always part of the standard build. It is best to ask whether SEO is included or priced separately before signing.

How long does it take to build a website? Timelines depend on the size of the site and how quickly content and approvals are provided. A standard website usually takes 2 to 3 months, including discovery, design, development, testing and content creation.
