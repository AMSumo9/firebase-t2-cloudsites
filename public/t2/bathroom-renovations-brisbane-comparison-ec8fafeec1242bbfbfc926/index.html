The Renovation Process

Knowing the sequence helps you understand why timelines look the way they do. Each stage depends on the one before it, and a couple of them simply cannot be rushed without risking failure down the track. Design and quote: finalise layout, fixtures and a fixed-price scope. Demolition: strip back to the studs and floor; inspect for hidden damage. Rough-in: licensed plumbing and electrical moved to new positions. Waterproofing: membrane applied and left to cure; this stage is certified. Tiling and screeding: floors and walls tiled, falls set to the drain. Fit-off: vanity, toilet, tapware, screen and accessories installed. For a project that reconfigures plumbing, the full bathroom renovation service usually runs three to five weeks on site. Allow extra lead time for ordering tiles and a custom vanity, since back-ordered stock is the most common cause of delay. Standard bathroom renovation timelines vary by scope: cosmetic updates take 1-2 weeks, standard renovations take 3-6 weeks, and full renovations with plumbing/electrical take 4-6 weeks.

Waterproofing & Materials

Waterproofing is the single most important stage in any wet area. In Queensland it must be carried out to the National Construction Code and certified by a licensed waterproofer. A failed membrane is the leading cause of expensive callbacks, so reputable builders document this step and never let tiling start before the membrane has fully cured. Brisbane humidity rewards finishes that shrug off moisture and mould. The choices below balance durability with the look most homeowners want. Tiles: large-format porcelain for walls and floors means fewer grout lines and easier cleaning. Benchtops: engineered stone or porcelain resists humidity better than timber. Tapware: solid brass bodies with a quality finish outlast cheaper plated alternatives. Ventilation: a properly ducted exhaust fan is the cheapest mould insurance you can buy. Screens: frameless or semi-frameless glass keeps a small room feeling open. Licensing & Permits

This is the part homeowners most often get wrong. Most like-for-like renovations do not need building approval, but the wet-area trades are tightly regulated for good reason. Plumbing, drainage and waterproofing must be done by a licensed contractor and certified. Any contract over $3,300 in Queensland requires a QBCC-licensed builder and a written contract. Structural changes, such as removing a wall, can trigger building approval through a certifier. Always confirm that your tradesperson holds a current licence before work starts. You can verify a contractor and read consumer guidance on the Queensland Building and Construction Commission website, which is the official regulator for the trade in this state.
