Questions That Reveal Loan Fit

Broker selection should be tested with specific questions, not broad confidence. The published page says standard home loans often have no direct broker fee, while a fee for complex scenarios should be disclosed in writing up front. Those points give borrowers a clean starting checklist.

  • Ask which lenders and loan features are being compared, including offset accounts and fixed versus variable rates.
  • Ask how broker remuneration works, including whether a direct fee applies to a complex file.
  • Ask how serviceability will be assessed, including the APRA buffer and declared expenses.
  • Ask whether LMI, a guarantor option or a government scheme changes the deposit pathway.
  • Ask what documents are needed for self-employed, investment, construction or bridging finance.

Deposit, LMI And Serviceability Checks

The published page gives several borrower facing numbers that should be treated as planning prompts, not an approval promise. It says most lenders require at least a 5% genuine savings deposit. It also says lenders usually require Lenders Mortgage Insurance when a borrower takes more than 80% of the property value, and that LMI protects the lender rather than the borrower.

Borrowing power is not only about income. The page says lenders consider income, existing debts, living expenses and serviceability, and may use the higher of declared expenses or the Household Expenditure Measure. It also says repayments are tested at the actual rate plus the APRA 3% prudential buffer. A borrower comparing a Western Sydney mortgage broker option should therefore bring real expenses and debts to the conversation, not just a target purchase price.

What A Practical Review Should Produce

A useful review should leave the borrower with next steps that match the file. For a refinance, that may mean whether to cut rate, restructure or release equity. For an investment property loan, it may mean comparing loan structure, offsets and serviceability. For construction or building finance, it may mean understanding progress payments before committing to a build timeline. For a standard purchase, it may simply mean knowing which documents and scenarios are worth testing with lenders.

The provider says it helps from application through to settlement and offers broker callbacks with no obligation and no pressure. An independent reader should translate that into measurable outputs: a short list of options, any disclosed fee, likely document gaps, scheme questions to verify, and a clear reason why one loan type suits the borrower's goal better than another.

  1. Define the loan goal. Separate purchase, refinance, investment, construction, self-employed or bridging needs before comparing lenders.
  2. Collect evidence. Prepare income, debts, expenses, deposit position and existing loan details so borrowing power can be assessed realistically.
  3. Ask about costs. Request written disclosure of any direct broker fee, especially if the scenario is complex.
  4. Compare features. Look beyond the rate by asking about offsets, fixed versus variable structure, LMI and settlement support.
