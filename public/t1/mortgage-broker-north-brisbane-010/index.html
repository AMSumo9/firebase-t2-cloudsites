Who this applies to

The clearest fit is a borrower whose file needs more than a single-bank answer. The source page specifically names first home buyers, refinancers, investors and construction borrowers. Each situation changes the questions worth asking, so the conversation should follow the file rather than a generic loan script.

  • First home buyers: ask how the Queensland First Home Owner Grant, the federal First Home Guarantee, deposit gaps and lender selection are being considered.
  • Refinancers: compare the current rate, fees and structure against available lender options before deciding whether switching is worth the effort.
  • Investors: ask how interest-only terms, offset use, SMSF lending or multiple-property structures affect the lender shortlist.
  • Construction borrowers: ask what documents, builder details and staged funding information will be needed before approval.

ASIC Moneysmart's home loan guidance is useful background because it helps borrowers review loan features, costs and repayment pressure before choosing a loan. That background does not replace credit advice, but it gives borrowers a practical checklist for the broker call.

Questions to ask before pre-approval

A pre-approval is most useful when it reflects the lender's actual treatment of the borrower, not just a headline borrowing estimate. The published enquiry process asks for the borrower's goal, deposit and income so the review can start with the facts that usually shape lender fit.

  • Which lenders are being shortlisted, and why do they fit this income and deposit profile?
  • What assumptions are being made about dependants, existing commitments and living expenses?
  • Is lenders mortgage insurance likely to apply, and what alternatives should be checked?
  • For a purchase, what settlement date is realistic once the contract is accepted?
  • For a refinance, what can delay discharge from the outgoing lender?
  • What commission will be disclosed in the Credit Quote and Credit Proposal Disclosure?

The published page says standard purchases may allow 30 to 45 days from accepted contract to settlement, while refinances usually take 4 to 8 weeks depending on the outgoing lender's discharge process. Treat those as planning ranges, then confirm the exact timing with the broker and conveyancer for the file.

How to use the first broker call well

Arrive with the information that changes the answer. Income, deposit, dependants, existing debts, credit limits, property purpose and timing can all affect borrowing power. The source page says the broker confirms borrowing power, lender shortlist and realistic timeline during the free review, then handles the application and follows up approvals.

First home buyers should ask grant and guarantee questions without assuming eligibility. Investors should ask how the loan structure supports the strategy rather than only chasing the lowest rate. Refinancers should ask for the break-even logic, including fees and whether the new structure improves the position enough to justify switching.

The final step is settlement and aftercare. The published process says the broker aligns with the conveyancer and lender for settlement day and remains available for annual rate reviews afterwards. A review after settlement can be useful when rates, income or property plans change.

  1. Prepare the file. Gather income, deposit, debts, dependants, property goal and timing before requesting the review.
  2. Ask for the shortlist. Have the broker explain which lenders suit the file and why alternatives were excluded.
  3. Check disclosure. Read the Credit Quote and Credit Proposal Disclosure before committing to an application.
  4. Confirm settlement timing. Align broker, lender and conveyancer dates before relying on a purchase or refinance timeline.
