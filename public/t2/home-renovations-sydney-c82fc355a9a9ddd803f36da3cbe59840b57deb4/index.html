Cost benchmarks for Sydney projects

Cost signals that help before a formal quote are best understood through the specific ranges provided for different scopes. A cosmetic refresh of a few rooms can sit in the tens of thousands, while a full whole-home renovation with structural change commonly runs from around $150,000 into the several hundred thousands. Materials are usually the largest single cost, with labour a significant share on top. For kitchen work, the source page draws a clearer line between a refresh and a rebuild. New doors, benchtop and splashback with no layout change often start in the low-to-mid tens of thousands. Costs rise when cabinetry material changes, benchtop choice shifts, appliances are upgraded, or plumbing and electrical work need to move. The practical takeaway is not to chase a citywide average. Use early price ranges only to decide whether the scope is realistic, then ask for a written fixed-price quote for your specific home and scope.

Key compliance and planning factors

Approval details and structural issues should be handled early in the process. Builder credentials, insurance details and written scope should be clear before work starts. The quoting contractor should set out scope, price, programme and exclusions in writing so the owner can decide with full information. If council pathways, waterproofing or structural work are only addressed later, the number on the page may not reflect the real build path. For bathrooms, the source page names one compliance point explicitly, waterproofing to Australian Standard AS 3740 is a non-negotiable part of any compliant bathroom. That gives owners a simple test, if the written scope is vague on compliance items, it is probably vague elsewhere too. A practical question to ask is whether the proposal assumes a same-layout job, a reconfiguration, or a scope that depends on approval outcomes.

How to prepare for a builder quote

The most useful inputs named on the source page are simple, suburb, project type, what feels broken or outdated, whether structural change or approvals are likely, and your rough budget. That is enough to make the first conversation more concrete without pretending every design choice has already been made. Before requesting a written quote, prepare a short brief covering the rooms involved, the layout problems you want solved, any access issue, the finish level you expect, and any approval concern you already suspect. If you are deciding between a kitchen-only update and broader renovation work, say that directly. If the bathroom will stay in the same layout, mention that too. This level of detail helps the builder provide a comparable quote rather than three different interpretations of the job.

  1. Identify the problem. Determine whether the issue is layout, outdated fixtures, leaks or a lack of space. Clarify if the project is room-specific or requires a broader rethink.
  2. Check for constraints. Assess whether heritage restrictions, council approvals or access issues will affect the build. Note any structural changes required.
  3. Prepare a brief. List the rooms involved, your rough budget, suburb and any unusual access or approval concerns. This helps builders provide comparable quotes.
  4. Request a written quote. Ask for a fixed-price quote that details scope, exclusions, programme and compliance items like waterproofing standards.
