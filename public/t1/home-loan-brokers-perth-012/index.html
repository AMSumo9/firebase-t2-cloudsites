Broker Help Or Going Direct

A broker-assisted path is strongest when the borrower needs comparison across lender policies, loan features or several finance types. Going direct may suit a borrower who already knows the lender they want and has a straightforward application. The published guide places lender comparison at the centre of the borrower decision.

ASIC Moneysmart says borrowers should compare home loans by looking beyond the headline rate, including fees, features and repayment flexibility. In a broker conversation, ask for the full comparison: upfront costs, ongoing fees, offset account availability, fixed and variable options, repayment structure and consequences if plans change.

Local relevance should come from the property plan. Ask whether the lender view changes for an established home, apartment, investment property or new build. If the answer is unclear, ask which documents are needed and whether the likely pressure point is income, deposit, valuation or loan purpose.

Questions Before You Submit Details

Use the first call to clarify the broker relationship. The page says borrowers are not charged, and brokers are paid by lenders when the loan settles. A careful borrower can still ask how the broker is remunerated, which lenders are available, whether any lenders are excluded and whether the broker regularly handles the same scenario.

  • Which lenders from the 100+ panel may consider this situation, and why?
  • What documents should be prepared before applying?
  • How will fixed rate, variable rate and offset account options be compared?
  • For a refinance, what costs or lost features could reduce the benefit of switching?
  • For an investment loan, how will equity access, interest-only options and rental income be treated?
  • For construction finance, how are progress payments explained before contracts are signed?

Perth Scenarios To Separate Early

The page gives three Perth examples: a first home buyer couple in the northern suburbs, a homeowner in the western suburbs with an older variable rate, and a transport operator needing equipment finance after a main bank decline. Those examples point to different broker tasks, even within one city.

For the first home buyer, the early checks are deposit, FHBG questions and possible LMI impact. For the refinancing homeowner, the focus is whether the current loan still compares well against available alternatives. For the business borrower, the first issue may be access to specialist lenders who understand business lending, equipment finance or commercial property needs.

Name the scenario before asking for products: purchase, refinance, investment, construction, commercial or vehicle finance. Then ask the broker which parts of the application need evidence and where lender criteria may narrow the options.

  1. Define the loan purpose. Start with purchase, refinance, investment, construction, commercial or vehicle finance so the broker can narrow the lender comparison.
  2. Prepare the borrower picture. Have income, debts, deposit, property plan and current loan details ready before asking for options.
  3. Ask about lender coverage. Request a plain explanation of which lenders may fit and why other lenders may not.
  4. Compare features as well as rate. Check fees, repayment flexibility, offset account options and fixed or variable structure before choosing.
  5. Confirm next steps. Ask what documents are needed and what happens after the first 4 business hour follow-up.
