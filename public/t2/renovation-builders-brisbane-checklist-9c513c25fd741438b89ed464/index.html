Renovation Builders Brisbane Checklist Explained

When considering renovation builders in Brisbane, understanding the service mix is the clearest starting point. This includes kitchen renovations, bathroom renovations, Queenslander work, extensions, and full home projects. These categories are important because they influence the specific questions you need to ask about structure, documentation, and site access. A simple kitchen refresh without layout changes will have different pricing considerations than altering a character home or extending a house.

Before requesting quotes, prepare a brief statement outlining the project scope. Specify which rooms are included, whether walls will be moved, if the property is a Queenslander, and which items are essential versus optional. This ensures each builder receives the same baseline information, making published budget ranges more useful. A room-by-room update can quickly escalate in cost if one quote assumes extensive joinery and services changes while another anticipates only a lighter cosmetic job.

Key details to list include:

  • The spaces in scope.
  • Any structural or layout changes planned.
  • Whether the home has heritage overlay issues requiring checks.
  • A distinction between must-have works and finish upgrades.

Use Licence and Project Fit as the First Filter

For renovations in Queensland, a QBCC licence is a legal requirement for work exceeding $3,300. Builders often also hold memberships with industry bodies like the Housing Industry Association (HIA) and Master Builders Queensland. While these credentials serve as an initial screening tool, they are not the sole determinant of a builder's suitability. The stronger test is project fit.

Inquire whether the builder regularly undertakes the type of work you are planning. Ask if they manage the project from concept through to completion and which trades they coordinate directly. This is particularly relevant for older Brisbane homes where timber restoration, VJ walls, or build-under work can significantly impact project sequencing and pricing. A seemingly tidy first quote is less valuable than a builder who can clearly articulate the assumptions behind demolition, services, structure, and finishes.

Check Approvals and Timing Before Choosing a Start Date

Council approval is typically required for renovations that alter a building's footprint or structure. A building permit is specifically needed for structural alterations, extensions, and significant layout changes. Internal renovations that do not involve structural changes or layout modifications usually do not require planning approval. Properties subject to heritage overlays will necessitate additional approvals.

These approval processes should be clarified before setting a firm start date. The timeframe for council approval can range from 2 to 8 weeks, depending on the project's complexity. Construction timelines vary: kitchen renovations generally take 4 to 8 weeks, bathrooms 2 to 4 weeks, and full home renovations, including approvals, can take 5 to 12 months. When comparing builders, it is crucial to ask whether their proposed timelines encompass feasibility, design documentation, and pre-construction phases, or if they only cover on-site works. This distinction often explains discrepancies between seemingly similar project schedules.

Read the Quote for Scope Gaps, Not Just the Total

Published cost bands are most useful when the written scope of work is clearly defined. For instance, kitchen renovations are estimated between $25,000 and $60,000+, and bathrooms between $22,000 and $35,000+. Extensions can start from $150,000, with full home renovations often beginning at $150,000+. A standard three to four-bedroom home renovation might range from $250,000 to $450,000, while raise-and-build-under projects could start from $650,000.

These figures should prompt detailed questions rather than a race to the lowest price. Verify if each quote accounts for the same scope of demolition, flooring, electrical rewiring, plumbing updates, painting, and fixtures, as these are common inclusions in a full home renovation. If one builder has factored in documentation support, approvals coordination, or necessary service upgrades while another has not, a lower total may simply defer unresolved costs to later stages. Confirm what elements are fixed costs and what remain allowances. Clarify who handles approvals coordination and whether selections must be finalised before the quote is firm. Compare site preparation and service assumptions line by line.
