Key takeaway

The advertised interest rate is only part of what truck finance costs in Australia. Application fees, ongoing account fees, early repayment charges, balloon residuals and end-of-term costs can all change the total. Chattel mortgages, finance leases, commercial hire purchase and business loans each carry a different fee and ownership profile. Comparing total repayments, fees, residual exposure, tax treatment and exit options gives a far more reliable picture than the rate alone.

Most buyers compare truck deals by the advertised percentage. That number matters, but it leaves out the fees and end-of-term obligations that sit alongside it. A larger balloon, for example, lowers the estimated monthly repayment while leaving more to pay or refinance when the term ends. The estimator illustration in the parent guide shows a repayment of $2,867 per month plus a final balloon of $36,000, a reminder that one number rarely tells the whole story.

A cheaper-looking loan can therefore become the more expensive truck decision. The practical test is the total cost across the full term: repayments, fees, residual exposure, tax treatment and exit options together.

The fee categories to ask about

Whatever structure you choose, the questions below surface the charges that change the comparison. Not every lender applies every fee, so confirm each item against the credit proposal and contract before signing.

  • Upfront charges: application or establishment fees, valuation of the truck, and any broker or documentation fees.
  • Ongoing charges: monthly account-keeping or administration fees, which recur across a term of three to seven years and quietly add up.
  • Early and late charges: early termination or payout figures, plus default fees described in the agreement. Commercial credit can carry fewer statutory protections than consumer credit, so reading the default terms matters.
  • End-of-term amounts: the residual or balloon on leases and hire purchase, plus any refinancing cost if you roll it over.

How fees differ across the four structures

Each pathway distributes cost differently between the term and the end of the deal, and that affects which fees you are most likely to meet.

  • Chattel mortgage: the business buys the truck and the lender takes security over it. Ownership from day one means fewer end-of-term obligations, but the business carries the full asset risk and any resale shortfall.
  • Finance lease: the financier owns the truck during the term and payments fund its use. Watch the end-of-term choices set by the agreement, because the options at expiry carry their own costs.
  • Commercial hire purchase: the financier buys the truck and hires it to the business, with ownership generally transferring after the final payment and residual. The staged path spreads cost but the residual still lands at the end.
  • Business loan: a secured or unsecured facility can fund a truck, but pricing and security may differ from asset-backed truck finance, which can shift fees rather than remove them.

Balloons, residuals and exit costs in practice

The balloon is where many fee conversations actually happen. Choosing a 20% balloon can make the monthly figure fit a tight cash flow, yet the amount left at the end must be paid, refinanced or traded through. Refinancing the residual starts a new term with its own establishment costs, so a lower payment today can produce a higher lifetime cost.

Exit questions worth asking up front include:

  1. What is the exact payout figure at each point in the term?
  2. Is there an early termination fee, and how is it calculated?
  3. What happens to the residual if the truck's resale value has fallen?
  4. Which fees are quoted in the comparison rate, and which sit outside it?

Who this fee check applies to

This comparison suits owner-operators buying a single rig, fleets that plan regular upgrades through leasing, businesses wanting staged ownership via hire purchase, and buyers using a general business loan for mixed or unusual assets. It also applies to anyone buying used, where an official PPSR search, mechanical inspection and realistic resale estimate affect the asset quality a lender will accept, and therefore the fee and rate offered.

Common questions

Is the lowest advertised rate the cheapest truck finance deal? Not necessarily. Fees, the balloon, residual exposure and exit options all add to the lifetime cost. The parent guide's estimator illustrates a monthly repayment alongside a final balloon, showing why the rate alone is an incomplete comparison.

Which fees should I confirm before signing a truck finance contract? Ask for the establishment or application fee, any ongoing account fees, early termination or payout figures, default fees, and the end-of-term residual plus the options for dealing with it. Check which charges sit inside and outside any comparison rate.

Do GST and deductions change the fee comparison? Yes. GST credit eligibility and timing depend on registration, business use and the structure, and deductions depend on how the asset relates to assessable income. The ATO publishes guidance on motor-vehicle GST credits and deductions, and outcomes vary by circumstances.
