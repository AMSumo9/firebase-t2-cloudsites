Ask the broker to separate three drivers: the property being purchased, the borrower's income and expenses, and the lender policy question. That makes the conversation more useful than a broad suburb-by-suburb comparison.

Loan structure questions worth asking

The published information mentions offset accounts, fixed versus variable rates, lenders mortgage insurance, home equity, bridging loans, investment structures and the NSW First Home Buyers Assistance Scheme. These are not interchangeable features. They affect flexibility, upfront cost, monthly repayment shape and the way a loan may behave if plans change.

A careful conversation should include whether an offset account is useful for the borrower's savings pattern, whether a fixed rate limits flexibility, and whether a variable rate suits the borrower's tolerance for repayment changes. ASIC Moneysmart provides consumer guidance on comparing home loans, including interest rates, costs, features and repayment options.

For investors, the question is not simply the lowest rate. Ask how the proposed loan structure deals with separate savings, future equity use, interest-only considerations and record keeping. For refinancing, ask whether the new loan improves the overall position after fees, discharge steps and feature changes are considered.

The published information says borrowing power depends on income, existing debts, living expenses and the lender's serviceability assessment. Before a broker review, gather income evidence, current loan statements, credit card limits, personal loan details, living expense estimates, savings history and property plans. Self-employed borrowers should expect extra attention on business income, contractor income or variable earnings, because the published information identifies full-doc and alt-doc options for business owners and contractors.

Use calculators as rough preparation only. The published information describes its borrowing calculator as an estimate. A broker review should test the full situation before a borrower signs a contract, applies for pre-approval or assumes a deposit position is enough.

How to compare recommendations

A broker recommendation should be understandable without jargon. Ask which lenders were considered, why a short list was selected, how the broker is remunerated, whether any fee applies, and which loan features are essential rather than optional.

ASIC is the Australian regulator named in the supplied authorities for credit and financial services information. Borrowers can ask for the broker's credit details, the written explanation of fees where relevant, and a plain-English explanation of the recommended loan.

Good comparison is more than rate shopping. It should weigh borrowing capacity, deposit, LMI, repayment comfort, property plans and settlement needs. A clear broker file should leave the borrower able to explain why the recommended loan fits the scenario.

  1. Define the goal. State whether the loan is for a first purchase, refinance, investment, construction, bridging or self-employed scenario.
  2. Prepare the numbers. Bring income, debt, living expense, savings and current loan information so serviceability can be tested properly.
  3. Ask about broker payment. Confirm whether a direct fee applies and request any complex-scenario fee disclosure in writing.
  4. Compare structure, not only rate. Review offset, fixed or variable choices, LMI, equity use and repayment flexibility before deciding.
  5. Check the explanation. Ask for a written explanation of why the recommended loan fits the borrower scenario.

Common questions

Does a broker replace independent decision making? No. A broker can compare lender options and explain loan structure, but the borrower still needs to understand costs, features, repayment comfort and suitability before proceeding.

When might LMI be discussed? The published information says LMI is usually required when borrowing more than 80% of the property value. It also says LMI protects the lender, not the borrower.

Can a broker help self-employed borrowers? The published information identifies full-doc and alt-doc options for business owners and contractors with variable income. The practical step is to prepare income records before the broker review.

Is pre-approval the same as final approval? No. Treat pre-approval as a conditional step that still depends on lender checks, documents and property assessment. Ask what must happen before unconditional approval.
