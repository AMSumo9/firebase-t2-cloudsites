Preparing Your Broker Brief

While a directory might suggest a job can be described in about 30 seconds, a productive conversation with a broker requires verified facts. Begin by clearly stating the purpose of your enquiry: buying, refinancing, investing, or construction and specialist lending. Document your property goal, noting whether the property is already owned, under contract, or still under research. Prepare your income, debt, and deposit details in a verifiable format. It is also important to ask about the broker's fee structure, commission arrangements, and any limitations on their lender access. Clarify the next steps following the initial conversation, including document collection, the comparison process, and the timeline for lodging an application.

Common questions

What is the difference between a broker directory and personal credit advice? A broker directory provides a list of potential brokers and their services, acting as a starting point for your search. Personal credit advice involves a detailed assessment of your financial situation by a qualified professional to recommend specific loan products tailored to your needs.

How do I ensure a broker is suitable for investment loans? Ask the broker about their experience with investment loans, how they assess rental income and potential tax benefits like negative gearing, and which lenders they recommend for investment properties.

What are common fees associated with using a home loan broker? Brokers are typically remunerated by lenders through commissions. However, some may charge a direct fee for their services, especially for complex or specialist loans. Always clarify the broker's fee structure upfront and understand if they receive commission from the lender.
