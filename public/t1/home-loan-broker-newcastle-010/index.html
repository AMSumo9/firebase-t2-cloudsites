Who This Applies To

This guide applies to Newcastle and Lake Macquarie borrowers who want to organise a loan conversation before regulated credit advice is given. It is most relevant when the borrower has a real purchase, refinance or build decision in front of them and needs to know what to prepare.

  • First home buyers: ask which deposit, grant, concession and lender-choice questions need checking against current rules before relying on a pathway.
  • Refinancers: prepare the current loan, equity position and switching-cost questions before comparing lender options.
  • Investors: separate rental income, tax-setting and loan-structure questions so the broker can test them against lender policy.
  • Construction borrowers: clarify whether the project is a knockdown-rebuild, house-and-land purchase or major build, because progress-payment finance needs different documents.
  • Self-employed borrowers: prepare for questions about variable, contractor or company income rather than assuming PAYG-style assessment.

Questions To Sort Before The Call

The source page repeatedly points borrowers back to context: suburb or postcode, purchase stage, loan type and timing pressure. Those details are not admin trivia. They help a broker decide whether the first discussion should focus on borrowing power, documentation, lender policy, scheme eligibility or a looming finance condition.

Before speaking with a broker, write down the decision that must be made next. For a first buyer, that may be whether the deposit and scheme pathway are realistic. For a Merewether upgrader, the guide raises bridging timing and valuation sensitivity. For a refinancer, it may be whether switching costs and equity make the move worth testing. For an investor, the key question may be how rental income and tax settings should be treated before a new loan is considered.

ASIC Moneysmart encourages borrowers to compare home loans and understand costs before committing. In practice, that means using a broker conversation to ask what assumptions sit behind any borrowing figure, what documents are still missing, and what could change once a lender assesses the full file.

Broker Conversation Versus Calculator Check

The source guide includes a borrowing power calculator, but it describes the tool as indicative and general. That distinction matters. A calculator can help frame the next question, while a licensed broker review considers the applicant's full circumstances before regulated advice is given.

The practical sequence is to use the calculator for orientation, then bring the result into the broker conversation with the assumptions visible. Ask whether the result depends on income type, living expenses, existing debt, property type, loan purpose or lender serviceability settings. The source guide also notes an APRA buffer in plain terms, so borrowers should treat tool outputs as a starting point rather than a lender decision.

Documentation And Timing Signals

A stronger loan review begins with documents and constraints, not a request for the lowest rate in isolation. The source guide says the enquiry form captures purchase stage, suburb, loan type and the main timing pressure. That information tells the broker whether the file is still exploratory or already moving toward a contract, refinance decision, build quote or settlement date.

For self-employed borrowers, the source guide highlights sole traders, contractors and business owners with variable or company income. For construction borrowers, it points to knockdown-rebuilds, house-and-land and major builds. For bridging borrowers, it refers to buying before selling or lining up settlement dates. Each scenario creates different questions about evidence, sequencing and lender policy.

Keep the first call factual. Bring the property context, the loan purpose, current debts, income structure and deadline. Then ask what must be verified before an application proceeds.

  1. Define the loan goal. State whether the file is for a first purchase, refinance, investment, construction, self-employed or bridging scenario.
  2. List the property context. Note the suburb or postcode, property type and purchase stage so location and valuation questions can be raised early.
  3. Record the timing pressure. Write down any finance deadline, settlement issue or build sequencing concern before the broker call.
  4. Separate facts from assumptions. Treat calculator results as general guidance until a licensed broker reviews the full circumstances.
  5. Ask what must be verified. Request a clear list of documents, policy checks and scheme checks needed before an application proceeds.
