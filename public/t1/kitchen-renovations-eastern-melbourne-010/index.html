Quote checks before you compare builders

The source page says each project starts with a measure-up and an itemised fixed-price written quote. A quote is easier to compare when it separates layout, cabinetry, benchtops, trades, appliances, splashback and finishing rather than presenting one broad allowance.

Before accepting a proposal, ask what has been measured, what has only been assumed, and what would trigger a variation. If drawings are supplied before cabinetry is made, use them to test storage, appliance positions, lighting and movement around the kitchen while changes are still on paper.

  • For a makeover, confirm which existing elements stay in place.
  • For a rebuild, confirm the order of strip-out, rough-in, cabinetry, benchtop templating and finishing.
  • For custom joinery, ask how drawers, butler's pantry storage and cabinet depths are specified.

Contracts, permits and compliance questions

The source page states that projects run under a written contract with the right permits and registered plumbing and electrical trades, in line with Victorian building requirements. It also states that a major domestic building contract over $10,000 requires a written contract under the Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995.

Before signing, treat compliance as a document trail. Ask which permits apply to the specific scope, who is responsible for them, which trades are licensed or registered for regulated work, and what warranty and compliance paperwork will be handed over at completion.

The page also notes that domestic building insurance is required in Victoria above the current threshold, commonly cited there as $16,000, and says the live figure should be checked with the VBA. Use that as a prompt to verify the current threshold and certificate requirements for the contract value.

Benchtop and material decisions

The source page says engineered stone with 1% or more crystalline silica was banned for manufacture, supply, processing and installation across Australia from 1 July 2024 under model WHS Regulations. It says the service installs low-silica alternatives instead, including porcelain, sintered stone, natural granite or marble, solid timber, stainless steel, laminate, or low-silica engineered stone under 1% crystalline silica.

For a practical selection process, separate appearance from installation implications. Ask what can be templated for the existing cabinetry plan, what edge and join details are available, and whether the chosen surface suits the way the kitchen will be used. A lower-cost refresh may keep the layout while changing visible finishes, while a full rebuild can align surface selection with new storage and lighting.

  1. Measure the existing kitchen. Record the layout, access issues, appliance positions and what you want to keep before the first in-home measure-up.
  2. Define the scope. Decide whether the job is a refresh, full rebuild, custom cabinetry project or period-home renovation before comparing quotes.
  3. Review drawings. Check storage, lighting, benchtop runs and appliance clearances on the drawings before cabinetry is made.
  4. Check contract documents. Confirm the written scope, fixed price, dates, payment stages, permits, trades, variations process and handover paperwork.
  5. Select compliant materials. Ask which benchtop products are available under the current engineered-stone restrictions and how they will be installed.

Common questions

How long should an eastern Melbourne kitchen renovation take? The source page states that a typical full kitchen renovation runs about 6 to 12 weeks from contract to handover. It breaks that into roughly 4 to 6 weeks for cabinetry and stone fabrication, then usually 2 to 4 weeks on site for install, trades, tiling and finishing.

Is a makeover the same as a full rebuild? No. The source page describes a makeover as door, benchtop and splashback updates that modernise a kitchen without changing the layout. A full rebuild includes demolition, rough-in, cabinetry, benchtops and finishing.

What should be in a fixed-price kitchen quote? The useful details are the measured scope, materials, cabinetry, benchtops, trades, appliances, splashback, finishing and any exclusions. The source page says its projects start with an itemised, fixed-scope written quote after a measure-up.

Do Victorian kitchen projects need written contracts? The source page says a major domestic building contract over $10,000 requires a written contract under the Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995. It also says the contract sets out scope, price, dates, payment stages and the variations process.

What benchtop options are named after the engineered-stone ban? The source page names porcelain, sintered stone, natural granite or marble, solid timber, stainless steel, laminate, and low-silica engineered stone under 1% crystalline silica as alternatives it installs.
