Chattel Mortgage Australia Comparison Explained

A chattel mortgage is a distinct finance product that differs significantly from a standard hire purchase or finance lease. The defining characteristic is that your business takes legal ownership of the asset from the moment of purchase. The lender secures the loan by registering a charge on the Personal Property Securities Register, but the equipment remains your property. This means you can use the asset freely, sell it if necessary, and treat it as a standard business asset on your balance sheet. In contrast, a finance lease often keeps ownership with the financier until the final payment is made, effectively meaning you are renting the equipment.

This structure is particularly popular for trades, transport operators, and businesses that rely on heavy plant and equipment. Because you own the asset, you are not restricted by the conditions often imposed by lessors in a lease agreement. Tax benefits and ownership structure

Tax treatment is one of the primary reasons businesses choose a chattel mortgage. If your business is registered for GST, you can generally claim the GST included in the asset price as an input tax credit on your next Business Activity Statement. This effectively reduces the upfront cost of the asset because you receive a credit from the Australian Taxation Office. Your repayments themselves are not subject to GST, which simplifies your accounting.

Furthermore, because you own the asset, you are eligible to claim depreciation on it under standard Australian Taxation Office rules. You can also claim the interest component of each repayment as a tax deduction for the business-use portion of the asset. This creates a strong cash flow advantage, as you are reducing your taxable income through both the interest deduction and the depreciation schedule. Eligibility and who it suits

Eligibility for a chattel mortgage focuses on business use and registration. The asset must be used predominantly for business purposes, typically defined as more than 50 per cent of its use. You need an active Australian Business Number (ABN) and, ideally, to be registered for GST. This structure suits a wide range of entities, including sole traders, partnerships, companies, and trusts. Established operators may qualify for low-doc options that require fewer financial statements, making it accessible for businesses with a solid trading history.

This product tends to suit businesses that want to build equity in their equipment rather than renting it. If you plan to use the asset for several years and want to own it outright by the end of the loan term, a chattel mortgage is often the most tax-efficient structure. It is a flexible product for businesses that value ownership and the associated tax benefits.

Steps to secure a chattel mortgage

Setting up a chattel mortgage involves a few clear steps. First, obtain a quote or invoice for the asset with the GST-inclusive price. Decide on the loan term and whether to include a balloon payment to manage your cash flow. A balloon payment is a lump sum deferred to the end of the loan that lowers your monthly repayments because you are financing less across the term. You will then need to compare rates and structures across different lenders to find the best fit for your financial position. A broker can help you navigate these options.

Once you have chosen a lender, submit your application with your ABN and supporting documents. Upon settlement, the lender registers its interest on the PPSR, and you take ownership of the asset. It is important to run the numbers on the full term, not just the monthly payments, to ensure the balloon payment is manageable at the end of the loan period.

Common questions

Can I claim GST on a chattel mortgage? Yes, if your business is registered for GST, you can claim the GST component of the asset price as an input tax credit on your next Business Activity Statement.

What happens at the end of the loan term? You can pay out the remaining balance, refinance the residual, or settle the balloon payment against the sale of the asset.

Who is eligible for a chattel mortgage? Eligible entities include sole traders, partnerships, companies, and trusts with an active ABN that use the asset predominantly for business purposes.
