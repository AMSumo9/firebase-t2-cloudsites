Technical repair queue, not just an audit PDF

Technical SEO earns its place on the checklist only when findings turn into a sequenced fix list. The published scope points to crawlability, indexation, Core Web Vitals, mobile usability and structured data as the areas that decide whether pages can be found, understood and used reliably.

  • Access: confirm nothing blocks search engines from reaching the pages you want indexed.
  • Duplicates and thin pages: flag weak or repeated content so it does not dilute stronger pages.
  • Speed: note load issues that affect both rankings and user behaviour.
  • Mobile behaviour: check the site performs properly on phones, where most local browsing happens.
  • Structured data: identify markup opportunities that help search engines interpret the pages.

Ask the provider to sort findings into urgent repairs and items that can wait. A smaller Brisbane site may only need a fixed discovery pass to judge whether monthly work is warranted; a larger site with many service or location pages needs checks to continue as new content appears, so fresh crawl or indexation faults do not accumulate unnoticed.

Local visibility starts with intent, not postcodes

The published scope names Brisbane CBD, Fortitude Valley, Newstead, West End and South Brisbane among its example planning areas. Use those names as prompts for questions rather than as a finished strategy. The real test is how customers in each area actually phrase their searches and which result format, map listings or standard organic results, holds the opportunity.

  • A nearby, ready-to-book search leans on profile optimisation, service-area wording, citations and review handling.
  • A research-heavy question needs a page that explains the offering clearly enough to compete beyond the map pack.
  • Suburb-specific wording belongs only where coverage is genuine, with next steps obvious on the page.

On your checklist, write one line per priority area pairing the suburb, the likely intent and the page that will answer it. If the provider cannot name the page for a given area, the coverage claim is not yet a plan.

Content and authority mapped to a sales path

Content activity passes the checklist only when every planned page has a job. Decide which searches justify a service page, which suit a supporting resource and which attract traffic without commercial value and should be left alone. The published approach describes keyword-mapped content tied to topical authority and conversion, which is the discipline that stops a blog from collecting visits that never enquire.

Authority work needs the same specificity. The scope describes backlinks built through digital PR, supplier mentions and resource placements, which is a sharper standard than a raw link count. Add three lines to your checklist:

  • Which placement is being pursued and why it is relevant to the business.
  • Which priority page each placement is meant to support.
  • How authority work connects to the pages that carry the commercial goal.

If the answer is a volume target rather than a relevance reason, the line stays unticked.

Reporting that separates tasks from results

The final block covers what lands in your inbox each month. The published cadence is monthly reporting spanning keyword movement, organic traffic and conversions. Read these together: positions can lift without enquiries, sessions can grow from low-value queries, and conversions go uncounted when forms, calls and enquiry paths are not tracked properly.

  • What changed: fixes shipped, content published and placements earned during the month.
  • What moved: visibility and traffic shifts for the agreed terms and areas.
  • What happens next: one clear recommendation rather than a task dump.

Keep paid and organic numbers distinct, since paid search can test demand quickly while organic pages and authority compound over time. Finally, hold the no-guarantee line: the published position is that no ethical agency can promise exact ranking positions, and certainty claims are a reason to pause, not a reason to sign.   1. Set the commercial goal. Write down what an enquiry is worth, which services matter most and the result search traffic should support before contacting any provider.
  2. Prepare your context. Have the website, service mix, priority suburbs and business goal ready so discovery can separate quick repairs from longer campaign work.
  3. Compare written scopes. Request deliverables, timing, cost and review points in writing, and check that baseline metrics are recorded before work begins.
  4. Review monthly against evidence. Each month, match completed tasks to visibility movement and tracked conversions, keeping paid and organic results clearly separated.
