Renovation Builders Brisbane Council and Approvals Explained

The fastest way to sort approvals is to name the job correctly before anyone prices it. Cosmetic and internal-only work typically does not require planning approval. Once a project changes the building footprint or structure, council approval enters the picture. Structural alterations, extensions and significant layout changes call for a building permit. Properties under a heritage overlay need additional approval on top of the standard pathway.

Queenslander work often mixes several triggers at once, such as timber restoration, VJ wall repairs or raise and build-under planning, so it rarely fits a single category. For the published figures and screening steps that sit behind these approval questions, see the full guide.

Planning approval, building approval or both

Two separate tracks are often confused. Planning approval deals with footprint, use and character, and runs through council or town planning. Building approval confirms the work meets the building code, and is usually handled through a building certifier. A project can need one, the other, or both, depending on the site and the design.

A builder can explain the construction implications of each pathway, but approval advice should be matched to the site, the drawings and the assessment route. For site-specific answers, the source guide recommends speaking with a building certifier or town planner rather than relying on a generic answer. Keep licence checks in view at the same time: the listed builders are described as QBCC licensed, with HIA and Master Builders Queensland memberships used as screening signals.

Where approvals sit inside the schedule

Approval time is part of the project, not an extra. The published sequence runs feasibility at 2 to 4 weeks, then design and documentation at 2 to 4 months, which is when drawings for assessment are prepared. Pre-construction takes about 2 to 4 weeks before work on site begins. On site, kitchens run 4 to 8 weeks and bathrooms 2 to 4 weeks, while a full home renovation typically takes 5 to 12 months including approvals.

That gap between design and construction is where approval risk lives. Ask each builder when design decisions lock in, who orders fixtures, which rooms become unavailable, and who manages the certifier, town planning or council interface. A quote that ignores this stage can look cheaper for the wrong reason.

Questions to ask before you sign

Approval responsibilities belong in writing, not in conversation. Before appointing anyone, confirm QBCC licence details, ask for examples of comparable Brisbane work, and request a clear statement of what is included. Check whether the builder carries approval tasks, or whether they sit with you, your designer or your certifier.

Price comparison only works when every builder answers the same brief. Request allowances, exclusions, likely sequencing and variation rules. A lower headline figure is misleading if design, certification, structural work, fixtures or site preparation fall outside the quote. If approval assumptions differ between quotes, the numbers are not comparable.

Cost ranges as approval context

Approval load tracks with project scale. The published ranges put kitchens at $25,000 to $60,000+ and bathrooms at $22,000 to $35,000+, which are usually internal jobs with lighter approval needs. Extensions start from $150,000, standard 3 to 4 bedroom renovations sit between $250,000 and $450,000, and full home renovations run from $150,000 to $500,000+ depending on scope. Raise and build-under projects start from $650,000 and carry the heaviest approval and structural load.

  1. Name the work type. Decide whether the job is cosmetic, structural, an extension, a layout change or heritage-related, since that drives the approval pathway.
  2. Gather site facts. Note the affected rooms, whether walls move, plumbing or electrical changes, finish expectations and any character features to retain.
  3. Confirm credentials. Check QBCC licence details and ask for comparable Brisbane project experience, including heritage work where relevant.
  4. Obtain matched quotes. Request 2 to 3 obligation-free quotes covering the same scope, drawings and approval assumptions for a fair comparison.
  5. Lock responsibilities in writing. Record who manages the certifier, town planning or council interface, plus variation rules, before signing.
