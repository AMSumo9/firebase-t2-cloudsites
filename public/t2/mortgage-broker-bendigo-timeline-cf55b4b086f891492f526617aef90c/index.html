Mortgage Broker Bendigo Timeline Explained

Every purchase follows the same broad sequence, even though the details shift with the borrower. The journey starts with a conversation about goals, moves into document gathering and pre-approval, then into house hunting, a formal application, valuation and lender assessment, and finally unconditional approval and settlement. A broker can help first home buyers, refinancers, investors and pre-approval seekers across Greater Bendigo and Central Victoria, with no obligation and no cost for the introduction. Before any of this begins, it is worth preparing the right questions to ask a Bendigo mortgage broker so each stage has a clear purpose.

Stage one: enquiry, documents and pre-approval

The opening conversation is where the task gets defined: purchase, refinance, investment or pre-approval. From there the borrower assembles income evidence such as payslips, proof of identity, deposit savings history and a record of existing liabilities. Once a lender is selected, pre-approval gives an indication of how much that lender is likely to lend before house hunting begins.

  • Pre-approval is conditional, not a guarantee of final lending.
  • Validity typically sits around 90 days, depending on the lender.
  • Missing payslips or unclear deposit evidence can slow the assessment.

Stage two: house hunting inside the approval window

A 90 day window sounds generous until weekends of inspections pass without a result. Buyers still searching in Kangaroo Flat, Strathdale or surrounding suburbs near the end of that period should raise the expiry date with their broker early, because a stale approval may need updating before an offer becomes a live purchase. If income, employment or deposit position changes during the search, flagging it promptly keeps the file accurate rather than forcing a late reassessment.

Stage three: formal application through to settlement

Once a contract is signed, the file moves to formal application. The lender orders a valuation, verifies the documents already supplied and issues a decision. From application, settlement commonly takes around 4 to 6 weeks, depending on the lender and the borrower's circumstances. Treat that figure as a planning guide rather than a promise.

  • Valuation questions can add time if the property is unusual.
  • A change in employment during the process may require extra explanation.
  • Keeping the broker informed of any changes reduces surprises before formal approval.

Where first home buyer steps fit the timeline

Eligible first home buyers may be able to purchase with as little as a 5 percent deposit without lenders mortgage insurance, subject to price caps reviewed annually under the federal First Home Guarantee administered by Housing Australia. The Victorian First Home Owner Grant can also be discussed for eligible new home purchases. These scheme checks belong at the start of the timeline, not after a contract is signed, because eligibility depends on the property type, deposit source, savings history and purchase price fitting current scheme rules. A broker conversation should separate what is possible from what is formally approved.

Refinance and investor timelines run on different clocks

Refinancers are not racing a 90 day approval window in the same way. Their sequence turns on comparing the current bank's offer against suitable alternatives across the lender panel, weighing variable versus fixed rates, offset accounts, switching costs and whether debt consolidation or equity access changes the loan term and total repayment behaviour. Investors add structure questions, including interest only versus principal and interest, and how rental income and existing equity are presented to lenders. None of these timelines should be rushed, and tax advice should not be treated as the same thing as credit advice.
