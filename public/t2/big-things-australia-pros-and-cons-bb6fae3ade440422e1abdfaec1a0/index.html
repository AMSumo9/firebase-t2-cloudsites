Big Things Australia Pros and Cons Explained

Australia is renowned for its collection of oversized roadside attractions, affectionately known as 'Big Things'. These landmarks, ranging from giant fruit and animals to quirky everyday objects, have become iconic symbols of the Australian road trip. Their appeal lies in their novelty, their often humorous designs, and the unique photo opportunities they provide. For many travellers, a road trip across Australia wouldn't be complete without a stop at one or more of these colossal structures. They serve as excellent 'anchors' for a journey, breaking up long drives and adding a sense of fun and discovery to the itinerary.

The 'Big Things' can transform a standard drive into a memorable adventure. They offer a chance to engage with local culture and creativity, often reflecting the primary industry or a unique characteristic of the town or region they represent. Planning a route that incorporates these landmarks can lead to unexpected discoveries and a deeper appreciation for the diverse landscapes and communities of Australia. The key is to integrate them thoughtfully, ensuring they enhance, rather than detract from, the overall travel experience.

Potential Drawbacks and Planning Pitfalls

Despite their charm, visiting Australia's 'Big Things' is not without its potential challenges. The most significant pitfall is the risk of poor planning, which can turn an anticipated highlight into a frustrating detour. A common mistake is treating a national list of 'Big Things' as a definitive itinerary. This can lead to excessive driving, time wasted on inaccessible attractions, or stops that don't align with the overall travel plan.

Before committing to a detour, it's crucial to conduct thorough research. This includes verifying the landmark's current location, checking for any access restrictions or fees, and assessing the time required for the detour itself. Relying on outdated information or unofficial guides can result in disappointment if a landmark has been removed, relocated, or is no longer accessible. Furthermore, some 'Big Things' are integrated into urban environments as public art and are best enjoyed as part of a city exploration rather than a dedicated long-haul detour. Understanding this context is vital for realistic itinerary planning.

Strategic Route Planning: The Route-First Approach

To maximise the enjoyment and minimise the potential downsides of visiting Australia's 'Big Things', a 'route-first' mindset is essential. This strategy prioritises building your road trip itinerary around your primary destinations and then using the 'Big Things' as natural breaks or points of interest along the way. Instead of creating a trip solely to see these landmarks, you incorporate them into a journey that already has a defined path and realistic driving windows.

The process begins with selecting a state or territory and establishing a feasible driving schedule for that leg of the trip. Once this framework is in place, you can then identify 'Big Things' that are conveniently located along your intended route. This approach ensures that detours are practical and add value to the journey, rather than becoming a burden. It transforms the 'Big Things' from the sole purpose of the trip into delightful enhancements that make the travel experience more memorable and engaging.

Essential Checks Before You Detour

Before adding any 'Big Thing' to your itinerary, performing a few essential checks can save considerable time and prevent disappointment. These checks ensure that your planned stops are worthwhile and accessible.

  • Verify Location and Access: Always confirm the landmark's current status and accessibility. Look for official sources such as local council websites, park authorities, or visitor information centres. These sources provide up-to-date information on opening hours, parking availability, any entry fees, and specific access conditions.
  • Assess Detour Practicality: Consider the time required for the detour itself, including driving off the main highway, parking, and walking to the attraction. Factor in time for photos and any associated activities. If the detour significantly disrupts your schedule or adds excessive driving time, it might be wiser to skip it or find a more convenient alternative.
  • Determine Stop Usefulness: Ask yourself why this particular 'Big Thing' belongs on your route. Is it a convenient rest stop with a unique photo opportunity? Is it located in or near a town you wished to explore? Does it offer a chance to experience local public art or naturally pair with another planned destination? If a 'Big Thing' doesn't add genuine value or convenience to your journey, it might be best to leave it off the itinerary.
