Switchboard Fault Finding Perth Explained

Information published by Switchboard Upgrades Perth separates a single faulty breaker from signs that the whole board needs attention. A main circuit that keeps tripping, power dropping out at night without an obvious explanation, or old ceramic fuses all justify an electrician's assessment. The result may be a repair, several safety switches or a complete replacement.

Record what happens before requesting a quote. Note which circuit trips, whether the outage affects the whole home, whether it happens at a particular time and whether a recent appliance or renovation changed the load. Do not assume that a tripping breaker alone proves the board must be replaced. Testing the existing circuits gives the electrician a basis for recommending the narrower repair or a new board.

  • Repeated trips on one circuit can point to a local fault that needs investigation.
  • Ceramic fuses, no RCDs or no spare room are indicators that a new board may be appropriate.
  • A crowded board can become harder to extend when solar, air conditioning or an EV charger is planned.

What the quote needs to account for

The published Perth pricing range for a standard home is $800 to $2,500 as at October 2026. That is a guide, not a fixed price. Board size, the number of circuits and the condition of wiring behind the board can change the work required. Adding a few safety switches is a different job from moving every circuit into a new enclosure.

Ask the electrician to separate the essential work from optional future capacity. The quote should make clear whether it includes circuit breakers, RCDs, circuit labels, removal of the old board, testing, any wiring repairs and the certificate issued after installation work. If the meter side is involved, ask whether Western Power needs to cut the supply before work begins.

An older board may have a cement sheet backing panel containing asbestos. The electrician can explain how it will be assessed and whether panel replacement is included. This is a quote question, not a detail to leave until the old board is being removed.

Repair, replacement or room for future loads

A repair can make sense when testing identifies a limited fault and the existing board remains suitable for the home's needs. Replacement becomes more likely where ceramic fuses, absent RCD protection, crowded breakers or insufficient room make the current arrangement unsuitable. The electrician confirms the recommendation on site.

Selling, leasing and older Perth homes

Selling or newly leasing a WA home changes the practical test. The published guidance states that at least two RCDs must protect power and lighting circuits in that situation. An older board may have none, so the owner or agent should ask for the existing protection to be checked before arranging a campaign or tenancy.

Local housing stock can also affect the inspection questions. Properties with older ceramic fuse boards, crowded breakers or backing panels need different quote inputs from homes with a relatively modern board. The relevant locality is the property and its electrical setup, not a suburb label alone.

Plan the outage and paperwork

Power is off while the old board is removed and the replacement is wired in, then it comes back on. It may not be off all day, but the actual duration depends on the number of circuits being moved and whether wiring needs repair. A small home with a tidy board can differ from a crowded installation.

Before booking, tell the electrician about the fridge, anyone working from home and nearby equipment that cannot simply be switched off. Confirm how the outage will be managed and what testing follows the installation. In Western Australia, electrical work must be completed by a person holding the appropriate electrical licence, and the homeowner is owed an electrical safety certificate within 28 days after installation work.

The certificate is useful evidence when the home is sold or leased. It also gives the owner a record of what work was completed, alongside the quote and any information about the board's future capacity.

  1. Describe the fault. Record which circuit trips, when the outage occurs and whether an appliance or renovation preceded it.
  2. Arrange an assessment. Have an electrician inspect and test the existing board and circuits before deciding between repair and replacement.
  3. Review the quote. Check circuit count, protection, labels, wiring repairs, asbestos-panel work, meter arrangements and future capacity.
  4. Plan the outage. Discuss the fridge, home-working needs and any Western Power supply isolation before the installation date.
  5. Keep the certificate. Retain the electrical safety certificate issued after installation work with the quote and property records.

Common questions

How much does a switchboard upgrade cost in Perth? The published guide gives a standard home range of $800 to $2,500 as at October 2026. Board size, circuit count and wiring behind the board affect the final written quote.

Does repeated tripping always mean the board must be replaced? No. One faulty breaker or circuit may call for a repair, while ceramic fuses, missing RCDs or limited space may support replacement. An electrician confirms the cause after inspection and testing.

How long will the power be off? Power is off while the old board is removed and the replacement is wired, then it comes back on. The electrician can give a timeframe after seeing the number of circuits and the condition of the wiring.

What changes if I am selling or leasing the home? The published guidance says a WA home sold or newly leased needs at least two RCDs protecting power and lighting circuits. Ask for the board and protection to be checked before the transaction or tenancy.

When should I receive the electrical safety certificate? For installation work in Western Australia, the certificate is owed within 28 days. Keep it with the quote and property records because it can be useful when the home is sold or leased.
