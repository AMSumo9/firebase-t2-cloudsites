What a written quote should spell out

Treat the document as the record of the agreement. It should show the scope, measurement basis, visible assumptions and terms before work is booked. If a boundary issue, pool status or access constraint was raised and then left out, ask for the quote to be corrected rather than guessing that it is covered.

  • Scope: height, material, gates, corners, returns and any retaining interaction.
  • Site: slope, access limits, corner block sight lines and exposure to salt air or wind.
  • Existing fence: condition, removal, disposal and making good after posts come out.
  • Compliance: whether the work may require a current QBCC licence with suitable scope.

Site conditions that move the number

The same run length can change once the ground is considered. Coastal sand, riverfront alluvium and hinterland clay affect how a post is dug and held. Beachfront exposure from Noosa Heads to Marcoola points toward corrosion resistance and fixing detail, while established hinterland streets often keep timber in the conversation for shared dividing fences. Around Doonan, Eumundi and Kin Kin, acreage and paddock boundaries can raise rural fencing, livestock and access questions.

  • Ask how the contractor allowed for footing conditions, not only panel count.
  • Ask how corners, returns and hard to reach boundaries are priced.
  • Use a calculator only as a planning range and conversation starter, never as a fixed offer.

Shared fences and pool barriers change the process

A dividing fence is part construction and part neighbour process. Queensland's Neighbourhood Disputes (Dividing Fences and Trees) Act 2011 is the named framework when adjoining owners consider a sufficient dividing fence. Begin with a written description of the work and at least one quote. Premium material, extra height or a higher finish is not automatically shared; where one owner wants more than sufficient, the difference may sit with that owner. If agreement is not reached, the general pathway described is a notice to contribute, one month to agree after the notice, then a possible QCAT application within two months of the notice. That is general information, not legal advice for a particular boundary. Pool barriers are compliance led and separate from ordinary boundary work; approval is confirmed by a licensed pool safety inspector, not by a contractor quote. A short correction loop before acceptance

Before saying yes, read the quote against the brief you gave. Mark every missing item: access, removal, disposal, neighbour assumptions, pool status, gate detail, returns and exposure. Send one short correction request and ask for an updated version. If the final scope is still vague, pause rather than accepting the cheapest line.

  1. Underline each assumption that could change price or method.
  2. Confirm which inclusions are fixed and which are provisional.
  3. Check that shared fence steps and pool safety steps are not blurred together.
  4. Accept only after omissions are corrected in writing.
  1. Write the brief. List suburb, fence line, old fence condition, access limits, height idea, gates, corners, returns and whether the run touches a neighbour, pool, slope or acreage boundary.
  2. Classify the site. Separate beachfront salt and wind from hinterland streets and acreage runs so material talk starts from purpose and environment.
  3. Resolve process questions. For shared boundaries prepare a written description and quote; for pools treat approval as inspector confirmed; for licensing ask whether a current QBCC licence with suitable scope may be needed.
  4. Compare inclusions. Request scope, measurement basis, assumptions and terms, then correct omissions before accepting any quote.

Common questions

Why can one quote be much lower for the same fence length? It may omit removal, disposal, access difficulty, footing conditions, gate hardware, returns or making good. A low headline rate can describe a narrower job, so compare inclusions and assumptions rather than the number alone.

What should I send before asking for a quote? Send the suburb, fence line, old fence details, access limits, intended height, gates, corners, returns and whether the project involves a neighbour, pool, slope or acreage boundary. Clear detail helps expose unknowns early.

Does a contractor quote settle pool safety approval? No. Pool barriers are compliance led and separate from ordinary boundary work. The inspection pathway is confirmed by a licensed pool safety inspector, so a quote should not be treated as the final word on pool safety approval.
