Quote checks that reveal scope

A useful written quote should make the hidden work visible. The source page links wall durability to footings, subsoil drainage and approval, so the quote should not stop at length, height and face material.

  • Site conditions: slope, retained height, access, boundary position and nearby structures.
  • Wall type: sleepers, blocks, rock, boulder or gabion, matched to the job.
  • Drainage: ag-drain, gravel, geofabric and a lawful stormwater outlet inside the boundary.
  • Approvals: whether height or surcharge brings engineering, certification and council approval into scope.
  • Finish: backfill, capping, clean-up and how the finished level meets paths, fences or garden beds.

If two quotes differ sharply, compare inclusions before comparing price. One may include design, excavation, drainage and paperwork, while another may leave those items to be resolved later.

Engineering and approval triggers

The published page gives a clear boundary: walls over 1.0 metre, or walls carrying a driveway or building, are designed to AS 4678 with RPEQ Form 15 and Form 16 certification and Toowoomba Regional Council approval arranged before work starts.

The load question can matter even where the visible wall looks modest. A wall near a driveway, structure, boundary or cut and fill change should prompt a direct question about who identifies the load case, who engages engineering, and who manages approval documents.

For repairs, ask for assessment before accepting a replacement price. A leaning, bulging or cracked wall may reflect missing drainage, inadequate footings or a wall type that did not suit the site.

Choosing the wall type

The service page describes concrete sleepers as steel-reinforced sleepers set between galvanised posts, a practical option for local blocks. Timber sleepers are positioned for gardens, terraces and low boundary retaining. Core-filled Besser block walls are described for taller engineered retaining and rendered finishes.

Rock, boulder and gabion walls are tied to sloping or free-draining situations. On a steep block, ask how the chosen system affects footprint, backfill, drainage and access for machinery. On a tight boundary, ask how posts, footings or baskets will fit without leaving unresolved water behind the wall.

No listed material removes the drainage question. The source page names ag-drain in free-draining gravel, geofabric to keep clay out of the drain, and connection to a legal stormwater point inside the boundary.

Who this applies to

This guide is for Toowoomba and Darling Downs homeowners dealing with level changes, steep range blocks, reactive black soil, failing walls or a proposed wall that may need approval. It is aimed at structural retaining decisions rather than decorative edging.

Rangeville, Mount Lofty and East Toowoomba are named in the source page because east-facing range blocks can add slope, seepage and erosion to the local soil issue. That does not make every property in those suburbs the same. It means sloping sites need careful drainage, excavation and engineering questions before the wall type is locked in.

Before requesting a quote, gather photos, approximate height and length, access notes, the reason for the wall and any visible leaning, cracking or wet ground. That brief helps a licensed specialist separate a simple landscape wall from a structural retaining job.

  1. Describe the site. Share wall height, length, slope, access, nearby structures and any wet or cracked areas.
  2. Ask about approval. Confirm whether height or retained load requires AS 4678 engineering, RPEQ certification and council approval.
  3. Check drainage detail. Ask where the ag-drain, gravel, geofabric and stormwater connection will sit in the finished build.
  4. Compare written scope. Judge quotes by included design, excavation, footings, drainage, backfill, capping and clean-up, not only price.

Common questions

Do all retaining walls in Toowoomba need engineering? The source page states that walls over 1.0 metre, or walls carrying a driveway or building, are designed to AS 4678 with RPEQ certification and council approval. For smaller walls, ask the builder to explain the site basis for that decision.

Why is drainage treated as structural? The published page says Darling Downs reactive black clay swells when wet and shrinks when dry, which can push, rotate and crack walls not built for it. It names ag-drain, gravel, geofabric and legal stormwater connection as core details.

Which wall material suits a sloping block? There is no single answer. The source page lists concrete sleeper, timber sleeper, Besser block, rock, boulder and gabion options, with selection depending on height, slope, soil, load and finish.

What should I prepare before asking for a quote? Prepare photos, approximate wall height and length, access notes, the wall purpose and any signs of leaning, cracking, seepage or previous failure. Those details help the quote address drainage, footing and approval scope.
