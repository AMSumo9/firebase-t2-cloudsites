Matching your purpose to the right category

The directory separates construction and specialist lending, first home buyer brokers, general home loan brokers, investment loan brokers and refinance brokers. This distinction matters because a purchase, refinance or investment discussion can turn on different documents, timing pressures and property plans. For example, a first home buyer may need help with the first home buyer grant, while an investor might focus on cash flow and negative gearing. A home loan brokers guide that aligns with your specific goal will provide more relevant advice. Construction and specialist lending often require different documentation than standard home loans, so choosing the right category early saves time. Refinancing discussions usually focus on exit fees and repayment structures, whereas investment lending requires a closer look at negative gearing and cash flow. First home buyers should ask about entry-level products and grant eligibility, while those looking to refinance should focus on whether a change will lower repayments or offer better features. Investment loan brokers can help structure loans to maximise tax benefits, while construction brokers manage progress payments linked to building milestones.

What to ask before you apply

Once you reach out, compare how each broker handles the substance of the loan discussion. ASIC Moneysmart notes that home loans can differ in fees, features and flexibility, and that borrowers should understand the total commitment before proceeding. Ask each broker to walk through the same set of issues so you can compare like with like. For an owner-occupier purchase, ask how they would structure the next steps and what information they need first. For a refinance, ask what changes they would test against your current arrangement. For an investment property, Moneysmart's property investment guidance is a reminder to discuss costs, cash flow pressure and downside scenarios, not just borrowing capacity. The strongest comparison is often the broker who identifies the awkward parts early, rather than the one who gives the smoothest first impression. Ask for the order of steps, not just the end result. Ask what documents would likely matter first. Ask which loan features are worth comparing for your case, and which are noise. Consider asking about interest-only options or lenders mortgage insurance if these factors are relevant to your situation.
