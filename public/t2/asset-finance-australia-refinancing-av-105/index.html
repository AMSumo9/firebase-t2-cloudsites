Asset Finance Australia Refinancing Explained

Businesses typically look to refinance when the initial structure no longer aligns with their cash flow or when the asset's life extends beyond the loan term. A common trigger is the arrival of a balloon or residual payment at the end of a facility. Rather than paying a large lump sum, many operators choose to refinance the residual amount over a new term, smoothing out the expense.

Another driver is the desire to lower interest rates. If your business profile has improved since the original purchase, through stronger trading history or increased credit strength, you may qualify for a sharper rate. Refinancing also allows you to consolidate multiple equipment loans into a single facility, simplifying administration and potentially freeing up working capital.

Selecting the right structure for refinancing

The structure you choose for the new loan will determine tax treatment and ownership. If your initial arrangement was a finance lease where the financier owned the asset, refinancing via a chattel mortgage can shift ownership to you. This change allows you to claim GST and depreciation benefits. Conversely, if you want to keep the asset off your balance sheet, you might refinance into another rental or operating lease.

Eligibility and valuation criteria

Approval for refinancing hinges on the asset's current market value. Lenders require the item to remain tangible, identifiable and resaleable to use it as security. As equipment ages, its value depreciates, which may affect the loan-to-value ratio (LVR) the lender is willing to offer. Newer, mainstream equipment typically attracts better terms than ageing or specialised plant.

Your business profile remains a critical factor. Lenders will review your ABN, trading history and recent financials to ensure you can service the new repayments. If the asset has been maintained well and holds significant resale value, it strengthens your application and can lead to more favourable interest rates compared to unsecured borrowing options.

The application process

Refinancing follows a similar path to the initial purchase application. You must confirm the asset's details and provide evidence of its current value, often through a valuation or insurer quote. It is important to gather your business documents, including financial statements and tax returns, to demonstrate your capacity to repay.

Comparing structured quotes is vital. Look beyond the headline interest rate to examine the balloon amount, term length and any fees associated with early payout or establishment. Once you select a suitable offer, the new lender pays out the existing facility, and the title or security interest is transferred to the new financier. Ensure you retain all settlement documents for your accountant and Business Activity Statement (BAS) records.

Key takeaway

Refinancing business equipment allows you to adjust loan terms, manage balloon payments or access equity tied up in owned assets. The process involves using the resale value of the tangible item to secure new funding, often for better rates or to consolidate debt. Success depends on the asset age, type and your business trading history.

Common questions

Can I refinance if I have bad credit? It depends on the severity of the credit issues and the asset's value. Strong equity in a tangible asset can support an application, though rates may be higher than for prime borrowers.

Is there a penalty for refinancing early? Some contracts include early termination fees or break costs. You should check your current loan agreement or request a payout figure to calculate the true cost of refinancing.

What happens if the asset value has dropped? A lower valuation may reduce the amount a lender is willing to finance. You may need to contribute a cash deposit to cover the gap between the old loan balance and the new secured limit.
