Written scope and VBA

The strongest practical filter in the source material is written scope. It says the quoting contractor should set out the renovation scope, exclusions and price in writing before demolition or ordering begins. For homeowners, that is where vague optimism needs to turn into specific questions. Ask what is included, what is excluded, who handles cabinetmaking, stone installation, plumbing and electrical work, and whether the job is being led by the right specialist for that part of the project. The page also states that work is carried out by an independent building contractor or cabinetmaker registered with the Victorian Building Authority for the class of work involved. This registration is a concrete checkpoint. If the proposal covers multiple elements, ask which trade is responsible for each part and whether the paperwork reflects that division.

Local factors and budget

The cost guidance on the source page is deliberately broad, which is more honest than pretending every kitchen can be priced from one headline figure. It says a full kitchen renovation in Melbourne typically ranges from the low tens of thousands into higher brackets, with cost pushed by kitchen size, finish level, plumbing or layout changes, custom cabinetry, stone benchtops, premium appliances, and any structural work. That means the budget conversation should be built around choices, not just a target number. It also notes that many homeowners allow a contingency of around 10 to 15 percent for unexpected issues. In practice, that is a planning tool, not permission to leave the brief loose. If you are still comparing options, it can be more useful to ask for scenarios such as cabinetry-only, benchtop-plus-cabinetry, or full rebuild than to chase one average price.
