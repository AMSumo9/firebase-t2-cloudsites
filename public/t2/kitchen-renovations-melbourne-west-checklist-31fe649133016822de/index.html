Kitchen Renovations Melbourne West Checklist Explained

Every useful kitchen renovations melbourne west begins with one decision: is this a cosmetic refresh or a full rebuild? A refresh keeps the existing plan and replaces visible surfaces such as door fronts, the work surface and the splashback. A rebuild goes further, relocating services, changing cabinetry runs and sometimes opening the room up entirely.

Before any colour or handle is chosen, record the room itself:

  • Wall lengths, ceiling height and the size of every window and door opening
  • Width and depth available for the fridge, oven, cooktop and dishwasher
  • Storage that fails today, such as a packed pantry, dead corners or nowhere to conceal the bin
  • Which elements are genuinely in scope: flooring, lighting, the splashback, the benchtop and the layout

The service model described for the western suburbs includes a no-cost in-home visit to measure the space and an itemised written quote at a fixed price, so a precise brief means that visit produces sharper numbers.

Reading a quote line by line

An itemised quote should show the benchtop, cabinetry, appliance installation, splashback, plumbing, electrical work and finishing as separate lines rather than one lump sum. That structure lets a homeowner compare two quotes honestly and see where one allowance is thin.

Three checks catch most problems:

  • Provisional sums. Anything provisional, excluded or dependent on what demolition uncovers should be flagged before signing.
  • Benchtop detail. Confirm the allowance reflects the actual material, slab size, joins, cut-outs and installation conditions rather than a placeholder figure.
  • Variations. Ask how changes are priced, approved and recorded once work is under way.

A fixed price only protects a household when the work it covers is described tightly. If two quotes differ widely, the gap is usually scope, not generosity.

Contracts, permits and the paperwork folder

For domestic building work over $10,000, Victorian law requires a written major domestic building contract under the Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995. Before signing, confirm the document sets out the scope, the total price, the payment stages and the process for variations. Plumbing and electrical work should be carried out by registered trades, and the permits should match the agreed scope.

Keep a single folder from day one. It should hold the signed contract, permit and compliance paperwork, and, at the end, warranty documents for cabinets, appliances and benchtops. Neutral references including ASIC Moneysmart, the Housing Industry Association and Master Builders Australia publish general guidance on contracts, budgeting and choosing a builder, while the quote itself must carry the exact inclusions for the individual kitchen.

A full project typically runs six to twelve weeks from signed contract to handover. Most of that time is off site: cabinetry and benchtop fabrication usually occupies four to six weeks, while the on-site stage of demolition, installation, plumbing, electrical and finishing generally takes two to four weeks. Benchtops after the engineered stone ban

Since 1 July 2024, engineered stone containing 1 per cent or more crystalline silica has been banned across Australia for manufacture, supply, processing and installation. Homeowners are now directed toward laminate, solid timber, natural stone, porcelain and sintered stone, stainless steel and compliant low-silica products.

Material choice belongs in the checklist early because benchtops are measured, fabricated and installed as part of a sequence with cabinetry, sink type, appliance layout and edge treatment. Ask whether the quoted allowance reflects real slab sizes, joins and cut-outs, or whether those details remain open. Choosing the material before drawings are finalised avoids a costly redesign once templating has happened.

Who this checklist suits

This checklist is written for owners planning kitchen work across Melbourne's west who are weighing a surface refresh against a custom cabinetry upgrade or a complete rebuild. It is especially handy for period-home owners who want character kept while day-to-day function improves, and for newer-home owners chasing better storage, more bench space or a smarter appliance layout.

It is less relevant for renters without owner approval, and for minor repairs that involve no design or installation work. Tradies quoting the job will still run their own contract and compliance processes; this list simply helps the household side of the table ask sharper questions.

  1. Define the scope. Decide whether the project is a surface refresh or a rebuild, then list what stays and what moves.
  2. Measure and record the room. Note wall lengths, ceiling height, openings and appliance dimensions before selecting finishes.
  3. Collect itemised quotes. Ask for the benchtop, cabinetry, appliances, splashback, plumbing, electrical and finishing work as separate lines, with provisional items flagged.
  4. Check contract and compliance. Confirm a written major domestic building contract for work over $10,000, the permits that apply and how variations are handled.
  5. Plan for the on-site weeks. Arrange a temporary cooking setup for the two to four weeks of demolition and installation.
