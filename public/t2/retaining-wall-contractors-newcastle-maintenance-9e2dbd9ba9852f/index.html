Approval Questions To Ask Early

In New South Wales, a retaining wall is considered exempt development only if it satisfies all the standards outlined in the SEPP (Exempt and Complying Development Codes) 2008. Key checkpoints for exempt status include a maximum height of 600mm, a minimum distance of 1 metre from each lot boundary, and a minimum distance of 2 metres from any other retaining wall. These figures are fundamental for initial project discussions.

For walls that exceed these exempt standards, either a Complying Development Certificate or a Development Application will be necessary. It is advisable to consult with the local council or a private certifier before commencing any work. When reviewing quotes, inquire about the approval path each contractor assumes. A comprehensive response will clarify whether the wall is being treated as exempt development, complying development, or requires a development application, and whether engineering services are needed due to factors like height, surcharge loads, or other conditions outside the exempt criteria.

Drainage In The Written Scope

The importance of proper drainage for retaining walls cannot be overstated, as it is a critical factor in preventing failures. The guide specifies that elements such as agricultural drain pipes, gravel backfill, and geotextile fabric should be explicitly detailed in the quote, rather than assumed. Effective drainage is essential for managing water pressure behind the wall, which is a common cause of residential wall failures.

When comparing quotes, pay close attention to the drainage provisions. A visually appealing wall face is insufficient if the quote does not adequately address how water accumulation behind the structure will be managed. Key details to confirm include the inclusion of ag pipes, appropriate gravel backfill, geotextile layers, and weep holes where applicable. Consideration should also be given to surface water management that could potentially concentrate behind the wall. Given the diverse ground conditions in the Newcastle and Lake Macquarie regions, including coastal sand, reactive clay, estuarine flats, and former colliery sites, the quote must demonstrate that drainage and subsoil decisions have been thoroughly considered before construction commences.

Choosing A Wall System

The selection of a retaining wall system depends heavily on the specific site conditions and project requirements. Concrete sleeper walls are often favoured for sloping backyards and ridge blocks due to their durability and ease of installation. Timber sleeper walls are a suitable option for garden terracing where the change in elevation is relatively minor.

Besser block walls offer robust capacity and can be rendered to match the aesthetic of inner-city properties with limited space. For a more natural and rugged appearance, rock and boulder walls are ideal for escarpment or bushland-edge blocks. Gabion walls, which consist of wire baskets filled with rocks, provide excellent drainage and are well-suited for sandy or erosion-prone ground. Engineered walls are the recommended solution for projects involving significant surcharge loads or those falling outside the standard exempt development criteria.

While a broader guide can offer insights into various retaining wall options, the final decision should be based on a thorough site assessment. It is beneficial to ask contractors to explain why certain options were rejected and to provide clear reasoning based on factors like height, soil conditions, site access, desired finish, retained load, and the applicable approval pathway. This detailed explanation is more valuable than a quote that simply names a product without justification.

Common questions

What are the NSW exempt development rules for retaining walls? In NSW, a retaining wall is exempt development if it is no taller than 600mm, at least 1 metre from each lot boundary, and at least 2 metres from any other retaining wall. It must also comply with the SEPP (Exempt and Complying Development Codes) 2008.

When is engineering required for a retaining wall? Engineering is typically required for retaining walls that exceed the exempt development height limits, are subject to significant surcharge loads (like driveways or buildings uphill), or are built on challenging soil conditions. It ensures the wall's structural integrity and compliance with standards like AS 4678.

Why is drainage so important for retaining walls? Water build-up behind a retaining wall creates hydrostatic pressure, which can lead to leaning, bulging, or catastrophic failure. Proper drainage systems, including ag pipes and gravel backfill, relieve this pressure and are crucial for the wall's longevity.
