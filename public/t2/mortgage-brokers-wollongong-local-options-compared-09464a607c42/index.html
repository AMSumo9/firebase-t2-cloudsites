Questions Revealing Lender Fit

A mortgage broker can compare options from multiple lenders, whereas a bank typically offers only its own products. Australian brokers are legally obligated to act in the borrower's best interests and must explain why a recommended loan is suitable. Regulatory bodies like ASIC Moneysmart also advise borrowers to fully understand loan features, repayment terms, and associated costs before making a decision.

To gain clarity, ask questions that prompt detailed reasoning:

  • Which lender policies are most compatible with this income structure, deposit level, and property plan?
  • What documentation is required to ensure pre-approval is meaningful?
  • What factors could potentially complicate or delay the application process?
  • How is the broker compensated, and will there be any direct fees applicable to me?
  • What are the steps involved between securing pre-approval, obtaining formal approval, and finalising settlement?

The question of fees should be addressed early. Most Australian mortgage brokers are remunerated via commission from the lender and do not charge a direct fee to standard home loan customers. However, some may charge for complex lending scenarios or specialised advice, and this should be clearly disclosed upfront.

Comparing Broker Support Practically

Practical comparison of broker support involves looking beyond general promises. Seek clear disclosure regarding credit licence arrangements, broker accreditations, remuneration structures, and the range of lenders considered. Enquiries are typically handled by brokers accredited with the Mortgage and Finance Association of Australia (MFAA) or the Finance Brokers Association of Australia (FBAA), operating under an Australian Credit Licence. These credentials should be provided in writing before you commit to engaging their services. It's important not to equate a rapid response time with a guaranteed loan approval. While brokers aim for same-day responses to initial enquiries, the actual loan assessment is contingent on the lender's criteria, the completeness of your documentation, the property specifics, and your individual financial circumstances.

  1. Define Your Goal. Clearly identify whether you are buying your first home, refinancing an existing loan, investing in property, building a new home, need bridging finance, or have self-employed income.
  2. Gather Your Information. Prepare details about your income type (PAYG, self-employed), deposit amount, desired property location or type, and any time pressures for settlement or construction.
  3. Prepare Key Questions. Formulate questions about lender policies, required documentation, potential application challenges, broker fees, and the steps from pre-approval to settlement.
  4. Enquire with Brokers. Contact local mortgage brokers, noting their response times and how they explain lender fit based on your specific situation.
  5. Assess Broker Disclosure. Review their credit licence, accreditations, and how they are remunerated. Ensure transparency regarding the lenders they work with.
