Conveyancing Services Brisbane Explained

Brisbane Conveyancers describes the work as contract review, property searches, transfer duty calculation, lender liaison and settlement through PEXA, with legal advice provided by Queensland Law Society-member solicitors. Use that as the checklist for any written quote.

A fixed professional fee should still show what sits outside that fee. The published page separates solicitor fees from government charges, searches, registration costs and transfer duty. Ask for the inclusions in writing before the file opens, especially if the contract has unusual conditions or tight dates.

  • Confirm whether review happens before signing or after engagement.
  • Ask which searches are recommended for the property type.
  • Check who manages lender contact and the other side.
  • Ask whether PEXA settlement is included in the ordinary scope.

Different Files Need Different Checks

A buyer file needs attention to the contract, searches, conditions, duty and settlement readiness. A seller file starts earlier, because the published page refers to contract preparation and mandatory Form 2 seller disclosure. The same firm may handle both, but the risk points are not identical.

First home buyers should treat grants and duty concessions as eligibility questions, not assumed savings. The source page says solicitors guide first home buyers through transfer duty concessions, the First Home Owner Grant and settlement steps. A separate guide to first home buyer contract review in Brisbane is most relevant where the main concern is understanding the contract before commitment.

Commercial property needs a different conversation again. The published page identifies leases, GST, due diligence and commercial settlement as part of that work, so a routine residential quote may not be the right comparison.

Timing And Cooling-Off Questions

Timing changes the value of legal review. The published page says most Queensland residential contracts have a 5 business day cooling-off period starting when the buyer receives the contract signed by both parties. It also says the cooling-off period does not apply to auction purchases.

That makes pre-signing advice important for auction bidders, off-the-plan buyers and anyone relying on finance, building and pest or other conditions. The page also refers to off-the-plan contracts, sunset clauses and disclosure rules, so the question is what must be checked before the contract becomes difficult to change.

  1. List the contract date, finance date and settlement date.
  2. Confirm whether cooling-off applies to the transaction.
  3. Ask what changes if a condition is missed or needs extension.
  4. Before settlement, confirm duty, searches, lender readiness and PEXA booking.

Reading A Fixed-Fee Quote

The published page says routine professional fees are typically a few hundred to around a thousand dollars. It also says government charges and disbursements such as title searches and registration are additional and vary by property.

Compare quotes by scope, not just headline amount. Look for contract review, searches, transfer duty handling, settlement booking, lender liaison and extra-work triggers. If the matter involves an auction, off-the-plan purchase, family title transfer, commercial premises or a contract amendment, ask whether the quoted work is still treated as routine.

A clear quote should state the professional fee, the likely separate cost categories and when the solicitor will contact the agent, lender and other party. Written separation is the practical protection against surprise cost categories.
