When planning a bathroom renovation in Melbourne, the initial conversation should encompass the specific suburb, the type of bathroom, the general scope of work, and any unusual aspects related to access or necessary approvals. This approach is valuable because two bathrooms that appear similar in photographs can require vastly different work behind the surfaces. A family bathroom needing an update, a bathroom with leaks, a compact ensuite, or an apartment wet area each present a distinct scenario for quoting purposes. Waterproofing, plumbing, electrical scope, layout efficiency, and the quality of finishes are all critical factors to consider within the same planning framework. This is important when determining whether the project is a complete overhaul, a smaller redesign, a focused waterproofing and tiling job, an accessibility upgrade, or work connected to an adjacent laundry or powder room. Clearly describe current frustrations, such as leaks, inadequate storage, poor lighting, tight clearances, or an impractical layout. State whether walls, plumbing connections, or door swings are intended to be altered. Before requesting a price, flag any constraints related to apartments, owners' corporations, parking, lift access, narrow entryways, or other site-specific challenges. Budget Signals Before You Ask For Quotes

A broad range for a full bathroom renovation in Melbourne is approximately $8,000 to $35,000. Premium tiles, stone benchtops, and high-end tapware can increase the cost of custom work beyond $35,000. Recent data from the Housing Industry Association indicates the average Australian bathroom renovation spend is around $26,000. These figures serve as a starting point, not a definitive quote. A more pertinent question before seeking quotes is what factors will push your project towards the lower, middle, or upper end of this range. Changes to the layout, waterproofing requirements, the need for licensed plumbing and electrical work, site access, demolition conditions, fixture selections, and finish choices can all impact the final cost. Before comparing contractors, compile a concise scope note. This should include the bathroom type, whether the layout will remain the same, your expected finish level, any known leaks or waterproofing issues, and any access or apartment-related constraints. Providing this information ensures that each contractor is pricing the same set of work assumptions, making quotes easier to interpret.

Compliance, Waterproofing, and Written Scope

Ensuring wet-area compliance is paramount and should be addressed before finalising visible fit-off choices. This does not diminish the importance of design; rather, it means that decisions about tiles, tapware, and cabinetry should be made within a room plan that has already accounted for waterproofing, plumbing, and electrical requirements. A written scope is the essential document to request before work commences. The contractor undertaking the renovation should provide a written outline of the scope, exclusions, and any assumptions the quote is based upon before any commitment is made. This document should clearly state what is included, what is excluded, what remains provisional, and the assumptions underpinning the price. Inquire about the sequence of waterproofing work and how it is factored into the quote. Confirm whether plumbing and electrical modifications are included or treated as separate allowances. Verify that demolition, tiling, fit-off, and finishing are all covered within the same written scope. Ask what procedures are in place if site access or existing conditions change the scope of work after the project has begun.

Layout, Storage, and Finish Choices

For small bathrooms and ensuites, decisions regarding clearance, storage, and ventilation must be made before selecting finishes. Compact bathroom remodels for terraces, apartments, and narrow floor plans require careful consideration of every centimetre of clearance. Ensuite upgrades for cramped, dark, or dated rooms should prioritise storage, ventilation, and improved layout. A useful design brief identifies the daily inconveniences in the current room. If the room is poorly lit, ask how lighting, mirror placement, and ventilation will be addressed. If storage is insufficient, consider whether vanity, recessed shelving, or cabinetry changes will impact available space. If the room feels cramped, evaluate whether proposed fixtures enhance movement or merely update the aesthetic. The quality of finishes should align with the overall budget and functional requirements.
