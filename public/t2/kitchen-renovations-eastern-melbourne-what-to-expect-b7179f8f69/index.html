Refresh or rebuild changes the whole rhythm

A makeover suits a footprint that still works but feels dated: doors, benchtops and splashbacks can lift the room without changing services. A full renovation is a larger commitment described as demolition and rough-in, then cabinetry, benchtops, plumbing, electrical work, splashback, appliances and finishing. The rhythm differs because a rebuild has dependencies: design locks joinery, joinery waits on fabrication, then trades sequence around installation.

Ask direct questions. Is the layout staying the same or being redesigned? Which trades are inside the quoted scope? Are drawings issued before cabinetry is made? How are variations priced and approved in writing? These questions protect the homeowner when the layout shifts or a selection changes after work begins.

Period detail matters in the east. The provider states experience with Edwardian, Californian bungalow and interwar homes, where the design task is balancing character with a modern workspace. Expect the better conversations to address proportions, trims and adjoining rooms, not only the island and tapware.

Materials now come with a compliance boundary

Selection should begin with use. A household that cooks often may prioritise cleaning, edge profiles, sink position and storage access over colour. The published list includes custom cabinetry, stone benchtops, porcelain, sintered stone, natural stone, solid timber, stainless steel and laminate options.

The stated regulatory limit is important: engineered stone with 1 percent or more crystalline silica was banned for manufacture, supply, processing and installation across Australia from 1 July 2024 under the model WHS Regulations administered by Safe Work Australia. The provider says low-silica alternatives are used instead, including porcelain and sintered stone, natural granite or marble, solid timber, stainless steel, laminate, or low-silica engineered stone under 1 percent crystalline silica.

  • Ask for the product name, not a category such as "stone look".

Contracts, permits and the handover file

The provider states projects run under a written contract with the right permits and registered plumbing and electrical trades, in line with Victorian building requirements. It also states that a major domestic building contract over $10,000 requires a written contract under the Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995, setting out scope, price, dates, payment stages and the variations process.

The contract stage is where verbal promises become checkable. Keep the fixed scope, agreed selections, payment stages, variation process, warranties and compliance records together. If a detail affects cost, timing, layout or finish, ask for it in writing before work starts.

At handover, the stated expectation is that the team finishes, cleans up and gives warranty and compliance paperwork in writing. Store those documents with the contract, quote and drawings so later repairs, appliance changes or sale questions can be answered from records rather than memory.
