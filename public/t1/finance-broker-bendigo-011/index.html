How to compare structures without guessing

The page names chattel mortgage, finance lease and hire purchase as available equipment finance options, and separately mentions balloon payments for asset finance. The decision should not be made from repayment size alone. Ownership, GST treatment, depreciation, end-of-term options and accountant advice all change the real cost and suitability.

The source says that with a chattel mortgage, the borrower owns the asset from day one, can claim GST upfront and may benefit from depreciation deductions. It says that with a finance lease, the lender owns the asset while the borrower leases it, GST is charged on repayments, and the borrower may return or purchase the asset at the end of the term.

Because the supplied page itself recommends accountant input, the practical sequence is to shortlist the finance structures first, then check tax treatment with an accountant before signing. That avoids choosing a structure only because it appears cheaper in the first monthly repayment.
