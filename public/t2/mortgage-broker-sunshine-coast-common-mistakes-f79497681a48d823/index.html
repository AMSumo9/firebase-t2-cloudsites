Mortgage Broker Sunshine Coast Common Mistakes Explained

The pattern behind most mortgage broker sunshine coast common usually starts before the first phone call. Many people open the conversation with one number in mind, the lowest rate they saw advertised. The source material frames the review as a fit exercise rather than a rate hunt: income, deposit and goals are weighed against a panel that can include big four banks, second tier lenders, mutuals and non banks, so a headline rate alone says little about whether a lender will suit the borrower.

Other early errors include:

  • arriving with no clear borrower type, so the session drifts instead of testing a purchase, refinance, investment or construction path
  • reading the $450,000 to $900,000 indicative range as an offer rather than a figure that needs live serviceability testing against lender policy
  • keeping quiet about dependants, ongoing commitments or a planned income change, each of which feeds the serviceability result
  • assuming scheme access is settled before the property, the price and the lender treatment have been checked

Scheme mistakes first home buyers keep repeating

First home buyers face a cluster of deposit related traps. One is assuming Queensland First Home Owner Grant funds will automatically count towards the deposit for the intended purchase, when treatment should be confirmed for that specific contract. Another is overlooking lenders mortgage insurance: eligible buyers may use the Australian Government 5% Deposit Scheme with no lenders mortgage insurance, because the government guarantees part of the loan, yet buyers often do not ask whether LMI is payable, reduced or removed under the structure proposed.

Timing is a further trap. Income caps and place caps were removed from 1 October 2025, but property price caps still apply and should be confirmed before contracting. Savings evidence is the last gap: buyers should ask what evidence of genuine savings the lender needs before application rather than assuming recent lump sums will be accepted.

Refinancing errors that quietly cost flexibility

Refinancers often repeat the rate fixation problem in a new form. Comparing only the advertised rate on the existing loan against a competitor ignores fees, loan splits and future flexibility, all of which belong in the same comparison. The source material says the broker flags whether a switch is worthwhile before anything is signed, so the fix is to ask for the keep or switch reasoning in writing or in a clear summary before signing new loan documents.

A second error is refinancing without a purpose. A lower repayment today can come with a structure that limits later options, so the review should connect the switch to a wider goal rather than to the rate alone.

Investor assumptions that skip suitability

Investors often arrive treating interest only terms, offset accounts, SMSF lending and multiple property structures as automatic options. The source material treats each as a suitability question, not a recommendation. The better approach is to ask which lenders support the structure, what documents each requires, and whether the proposed loan still fits the wider property plan. ASIC Moneysmart home loan guidance encourages comparison of features, fees and repayments rather than the rate alone, and its property investment material points borrowers towards costs and risk, so repayment capacity belongs in the review alongside lender selection.

How to leave the review with outputs you can check

The antidote to the mistakes above is a review that ends in checkable outputs rather than vague reassurance. The source material sets out a four step path: submit a short form, take a broker callback, complete a review that confirms borrowing power, lender shortlist and realistic timeline, then proceed through application and settlement support, with annual rate reviews available after settlement.

Done well, that session leaves a borrower with a lender shortlist to test, a missing document list, and a reason to act now or wait. Common questions

Is a standard home loan review free? The source material says the review is free and there is no direct fee for a standard home loan review. Any commission, fee or clawback arrangement must be disclosed in writing before the borrower commits.

What if my planned price sits above the indicative range? Treat the $450,000 to $900,000 range as an indication only. A precise figure needs live serviceability tested across lender policy, which is what the review stage is for.
