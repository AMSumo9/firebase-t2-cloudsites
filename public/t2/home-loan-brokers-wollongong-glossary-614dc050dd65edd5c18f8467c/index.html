Deposit and security terms

Three ideas govern how much a lender will offer and on what conditions.

  • Loan to value ratio (LVR): the loan amount divided by the lender's valuation of the property. A lower LVR generally means a stronger deposit position and a wider lender choice.
  • Lenders mortgage insurance (LMI): an insurance cost that can apply where the deposit falls below the threshold a lender sets. It protects the lender rather than the borrower, so it is worth asking how it changes the total cost of the loan.
  • Valuation: the lender's view of what the property is worth, which can differ from the purchase price and can matter for off the plan purchases.

Deposit position includes savings plus any concession or guarantee pathway, such as the First Home Guarantee for eligible first home buyers, and it helps to state that position clearly at the first conversation.

Repayment structure and loan purpose terms

Structure words describe how repayments and the loan purpose fit together.

  • Serviceability: the lender's assessment of whether income can support the repayments once existing debts are counted.
  • Principal and interest: repayments reduce the loan balance as well as covering interest.
  • Interest only: repayments cover interest for a set period, which can suit investor cash flow but leaves the balance unchanged.
  • Refinancing: replacing or restructuring an existing loan, often to review the rate, consolidate debt or release equity.
  • Pre-approval: a conditional early assessment that indicates a borrowing range rather than a final approval.
  • Bridging finance: short term funding that can help a borrower buy before they sell, with the assessment shaped by settlement timing on both properties.
  • Alt doc and full doc: evidence pathways for self employed borrowers, where the income period and business structure decide what a lender will accept.
  • Negative gearing: where investment property costs exceed rental income, a concept investors may weigh against cash flow goals.
  • Settlement: the stage where loan funds are provided and the property officially changes hands, with broker support continuing through to completion.

Using the glossary in a broker meeting

The vocabulary earns its keep when it sharpens questions. Australian brokers must act in the borrower's best interests, and credentials are disclosed in writing before a borrower commits, which supports asking directly: which lenders are being compared, including bank and non bank options; why a lender suits the income shape, deposit and plans; how the broker is paid and whether any direct fee applies to the file; and which documents to prepare before pre approval or a refinance assessment. For construction or bridging files, timing points can affect approval or settlement, so ask what could shift between conditional approval, final approval and settlement day. Pre-approval is a stage rather than a finish line. ASIC Moneysmart offers neutral explanations of loan features and repayments for borrowers who want a second reference point.

  1. Name the loan purpose. State whether the credit is for buying, refinancing, investing, building, bridging or self employed borrowing, because the purpose decides which terms matter most.
  2. Match terms to your file. Pick the three or four definitions that connect to your deposit position, income evidence and timing pressure, and note the question each one raises.
  3. Prepare evidence early. Line up payslips, liability details or self employed records before pre approval so the conversation can move from broad options to a reasoned shortlist.
  4. Keep asking through to settlement. Treat pre-approval as one stage, and keep asking what could change between conditional approval, final approval and settlement day.

Common questions

What is the difference between pre-approval and final approval? Pre-approval is a conditional early indication of a borrowing range, while final approval follows full verification and valuation of the property. Conditions can still change between the two, including where valuations move, income evidence ages or debts change, so it helps to ask what could shift before settlement day.

Does a low deposit always mean lenders mortgage insurance? LMI can apply where the deposit falls below the threshold a lender sets, but it is not automatic in every low deposit case. Eligible first home buyers may have a guarantee pathway available, and the useful question is how any insurance or guarantee cost affects the loan amount and the total cost across the life of the loan.
