What to gather before you book

Comparison quality depends on what you bring. Before asking for lender options, prepare the following.

  • Income and employment details, so serviceability can be tested realistically rather than guessed.
  • Savings and deposit evidence, because deposit size shapes lender fit and scheme questions.
  • Current loan statements if refinancing, so the existing loan can be weighed against alternatives with costs included.
  • Property and contract information, which matters for construction borrowers and growth corridor purchases.
  • Scheme questions, such as grant or postcode-sensitive settings that need official confirmation.

Documents are the quiet bottleneck. Income, savings, current loan, property and contract information should be ready before lender comparisons are requested, because a shortlist built on incomplete detail tends to shift once a lender verifies the file. Being organised shortens the loop and stops a comforting estimate replacing a tested borrowing range.

How commission shapes the advice

Most mortgage brokers receive lender commission and do not charge a direct fee to standard home loan customers. Any fees or clawback arrangements should be explained upfront before proceeding. That model is worth understanding, not fearing, because it shapes how comparisons are organised.

Commission does not remove the need to ask what is excluded from a comparison. A lower repayment can still be the wrong fit if fees, features or future plans are ignored. ASIC Moneysmart frames home loan choice around costs, features and repayment fit, which is a useful standard to apply to any broker conversation.

Practical questions include what is being compared, what is excluded, and what would make a different lender more suitable for your scenario. It also helps to remember that the borrower still owns the decision. A broker can organise comparison, but the trade-offs remain yours to weigh, which is why those questions matter more than any single recommendation. Scheme checks to confirm before you rely on a number

Scheme settings can move, so confirm them against official sources before relying on any figure. For Queensland first home buyers, check the current First Home Owner Grant amount and contract-date settings from Queensland Revenue Office guidance. Federal low-deposit scheme price caps can turn on postcode classification, so confirm the current official postcode result for the property you are considering.

The safer habit is to ask for the source used, the date it was checked, and how the setting affects the lender shortlist. A figure quoted without a date is a starting point, not a fact to build a contract around. First home buyers in particular should treat grant and concession questions as official checks rather than estimates passed along in conversation.
