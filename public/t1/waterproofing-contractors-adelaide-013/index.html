Who This Applies To

This guide is for Adelaide homeowners, unit owners, renovators, strata decision makers and commercial or managed-property contacts who need to brief a waterproofing contractor clearly. It is especially relevant when the job involves a wet area, a tiled balcony, a roof deck, a below-ground space, a retaining wall or an unexplained moisture path.

  • Home renovations: bathrooms, laundries and WC areas should be discussed in relation to AS 3740 wet-area work.
  • Apartment and strata settings: balconies, basement car parks and shared roof or podium areas may need a clearer scope because access and responsibility can be less straightforward.
  • Older properties: character homes, older tile beds and renovated wet areas may need membrane reinstatement rather than surface resealing alone.
  • Foothills or sloping sites: retaining walls, basements and below-ground areas should be discussed in terms of water path, site levels and hydrostatic pressure control where relevant.

Matching the Job to the Surface

The published service list is broad, so the practical task is to narrow the enquiry. Wet areas such as bathrooms, showers and laundries should be framed around internal use, tiling, drainage and the required membrane work. External surfaces such as balconies, terraces and roof decks should be framed around exposure, joints, traffic and whether the existing membrane needs repair or replacement.

Basements and retaining walls sit in a different category because the work can involve below-ground tanking, positive-side or negative-side membrane systems, sump systems and hydrostatic pressure control. Those terms should not be treated as interchangeable. A useful quote explains which side of the structure is being treated, what access is assumed and how the contractor has identified the moisture path.

Reading a Written Quote

A written quote should do more than name a lump sum. The source page says enquiries move from project details to site inspection, then a written quote with clear scope, price and warranty terms. For waterproofing, the scope should identify the area, the substrate or surface type where known, the suspected or confirmed leak source, the membrane work proposed and any exclusions that would change the final job.

Warranty wording also needs to be attached to the actual work. The source page states that full membrane installations are backed by a written workmanship warranty from the contractor. Ask whether the quoted task is a full membrane installation, a repair, resealing, investigation or replacement, because those categories may carry different documents and expectations.

  1. Describe the area. Give the contractor the location, surface type and visible moisture signs before the inspection.
  2. Ask for diagnosis first. Confirm whether leak detection or invasive investigation is needed before repair language is used.
  3. Check licence and standard. Ask how the work relates to the stated South Australian licence requirement and AS 3740 where wet areas are involved.
  4. Read the quote. Look for a written scope, price, warranty terms and any exclusions before work begins.

Common questions

What should I ask before accepting a waterproofing quote? Ask what area was inspected, what leak source was identified, what membrane work is proposed, whether AS 3740 applies and what warranty terms are written into the scope. The source page states that the process includes site inspection and a clear written quote.

Is leak detection the same as waterproofing repair? No. The source page lists leak detection and repair separately from membrane services. Detection is about tracing moisture and identifying the leak source, while repair or replacement is the work proposed after that assessment.

When is AS 3740 relevant? The source page connects AS 3740 compliance with domestic wet-area waterproofing. Bathrooms, laundries, WC areas and shower work are the obvious places to ask how the standard applies to the quoted scope.

Do all waterproofing jobs need a full membrane installation? Not necessarily. The source page mentions membrane selection, installation, repair and replacement, as well as shower diagnosis and resealing. The quote should make clear whether the job is investigation, repair, reinstatement, replacement or a full installation.
