Kitchen Renovations Melbourne Myths Explained

The highest quote does not signal the best build. Cost movement in a Melbourne kitchen project usually tracks scope rather than any single visible product. A refresh that keeps the existing layout and changes door fronts, benchtop and splashback carries indicative figures nearer $12,000 to $30,000. A full project covering new cabinetry, plumbing and electrical updates, splashback and soft-close hardware under a fixed-price written contract commonly sits between $25,000 and $60,000, and natural stone, custom joinery or a butler's pantry can push it past $80,000. For readers sorting competing figures, the kitchen renovations melbourne planning guide overview keeps budget, layout and timing in one conversation.

  • Ask whether soft-close runners and hinges are inside the base price.
  • Ask which hardware brand the quote allows for.
  • Ask how variations are documented if the brief changes after signing.
  • Ask whether cabinetry, benchtops, splashback, trades and appliances are separated clearly enough to compare.

Myth two: a compact kitchen is always a cheaper kitchen

Size is a weak guide to price. A compact apartment kitchen may use less material, yet access limits, protected common areas, noise rules and owners corporation documentation can shape labour as much as joinery. Apartment examples around Richmond, Brunswick, Footscray and the inner city often bring lift bookings, parking and approval questions into the quote conversation.

Older detached homes carry their own surprises at measure-up. Uneven floors, existing services and cabinet runs that must be scribed rather than assumed square all add detail work. The practical variable is property type, not suburb, so access protection and documentation should be priced up front rather than discovered mid-build.

  • Compact kitchens use less material but can carry awkward access and building rules.
  • Period terraces, Edwardians and bungalows may need made-to-measure cabinetry that respects existing character.

Myth three: the clock starts on demolition day

A short on-site window does not equal a short project. Before any cabinet is lifted into place, the design must be locked, measurements confirmed, cabinetry and stone fabricated, and appliances selected. Fabrication alone typically takes around 4 to 6 weeks as a lead-in, and those weeks pass before demolition begins.

For a mid-range full renovation, the indicative run from contract signing to handover is roughly 6 to 12 weeks. Once cabinetry is ready, on-site work usually runs 2 to 4 weeks, with finishing adding another 1 to 2 weeks. Households planning to stay in the property should ask when the old kitchen becomes unusable and which handover items still sit after installation.

  • Confirm the design and measurements before manufacture.
  • Lock appliance selections before cabinetry is finalised.
  • List finishing items that follow installation so handover is not a surprise.

Myth four: the plan can flex once work begins

Changing the brief after contract signing is possible, but it is not free or instant. A variation is the written record of a change, and it can alter cost and lead time because tradespeople and installers are sequenced around the original scope. Allowances held for items not yet selected can also move the final figure if choices land above the allowed amount.

The antidote is a written fixed-price contract with readable lines for cabinets, benchtops, splashback, trades and appliances, plus a clear variation process. Industry bodies such as the Housing Industry Association and Master Builders Australia publish guidance on building contracts and consumer obligations, and ASIC Moneysmart's renovating guidance supports comparing written quotes and holding a buffer for surprises.

Who these myths catch most often

These misconceptions matter most for Melbourne owners who know a kitchen project is coming but have not settled how far to take it: people weighing a refresh against a full rebuild, apartment owners preparing for documentation questions, and households considering made-to-measure cabinetry for a period terrace, Edwardian or bungalow.

The counterweight to every myth is the same sequence: a clear brief, a renovator callback, a free in-home design consult, measurements, a layout discussion and then a written quote. Skipped steps produce estimates that cannot reflect the room, the building rules or the real lead time. This framing is less useful for a single appliance swap, a benchtop-only purchase, or anyone expecting a firm price without an in-home measure.

  1. Write the brief in plain terms. List what must change, what can stay, who cooks, storage pain points and any apartment or period home constraints.
  2. Book the in-home consult. Use the callback and free design consult to test layout options before manufacture locks the room.
  3. Confirm selections early. Lock measurements and appliances before cabinetry production so dimensions and services match.
  4. Compare like-for-like quotes. Separate cabinetry, benchtops, splashback, trades and appliances, and note hardware brands and exclusions.

Common questions

What pushes a Melbourne kitchen renovation past $60,000? Indicative fitted work sits between $25,000 and $60,000. Layout changes that shift plumbing and electrical points, natural stone, custom joinery and a butler's pantry sit at the higher end and can push a premium project beyond $80,000.

How long does a full renovation really take? Allow roughly 6 to 12 weeks from contract signing to handover for a mid-range full project. Cabinetry and stone fabrication take around 4 to 6 weeks before installation, on-site work runs 2 to 4 weeks once cabinetry is ready, and finishing adds about 1 to 2 weeks.

Is an apartment kitchen cheaper because it is smaller? Not necessarily. A compact kitchen may use less material, but access limits, noise rules, protected common areas and owners corporation documentation can all shape labour and the final quote.
