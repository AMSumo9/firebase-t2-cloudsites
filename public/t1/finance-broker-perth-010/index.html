Who This Applies To

This guide is most relevant to borrowers who know the finance problem but have not narrowed the lender field. The published information names first home buyers, upgraders, investors, refinancers, construction borrowers and business borrowers, so the strongest preparation is borrower specific rather than generic.

  • First home buyers: ask about FHBG references, deposit strategy, LMI, and whether any grant or scheme question needs checking against official eligibility rules.
  • Refinancers: bring the current loan balance, rate, repayment, property estimate and any known discharge or switching costs.
  • Investors: ask how rental income, existing debt, interest-only options and portfolio lending are assessed.
  • Construction borrowers: ask how progress payment lending is structured for a new build or knockdown rebuild.
  • Business borrowers: clarify whether the request is for equipment finance, commercial property, cash flow or another business purpose.

Questions To Ask Before Choosing A Loan

A broker conversation should test suitability, costs and loan features. ASIC Moneysmart says borrowers can compare home loans and look at interest rates, fees, repayments and features, so the same discipline should sit behind any recommendation.

  • Which lenders were considered, and why were some unsuitable?
  • Is the comparison looking at fixed rate, variable rate, or both?
  • Would an offset account change the recommendation?
  • Are there fees, switching costs, valuation requirements or loan feature trade-offs?
  • For investors, how is rental income treated in serviceability?
  • For construction borrowers, how are progress payments handled?

For first home buyers, ask whether any grant, guarantee or concession question should be checked against official rules before relying on it in a purchase budget.

How To Read Broker Claims Carefully

The published information says independent brokers hold appropriate credit licences and qualifications, and refers to MFAA and FBAA brokers committed to industry association codes of conduct and ethics. Treat those as starting checks. Ask the individual broker for licence or representative details, lender panel, commission explanation and any conflicts that may affect the recommendation.

The guide also names major banks including CBA, Westpac, NAB, ANZ and Bankwest, plus specialist lenders. A broad panel can widen the comparison, but the shortlist still needs a plain explanation of repayments, loan features and approval conditions.

Keep records of the recommendation, the loan features discussed and the assumptions used. For a refinance, note the current loan and the proposed replacement. For a purchase or build, separate pre-approval limits from the contract, valuation and settlement steps.

  1. Define the purpose. State whether the enquiry is for a purchase, refinance, investment, build, vehicle, equipment or commercial need.
  2. Gather documents. Prepare income, existing debts, property details and any current loan information before the broker review.
  3. Compare structure. Ask whether fixed, variable, offset, interest-only or principal and interest options are being considered.
  4. Check obligations. Request a clear explanation of fees, commissions, lender conditions and why the shortlist suits the borrower profile.
  5. Confirm next step. Before applying, confirm what is still conditional, including valuation, lender approval and any official scheme eligibility.
