Who This Applies To

The published service is aimed at several borrower groups on the Central Coast. Owner-occupiers are comparing home loan structure and rate. Refinancers are checking whether switching costs, equity release or debt consolidation make sense. First home buyers are mapping possible pathways, including the NSW First Home Buyer Assistance Scheme and the federal First Home Guarantee, without assuming eligibility before advice is given.

Investors have a different set of questions. The source page refers to investment loans, rentvestor decisions, and interest-only versus principal-and-interest structures. A borrower should ask how the proposed structure affects cash flow, repayment discipline and future borrowing capacity. ASIC Moneysmart's property investment guidance is a useful external reference point because investment property decisions involve both loan cost and investment risk.

Self-employed borrowers are also named in the source material. The practical issue is evidence. The page says specialist lenders may accept BAS, accountant declarations and business bank statements as alternative income documents, so the useful question is which document trail is strongest before an application is lodged.

Low Deposit, LMI And First Buyer Questions

The source page specifically mentions low deposit home loans for buyers under a 20 per cent deposit, Lenders Mortgage Insurance, the federal First Home Guarantee and construction loans for new builds. A borrower should not treat those labels as automatic approval. They are prompts for a careful discussion about deposit size, eligibility, loan purpose, property type and whether a guarantee or insurance cost is being considered.

For a first home buyer, the useful checklist is direct: ask whether the NSW First Home Buyer Assistance Scheme is relevant to the property being considered, whether the First Home Guarantee could apply, and what evidence the lender will need. Do not rely on a general summary when a scheme, concession or guarantee may turn on personal circumstances.

For construction finance, ask how progress payments, valuation stages and the loan structure will be explained before signing. The source page names construction loans, but it does not give detailed construction approval rules, so a borrower should ask for those details in the broker discussion.

How To Judge The Recommendation

A broker recommendation should be understandable in plain English. The source page says borrowers compare rates and features side by side, then choose, with no pressure and no obligation. That side-by-side comparison should show the lender, product type, repayment type, key features, estimated costs and the reason each option is suitable.

ASIC states that mortgage brokers must act in the consumer's best interests when providing credit assistance. The source page also says its broker partners operate under Australian Credit Licence requirements and belong to the MFAA or FBAA. For a borrower, the practical step is to ask how the recommended loan meets that duty, how the broker is paid, and what alternatives were considered.

  • Ask for the comparison in writing before applying.
  • Ask what changes if interest rates move before settlement.
  • Ask whether the loan still suits if income, rent, renovation plans or investment goals change.
  1. Define the borrower scenario. State whether the loan is for a first home, refinance, investment, construction, low deposit purchase or self-employed application.
  2. List the decision inputs. Prepare income type, deposit position, property purpose, timing and the loan features you already know you need.
  3. Request the comparison. Ask for lender options side by side, including rate, features, fees, structure and the reason each option is suitable.
  4. Check obligations and payment. Ask how best interests duty applies, how commission is disclosed, and whether any borrower fee applies before work begins.

What should a refinancer ask before switching? Ask whether the savings outweigh switching costs, whether equity release or debt consolidation changes the total loan, and whether the new structure still fits your repayment plans.
