Safety Switches, Breakers And Board Questions

The source guide gives a plain distinction that is worth keeping in view. A safety switch monitors for leakage of electrical current and cuts power when that fault is detected. A circuit breaker primarily protects wiring and appliances. Some devices combine both functions, so unclear board labels are a question for a licensed electrician.

For a switchboard assessment, avoid diagnosing the board yourself. Ask whether the issue is coverage, capacity, circuit protection or labelling. If a device will not reset, say which area lost power and what was in use at the time. If the enquiry is about an upgrade, explain the planned load rather than asking for a generic bigger board.

For smoke alarms, the source guide names compliant alarms as photoelectric, interconnected and meeting AS 3786-2014. It also says the required power supply and replacement choice depend on the dwelling and existing alarm arrangement.

What To Put In An Enquiry

A good enquiry does not need technical language. It needs enough plain facts for a provider to decide whether the work is in scope and what follow-up is needed. The source form asks for suburb or postcode, job type, access and nearby structures, with photos only later if a provider asks for them.

Keep the request narrow. For a power point, say whether it is new, damaged or being relocated. For lighting, say whether it is a fixed fitting or a fault. For ceiling fans, note the intended location and whether existing wiring is present, without assuming that wiring is suitable.

The source page is also clear about identity and expectations. It is an information and enquiry website, not an electrical contractor, booking service or emergency response channel. Submitting details does not confirm availability, timing or acceptance of the work.
