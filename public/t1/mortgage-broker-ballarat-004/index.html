Investors need a different lens again. The source mentions comparison rate, interest-only periods and tax-aware setup. For Ballarat and regional Victorian purchases, that means your questions should focus on how the loan behaves over time, not only on the opening repayment.

Broker or bank, where the comparison changes

A bank offers its own products. The supplied page says a broker compares multiple lenders against your goals and situation, which is the central difference if your file is not straightforward or if features matter as much as rate. That can be relevant for regional Victorian borrowers whose income, deposit, property type or construction timeline does not fit a single-box application.

The same source also says mortgage brokers must act in the best interests of consumers under the statutory duty guided by ASIC RG 273, and that this has applied since 1 January 2021. That does not remove the need to ask questions, but it gives you a clear standard for how recommendations should be framed.

Cost is another common point of confusion. The page says brokers are typically paid commission by the lender rather than by the borrower, while some brokers may charge a fee in specific situations and must disclose that upfront in the credit quote. If fees are mentioned, ask what triggered them and whether the same outcome is possible without them.

A short checklist for the first broker conversation

Keep the first call practical. The source page promises a simple process, tell the broker your goals, discuss lenders and pre-approval, then move through application, settlement and ongoing review. The useful preparation is to bring the missing context that changes lender fit.

  1. State your scenario clearly, first purchase, refinance, investment, or construction.
  2. Ask which loan features are shaping the shortlist, especially offset, redraw, cashback, comparison rate or interest-only structure.
  3. Check whether pre-approval is appropriate now, and remember the source says it is typically valid for around 3 months and remains conditional on final checks.
  4. Ask whether any scheme or low deposit pathway is worth testing, rather than assuming it applies.
  5. Confirm how the broker is paid and whether any borrower fee could arise in your situation.

That approach keeps the discussion grounded in credit rules, timing, and loan structure, not broad marketing language.

  1. Define the scenario. Say whether the loan is for a first home, refinance, investment purchase or construction before discussing products.
  2. Check loan features. Ask which lenders suit your deposit, timing and feature needs such as offset, redraw or interest-only options.
  3. Test pre-approval timing. Confirm whether pre-approval should be arranged now and what conditions still need final lender checks.
  4. Review total switch costs. For a refinance, compare the new loan against discharge, valuation and any other upfront costs before changing.
  5. Confirm payment and disclosure. Ask how the broker is paid and whether any borrower fee could apply in your case.
