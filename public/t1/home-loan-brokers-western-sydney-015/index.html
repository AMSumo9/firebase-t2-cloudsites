Deposit, LMI And Serviceability Questions

The home page gives several useful guardrails. It says most lenders require at least 5% of the purchase price as genuine savings, and that borrowing more than 80% of the property value usually brings Lenders Mortgage Insurance into the discussion. It also says the site's borrowing calculator uses the APRA 3% prudential buffer and that lenders may apply the higher of declared expenses or the Household Expenditure Measure.

These figures do not decide the application by themselves. They create the right questions. If the deposit is small, ask whether the lender will treat it as genuine savings and whether LMI applies. If a family guarantee is being considered, ask how the guarantor release pathway is explained. If borrowing capacity is tight, ask what expenses, debts, credit limits and proposed repayments are being tested. ASIC Moneysmart also frames home loan decisions around understanding costs, features and repayment commitments, so a borrower should leave the broker conversation able to explain the loan in their own words.

What To Ask Before Proceeding

Before lodging an application, ask the broker to set out how they are paid. The source page says most standard home loans do not involve a direct broker fee because brokers are typically paid by the lender after settlement, while complex scenarios may involve a fee that should be disclosed in writing up front. That disclosure is not a minor admin item. It helps the borrower understand the advice relationship before paperwork starts.

  1. Which lenders were considered, and why were others ruled out?
  2. What deposit, LMI and serviceability assumptions are being used?
  3. Is the loan fixed, variable, split, offset-linked or interest-only, and why?
  4. What documents are needed before a realistic pre-approval review?
  5. Is any direct fee payable, and has it been disclosed in writing?
  1. Define the loan goal. State whether the file is a purchase, refinance, investment, construction, bridging or self-employed scenario before discussing lenders.
  2. Prepare the evidence. Collect income, debt, expense, savings and property details so the broker can test the file realistically.
  3. Ask for the comparison logic. Request a plain-English explanation of the lenders considered, the product features and the trade-offs.
  4. Confirm remuneration. Ask whether any direct fee applies and whether the broker's payment has been disclosed in writing.
  5. Review before signing. Check the loan amount, repayment type, offset features, LMI position and settlement steps before committing.
