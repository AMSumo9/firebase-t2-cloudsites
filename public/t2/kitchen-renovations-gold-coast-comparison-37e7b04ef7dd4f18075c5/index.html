Kitchen Renovations Gold Coast Comparison Explained

The first step in comparing kitchen renovations gold coast options is to define the project type, as this shapes the cost and timeline before cabinetry colours or appliance brands are even discussed. The published guide distinguishes between a budget refresh, a full rebuild, and specialised projects such as canal-front luxury kitchens or strata-approved apartment renovations. A refresh typically involves updating finishes, splashbacks and hardware without moving the layout, whereas a full rebuild includes new cabinetry, stone benchtops, plumbing and electrical updates. Canal-front and prestige homes across Hope Island, Mermaid Beach and Broadbeach Waters require bespoke joinery, natural stone and integrated appliances, which pushes the budget into the premium band. Conversely, apartment renovations in high-rises like Surfers Paradise or Main Beach involve body-corp documentation, plumbing through party walls and acoustic underlay, which adds administrative complexity but does not always increase material costs. Being upfront about the property type helps the builder identify access limits or approvals that must appear in the written scope before demolition starts.

Cost drivers and price bands

The published price bands are most useful as scope filters rather than promises. They sit at about $12,000 to $30,000 for a budget refresh, about $25,000 to $60,000 for a mid-range full renovation, and about $45,000 to $94,000 or more for premium or waterfront work. If a proposal falls outside the band you expected, the right question is what has changed in the brief. The main cost drivers are cabinetry quantity and finish, benchtop material, whether the layout and plumbing move, appliance grade, and access on high-rise or canal-front sites.

Labor is roughly 30 to 40 percent of the total cost, so the remaining balance is split between materials, demolition, disposal and site preparation. For example, moving a sink or stove to a different wall requires new plumbing and electrical work, which adds to the quote. Similarly, choosing 2-pac cabinetry over laminate or natural stone over engineered quartz significantly increases the material spend. When comparing quotes, check whether appliances are included, excluded or left to owner supply, and whether access costs are priced now rather than left for later variations.

Timeline breakdown

Timing is easier to read when it is split into response, quoting and build stages. The guide says enquiries receive a response within one business day, the free in-home design consult runs for 60 minutes, and the written fixed-price quote is usually emailed within 5 to 10 working days after that visit. That sequence tells you what should happen quickly and what still depends on measuring the site properly. For the build window, the guide gives 6 to 12 weeks from contract signing to handover for a standard mid-range project.

This timeline breaks down into 4 to 6 weeks of cabinetry and stone fabrication lead-in, then 2 to 4 weeks of on-site work once cabinetry is on site. If the property sits under body corporate rules or restricted work windows, ask which part of that timeline absorbs the delay. Use the consult to settle measurements, layout moves and finish level. Read the quote against the fabrication lead-in, not only the handover date. Ask whether approvals or access bookings can postpone demolition or installation.

Contract and warranty checks

Before approving anything, read the paperwork against the site discussion. The guide says the independent renovator handles the design, fixed-price quote, QBCC contract and build end to end, and that build and handover sit under a fixed-price HIA or Master Builders contract with a transparent variations process. It also says QBCC home warranty is included. Those points matter only if the written scope reflects what was actually discussed in the room. Look for demolition assumptions, appliance supply, plumbing changes and access limits in the contract documents, not only in email summaries.

Warranty wording deserves the same check. The guide says most reputable local renovators offer 5 to 10 years on workmanship and custom cabinetry, and that the QBCC scheme provides statutory cover of 6 years 6 months for structural defects and 12 months for non-structural defects. At handover, ask for the contract set, variation record and warranty documentation to confirm the coverage period matches what was promised during the consult.

  2. Request the free in-home consult. Submit your details through the service provider. A QBCC-licensed renovator will contact you within one business day to arrange a 60-minute visit to your property.
  3. Review the fixed-price quote. After the visit, the builder will email a written quote within 5 to 10 working days. Check that the quote includes cabinetry, appliances, benchtops, demolition, and any access or approval costs.
  4. Sign the QBCC contract. Review the fixed-price HIA or Master Builders contract and the QBCC home warranty. Ensure the scope matches the discussion and that the variations process is transparent before signing.
