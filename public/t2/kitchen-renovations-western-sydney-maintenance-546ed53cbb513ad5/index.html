Routine Care By Component

Break the room into parts and give each a small recurring task. None of it is heavy work, and ten minutes a week plus a seasonal check stops small issues becoming trade callouts.

  • Cabinetry and hardware: wipe doors and drawer fronts, and tighten or adjust hinges, runners and handles before wear becomes visible.
  • Benchtops: follow the fabricator's cleaning instructions, since engineered stone, porcelain, sintered stone and natural stone each respond differently to heat, knives and cleaning products.
  • Splashbacks: keep grout lines and joins clean so moisture is not sitting behind the surface.
  • Sinks and taps: fix slow drainage or drips promptly, because water pooling under a sink can swell cabinet floors.
  • Appliances: keep ventilation gaps clear and book servicing on the manufacturer's schedule rather than waiting for a breakdown.

Keep a simple log of what was done and when. That record becomes useful if a warranty question ever arises.

The practical checks do not finish when the last tradesperson leaves. Ask what the warranty covers, how long it runs, and which care steps it expects the household to perform.

Scheduling Care Around The Build

Maintenance planning starts during the project, not after it. The published guide sets a four to eight week window between contract signing and handover, with the on-site stage commonly running two to three weeks after cabinetry arrives on site. Cabinet, stone and appliance lead times fill the front of that schedule, which is exactly when product decisions, measurements, drawings and contract details need to line up.

Use the waiting period to collect care instructions for every material being supplied, and ask who to call once handover passes. Knowing whether adjustments, resealing or servicing sit with the provider, the fabricator or the appliance manufacturer removes guesswork later.

Older Homes And Strata Considerations

In an established home with older building fabric, inspect surrounding surfaces as part of the upkeep routine, since movement or moisture in walls and floors can show up around new joinery. In a strata property, ask the strata manager early about which parts of the room are your responsibility and what written approvals applied to the original work, then keep that correspondence with your renovation file.

  1. List the components. Write down every element being supplied: cabinetry, benchtop, splashback, sink, tap and appliances, noting the supplier of each.
  2. Collect care instructions. Ask each supplier for written cleaning and care guidance, and file it with the contract before handover.
  3. Set a weekly and seasonal rhythm. Wipe surfaces weekly, then run a deeper seasonal check of hardware, joins, drainage and ventilation gaps.

Common questions

How often should a renovated kitchen be maintained? A light weekly wipe-down of joinery and benchtops, plus a short seasonal check of hinges, joins, drainage and appliance ventilation, is enough for most households. Follow the fabricator's and manufacturer's written guidance for anything specific.

Does routine care affect the warranty? It can. Ask the provider before work begins what care the warranty expects, how long coverage lasts and which items it includes. Keeping a maintenance log and the original paperwork makes any future claim far easier to support.

Who is responsible for upkeep in a strata kitchen? That depends on the scheme. Ask the strata manager which parts of the room are lot property versus common property, what written approvals applied to the renovation, and keep those answers filed with your contract documents.
