Who This Applies To

This guide fits borrowers whose needs match the source page: first home purchase, refinance, investment lending or conditional approval before making offers. It is less useful for borrowers wanting personal tax advice, conveyancing advice or a guaranteed approval outcome, because those claims are not made in the published material.

  • First home buyers: ask how your deposit, savings history and any eligible government support will be checked before contract stage.
  • Refinancers: ask whether the new loan improves your total position after fees, repayments and useful features are considered.
  • Investors: ask how existing equity, rental income assumptions and repayment type are treated by different lenders.
  • Pre-approval seekers: ask what remains conditional, and what can change before formal approval.

ASIC Moneysmart's home loan guidance is a sensible baseline for repayments, comparison rates and loan features. Bring those basics into the broker call so the discussion stays on your evidence, not only on advertised rates.

Questions To Ask Before You Choose

Start with payment and scope. The Bendigo guide says the introduction is free and that Australian residential brokers are typically paid commission by lenders, with exceptions disclosed upfront under ASIC rules. Ask how that payment is disclosed, which lenders are considered, and why the recommended option suits your circumstances.

Before an application starts, get plain answers to these checks:

  • Which lenders were compared for my scenario, and which were ruled out?
  • Is the option fixed, variable, split, interest only or principal and interest?
  • What documents are needed before the borrowing estimate is reliable?
  • What assumptions are being made about income, debts, deposit and property value?
  • What could delay assessment or settlement after lodgement?

The source page says partner brokers hold an Australian Credit Licence or operate as credit representatives under a licensee. It also refers to broker best interests duty and AFCA external dispute resolution. Those protections help, but a borrower still needs clear written reasoning before choosing a loan.

Timing, Pre-Approval And Settlement

Pre-approval is described as conditional approval that helps estimate how much a lender is likely to lend before house hunting. The Bendigo guide says typical validity is around 90 days, depending on the lender. Treat it as a planning tool, not final approval for any property.

Application to settlement is described as typically taking about 4 to 6 weeks, depending on the lender and circumstances. Ask what happens during that period: document collection, lender assessment, valuation if required, loan offer review, signing and settlement coordination.

If your purchase or refinance has tight timing, ask which tasks are yours. Complete payslips, bank statements, identification and current liability details can reduce back and forth. The broker should explain when an answer is conditional, when it is final, and what could trigger reassessment.

Comparing Loan Structures

Loan comparison should begin with the job the loan must do. A first buyer may value certainty and eligibility checks. A refinancer may focus on repayments, fees, debt consolidation and whether an offset account is useful. An investor may need to compare cash flow, equity use and the difference between interest only and principal and interest repayments.

The Bendigo source page specifically mentions variable versus fixed rates, offset accounts, interest only versus principal and interest, and existing equity. Each feature can help or hurt depending on borrower behaviour. A lower headline rate can still be a poor fit if the structure, fees or conditions do not match the goal.

Ask the broker to explain the rejected options as well as the preferred loan. Clear rejection reasons often reveal whether the recommendation is based on lender policy, serviceability, loan features or timing.

  1. Define the goal. State whether you are buying, refinancing, investing or seeking pre-approval before lenders are compared.
  2. Prepare documents. Gather income, deposit, identification, debts and current loan details so estimates are based on evidence.
  3. Compare structures. Ask how fixed, variable, offset, interest only and principal and interest options affect your goal.
  4. Confirm the pathway. Check what happens from application to settlement, including conditions, signing steps and timing dependencies.
