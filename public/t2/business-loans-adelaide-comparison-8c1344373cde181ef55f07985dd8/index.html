Facility types and when each fits

Term loans provide a lump sum repaid over an agreed schedule. They can be secured against property or business assets, or unsecured for smaller amounts. Equipment finance is asset backed: the equipment serves as security, which is why trades, hospitality and manufacturing often use it when a productive asset is needed without draining working capital. Cash flow facilities are revolving credit that bridges the gap between invoices issued and payments received, suiting seasonal and project based operators. Commercial property loans are assessed on rental income, lease tenure and loan to value ratios.

The comparison point is not only rate. It is whether the facility matches the asset life, the repayment rhythm and the risk the borrower is prepared to carry.

Secured versus unsecured in plain terms

Secured loans allow higher borrowing amounts at lower rates because the lender has recourse to an asset. Real property is the most common security in South Australia, while business equipment and debtor ledgers qualify with some lenders. Unsecured facilities carry a higher rate and shorter term, but they do not put property at risk, so they can suit businesses with strong cash flow and limited physical assets.

Both types are still assessed on time in business, revenue consistency, existing debts and director creditworthiness. A lower advertised rate is not the whole answer if valuation time, covenants or guarantor exposure slow the deal down.

How lenders assess Adelaide applications

Most lenders focus on four areas. Financial statements: two years of tax returns and BAS are standard, with some low doc options for sole traders. Cash flow evidence: bank statements showing regular deposits and manageable outgoings. Existing debt load: ATO payment plans, lease commitments and guarantor debts all count. Credit and industry: defaults can restrict mainstream options, while some specialist lenders work with impaired profiles; higher risk sectors may find non bank lenders fill the gap.

For equipment specifically, chattel mortgage, finance lease and hire purchase have different GST and accounting treatment, so the structure should suit the reporting setup before applying.

Common questions

Startups or businesses seeking larger facilities may be asked to provide projections, though requirements differ by lender.
