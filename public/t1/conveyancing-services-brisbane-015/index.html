Conveyancing Services Brisbane Explained

Brisbane Conveyancers describes fixed-fee property settlement handled by Queensland Law Society-member solicitors, with contract review, searches, transfer duty calculation and PEXA settlement management. For a buyer, those tasks help test the contract before settlement obligations tighten. For a seller, they support contract preparation, Form 2 seller disclosure and settlement handling.

A useful file starts with the signed or draft contract, the property details, settlement date, finance position if relevant and any special conditions. The solicitor can then identify which searches fit the matter, what must be signed, how duty will be arranged and what the lender or other party still needs.

Fee clarity should be checked early. A fixed professional fee is not the same as the final amount payable, because searches, registration, government charges and transfer duty sit separately.

Who This Applies To

This guide is for Brisbane buyers, sellers and transferring owners who need legal handling from contract to settlement. The source material names first home purchases, investment sales, off-the-plan contracts, auction purchases, commercial property and family title transfers, so the questions below focus on those situations.

  • First home buyers: ask how contract conditions, transfer duty questions, grant or concession questions and lender steps will be explained.
  • Sellers: ask what information is needed for the contract and Form 2 seller disclosure before signing.
  • Auction buyers: arrange contract review before bidding, because auction purchases are stated not to have a cooling-off period.
  • Off-the-plan buyers: ask how disclosure documents and sunset clauses will be reviewed.
  • Commercial parties: ask how leases, GST, due diligence and settlement will be handled.

Reading A Fixed-Fee Quote

The source FAQ says routine professional fees are typically a few hundred to around a thousand dollars, with government charges and disbursements such as title searches and registration added separately. That means the written quote should show the professional fee, likely third-party charges and any work that may fall outside a routine matter.

Compare quotes by scope, not just by the headline figure. Ask whether contract review, search ordering, duty arrangements, lender liaison, settlement booking and post-settlement confirmation are included. If the contract has unusual conditions, commercial features, off-the-plan terms or transfer issues, ask whether the fee changes before you rely on it.

Cooling Off, Auctions And Signing Timing

Timing is one of the main reasons to seek review before signing. The source FAQ states that most Queensland residential contracts have a 5 business day cooling-off period, starting when the buyer receives the contract signed by both parties. It also states a termination penalty of up to 0.25% of the purchase price may apply during cooling-off.

Auction contracts need earlier attention because the same FAQ states cooling-off does not apply to property bought at auction. Before bidding, check the contract terms, deposit arrangements, settlement date, finance assumptions and any searches that should be ordered.

Off-the-plan contracts need a different set of questions. The service material identifies sunset clauses and disclosure rules, so buyers should ask what is being promised, what may change before completion and when settlement is expected to occur.

Documents And Next Questions

Before requesting a quote, prepare the contract if available, property address or suburb, whether you are buying, selling, transferring or reviewing, the expected settlement date and any lender details. If the matter involves an auction, off-the-plan contract, commercial premises or family transfer, state that at the start.

Ask who provides legal advice, how documents will be signed, how PEXA settlement will be coordinated and when funds must be ready. A clear answer should identify the solicitor role, the key dates, the searches to be ordered and the items still dependent on the lender, the other party or a search result.

  1. Collect the contract. Gather the signed or draft contract, property details, settlement date and any special conditions before seeking legal review.
  2. Separate fee types. Ask which amount is the fixed professional fee and which items are searches, registration, duty or other disbursements.
  3. Check timing rules. Confirm cooling-off, auction, finance and settlement dates before relying on any exit or extension.
  4. Confirm the solicitor role. Ask who provides legal advice and who will communicate with the lender, agent and other party.
  5. Track settlement tasks. Keep a checklist for searches, duty, signing, funds, lender readiness and PEXA settlement steps.

Common questions

What does a conveyancer do for a Brisbane buyer? The source FAQ says the work includes contract review, title and property searches, advice on conditions and dates, transfer duty arrangements, lender liaison and settlement management so ownership transfers correctly.

Is a fixed-fee quote the full cost? Not always. The source material separates fixed professional fees from government charges, searches, registration and transfer duty, so the written quote should show what is included and what remains separate.

Do Queensland buyers always get a cooling-off period? No. The source FAQ says most residential contracts have a 5 business day cooling-off period, but it does not apply to auction purchases.

What penalty can apply during cooling-off? The source FAQ says a penalty of up to 0.25% of the purchase price may apply if a buyer terminates during cooling-off.

Why is PEXA mentioned in settlement? The source material says settlements are completed online through PEXA, the digital platform used to lodge documents and transfer ownership.
