Match the fence to the job it must do

Do not begin with the cheapest material. Begin with the function. Steel panel fencing is framed as a boundary option where coastal wind exposure matters. Timber suits many residential and hinterland settings. Aluminium slat is described as a front fence and privacy screen choice that fits coastal architecture. Pool fencing is different from an ordinary boundary fence: pool barriers are compliance-led work built to the Queensland pool safety standard, with the inspection pathway confirmed by a licensed pool safety inspector. Rural fencing is separated because acreage boundaries can raise different QBCC scope questions.

Ask what assumptions sit behind post spacing, footings, gate hardware and finishes. A quote that ignores function may look neat while hiding the allowances that decide value.

Talk early with a neighbour

For a shared line, treat the fence as a building decision and a neighbour process. Queensland's Neighbourhood Disputes (Dividing Fences and Trees) Act 2011 gives adjoining owners considering a sufficient dividing fence a formal path. A preferred premium material, extra height or upgraded finish is not automatically shared where it goes beyond what is sufficient.

  1. Record proposed position, material, height and scope before locking the look.
  2. Get at least one quote and a written description of the work.
  3. If agreement fails, use a notice to contribute, allow one month to agree, then note the possible QCAT application within two months.

Check the boundary before finalising panels, ask how retaining affects combined height, and on a corner block ask whether sight lines alter front or side treatment.

Turn a metre rate into line items

A metre rate is a planning tool only. The stronger test is process: clear scope, a sensible site measure, visible assumptions and written terms before work is booked. Two quotes can cover the same length yet differ on access, removal, footing conditions, old posts, disposal, making good, returns, corners, gate automation and exclusions.

Use a project range calculator as a conversation starter, not a site measure or fixed offer. When the written quote arrives, compare what is included, what is excluded, and whether removal and disposal of the existing fence are stated plainly. Licensing belongs in the same conversation: for work that requires licensing, check a current QBCC licence with the right scope.

Who this beginner checklist suits

This angle suits Noosa and Sunshine Coast owners moving from rough ideas to a written scope: replacing an old dividing fence, planning a pool barrier, choosing a front privacy screen, or mapping a rural boundary on acreage. It is less useful where a final surveyed boundary, approved drawings and a signed specification already exist; then the task is contract comparison rather than early planning.

For everyone else, keep the short brief practical: describe the line, state the purpose, note exposure, old fence condition and any neighbour, pool, slope or acreage factor. Locality should explain the job, not decorate it.

  1. Describe the line. Note suburb, run, access, old fence state and purpose.
  2. Choose by function. Match material to privacy, safety, containment or street frontage.
  3. Handle the boundary. Confirm position, retaining and corner sight lines early.
  4. Compare written scope. Check removal, footings, gates, making good and exclusions.
  5. Verify compliance. Use the pool inspector pathway and QBCC licence checks where required.

Common questions

What should a first fencing brief include? A short site note is enough: fence line, suburb, purpose, exposure, old fence condition, access limits, and whether a pool, slope, neighbour or acreage boundary is involved. That gives contractors a boundary to price rather than a vague length.

Is a metre rate enough to compare quotes? No. Treat it as a planning figure only. Compare written scope, site measure, assumptions, access, removal, disposal, footings, returns, corners, gate hardware, exclusions and written terms before work is booked.

When do extra compliance checks apply? Pool barriers are compliance-led work under the Queensland pool safety standard, with inspection by a licensed pool safety inspector. Some rural acreage work can raise QBCC scope questions, and licensed work needs a current QBCC licence with the right scope.
