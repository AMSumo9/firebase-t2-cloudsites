Three planning numbers should be held in mind before any rate discussion: the APRA 3% buffer that sits on top of the actual rate during serviceability assessment, the usual minimum of 5% genuine savings, and LMI once borrowing passes 80% of the property value. These figures guide what is realistic, yet they remain planning inputs rather than approval promises.

Documents and numbers to assemble

A lender-ready file shortens the path from first call to assessment. Worth gathering early:

  • Income evidence and a clear record of existing debts and living expenses, since serviceability depends on the full position rather than salary alone.
  • A genuine savings history showing how the deposit has been built, because most lenders ask for at least 5% held as genuine savings.
  • For a refinance, the current loan balance, repayment type, offset balance, any fixed-rate status and the purpose of any equity release.
  • For self-employed applicants, the choice between full-doc and alt-doc treatment makes documentation the central question.
  • For construction plans, the build contract and expectations around progress-payment drawdowns should be ready before lender discussions.

Questions that test process quality

The published service points for this niche include a wide lender panel, panel comparison and no direct broker fee for most standard loans, with any complex-scenario fee disclosed in writing up front. Those claims can be tested with direct questions:

  • Which lenders were considered for this scenario, and why was the shortlist narrowed that way?
  • Which product features were excluded from consideration, and what would bringing them back change?
  • Does any direct fee apply here, and will that be confirmed in writing before proceeding?
  • Is LMI being capitalised into the loan amount, and how does an offset account alter the product cost?
  • Do fixed or variable repayments better suit the stated plan, given the buffer applied at assessment?

A narrow recommendation can still be suitable, but the reasoning behind it should be clear enough to follow.

Scenario planning for different borrower goals

Each borrower type enters the meeting with a different priority. A first home buyer usually leads with deposit evidence, whether LMI may apply, and whether a government scheme question should be tested with the lender. A refinancer should start from the existing loan rather than the new rate, weighing the current balance, equity, discharge costs and the reason for any equity release. An investor needs the structure assessed for cash flow and future borrowing capacity, because offsets, interest settings and serviceability shape what comes next.

Debt recycling, rentvesting, trust loans and SMSF loans appear in this topic family as specialist paths, so they warrant verification rather than assumptions. Keeping the comparison grounded

A lower repayment is not automatically the better outcome when the borrower needs offset access, construction drawdowns, bridging finance or documentation flexibility. The practical test is whether the recommended structure still works when income, expenses or rates move. Borrowers sitting near a deposit or serviceability limit should avoid leaning on a single calculator estimate, because lender settings vary and the buffer already tightens the numbers.

  5. Confirm fees and licensing in writing. Ask how the broker is paid, whether any direct fee applies to the scenario, and have any complex-case fee disclosed in writing up front, alongside licence or credit-representative status.

Common questions

Is a broker fee charged directly to the borrower? For most standard home loans the broker fee is not paid directly by the borrower. Where a scenario is complex, a fee may apply and it should be disclosed in writing up front, so asking about payment structure early is sensible.

How much deposit should a Western Sydney borrower plan for? Most lenders expect at least 5% held as genuine savings. Reaching 20% of the property value, using a guarantor or qualifying for the First Home Guarantee may remove the need for LMI, which usually applies above 80% loan-to-value.

What rate is used when lenders test repayment capacity? Lenders assess repayments at the actual rate plus the APRA 3% prudential buffer. Income, existing debts and living expenses are also assessed, so the result can differ from a single online calculator estimate.
