Seo Audit Melbourne Explained

A site audit is a structured diagnostic review that identifies the technical and content barriers preventing a site from ranking well in organic search. Unlike a monthly traffic report, it looks at underlying causes. A thorough audit covers six areas: crawlability, indexation, Core Web Vitals, on-page signals, internal link structure, and backlink profile.

The output is a prioritised action list that sequences fixes by impact, so the business knows what to address first. You can hand it to an internal developer, use it to brief another agency, or use it as the starting point for a retainer.

Crawlability and indexation barriers

Crawlability and indexation problems are the most common findings in Melbourne site audits, yet they are often invisible to site owners because the site looks normal in a browser. A search bot does not behave like a human visitor.

  • Misconfigured robots.txt files that block key directories
  • Redirect chains that burn crawl budget before reaching the destination
  • JavaScript-rendered content that some crawlers do not fully process

Indexation problems arise when a site accumulates duplicate versions of content across HTTP and HTTPS, www and non-www, or paginated category pages. If canonicalisation signals conflict, the wrong version may be indexed.

Core Web Vitals and page experience

Page experience signals, including Core Web Vitals, are part of Google's ranking systems. For Melbourne businesses in competitive categories like legal services, trades, or finance, improvements in page-experience scores can shift a result from the second page to the top positions.

An audit identifies which pages fail Core Web Vitals thresholds and traces the cause. Slow Largest Contentful Paint is often caused by unoptimised images or render-blocking scripts. Layout shift frequently comes from ads or embeds that load after the visible content. For businesses targeting suburbs such as Box Hill, Carlton, or Glen Waverley, mobile page speed matters especially, as a significant share of local searches originate on mobile.

Pricing and process

Project-based audits typically start from around $1,500, as noted by The SEO Company Melbourne. Price varies with site size, backlink review depth, and whether competitive gap analysis is included.

A standard audit follows a clear sequence: crawl the site and export a URL inventory; identify indexation and crawlability issues; review Core Web Vitals using both field data and controlled tests; assess on-page signals across priority pages; evaluate the backlink profile for quality and risk. All findings are compiled into a prioritised report with recommended fixes and effort estimates.

A reputable provider does not hold findings hostage to a sign-up. You can use the report to act on however suits the business.

Who should commission a site audit

A site audit is most valuable in four situations: before starting a retainer, it establishes a baseline and ensures budget is directed at actual problems; after a ranking drop, following a migration, platform change, or algorithm update, an audit identifies whether the cause is technical, content-related, or both; before a site rebuild, moving to a new CMS without an audit risks losing existing indexation and the authority built in the current backlink profile; when results have plateaued, if rankings have stalled despite content production, a technical audit often surfaces a crawl or indexation constraint suppressing the whole site.

  1. Crawl the site. Export a full URL inventory and identify crawl errors, redirect chains, and robots.txt blocks.
  2. Check indexation. Confirm the right pages are indexed and that duplicate or thin content is not diluting authority.
  3. Review Core Web Vitals. Use field data and controlled tests to find pages failing load speed, layout stability, or responsiveness thresholds.
  4. Assess on-page signals. Audit title tags, heading structure, meta descriptions, structured data, and keyword alignment on priority pages.
  5. Evaluate backlinks. Analyse the quality, relevance, and diversity of external links pointing to the site and flag risk.
