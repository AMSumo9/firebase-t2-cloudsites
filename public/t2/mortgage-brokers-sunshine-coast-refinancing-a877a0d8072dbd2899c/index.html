Evidence that changes the estimate

The published FAQ lists two recent payslips, three months of personal account statements, identification, current loan or rent details and an idea of property type. Bringing these early lets policy be tested rather than relying on a calculator. For PAYG income, check payslips against bank statement deposits. For debts, list repayments, limits and loans being retained. For a purchase, keep deposit funds separate from costs outside the loan.

  • Collect statements and identification before the callback so the review can test lender policy.
  • For refinancing, bring the existing balance, rate, fees and loan structure.
  • For debt-heavy files, show limits and retained loans even if the aim is a cheaper rate.
  • Treat any borrowing figure as provisional until live serviceability is run.

Who should ask different questions

The guide names first home buyers, refinancers, investors and construction borrowers. A first buyer may need deposit gap help, Queensland First Home Owner Grant paperwork and the Australian Government 5% Deposit Scheme; price caps still apply, so eligibility should be checked before contract. Family guarantor structures and the scheme can reduce or remove lenders mortgage insurance cost only where eligibility and property price caps are met.

  • Investors should ask how rental income is assessed and whether the structure suits another purchase.
  • Ask about interest-only lending, offset accounts and SMSF borrowing only where relevant.
  • Construction borrowers need build documents and staged funding details before assessment.
  • Every borrower should ask what change would make the loan unsuitable before signing.
