Earthmoving Contractors Adelaide Checklist Explained

Most Adelaide earthworks enquiries fit one of six categories named in the source guide: site cuts and foundations, trenching and drainage, clearing and preparation, soil removal and cartage, driveway or shed pad preparation, and pool or tight-access excavation. The category shapes everything that follows, because a pool dig behind an established house carries different access, service and approval questions from a site cut on an open block.

Avoid opening the conversation with a machine name. A machine that performs well on one property can be the wrong choice next door, since levels, soil, entry and spoil destination all bear on suitability. Instead, state the result you want standing when the job is done: a level building pad, a shaped service trench, a graded driveway base, a shed pad at known height, a cleared block, or spoil lawfully removed. A house site or extension may call for survey-led preparation and known building levels, while trenching may need a service route or stormwater shape that avoids known assets.

The brief to write before anyone visits

Before any contractor visit, write a one-page brief. Include:

  • suburb or postcode and the intended finished use
  • approximate dimensions, depth and levels where known
  • photos, plans or sketches showing access and nearby structures
  • ground conditions such as sand, reactive clay, fill or rock
  • whether spoil, rubble or unsuitable surface material must leave site

For driveways and hardstands, record excavation depth, grading, base preparation and whether cartage is included. Photos taken from the street, side access and the work area usually communicate more than a long written description, and they let each contractor price the same job. Access checks that move plant choice and cartage

Access is judged in three dimensions: the entry, the turns and the sky above the machine. The source guide lists turns, overhead clearance, surfaces and the loading route as the checks that change plant choice and cartage assumptions. A wide gate means little if the machine must swing through a sharp corner inside the yard, and an established block with branches or lines overhead needs a different plan from open ground.

  • exact entry width
  • available turning space
  • driveway surface and slope
  • overhead lines or branches
  • the nearest practical loading position

Straightforward access and soil can keep a small backyard dig to a day or two of machine time. Tight laneways and limited entry stretch the schedule because smaller equipment means more passes, different loading arrangements and extra trips to remove spoil. Photograph the route rather than describing it from memory.

Ground, spoil and written inclusions

Sand, reactive clay, fill and rock each carry different implications for plant choice, stability, time and disposal, so treat ground as a method question, not a footnote. Keep the conversation split into clear lines: excavation, cartage, materials, testing, approvals and downstream trade responsibilities. That separation stops a quote from appearing complete when important tasks sit outside it.

  • whether loading, separation and lawful cartage of clean spoil, rubble and excavated material are included
  • whether vegetation, debris and unsuitable surface material are in scope on clearing jobs
  • how finished levels will be confirmed on pads, driveways and retaining preparation
  • who is responsible for compaction, testing and follow-on trade requirements

Engineering, approvals, contractor selection and the final written quote remain separate decisions.

Services, approvals and licence checks

Buried services should be checked against current asset information with site-specific locating before excavation starts, whether the job is trenching, foundations, a pool dig or general clearing. Before work, know who gathers the service information, who books the locating, and how the contractor will respond if unknown services appear.

Approval is scope dependent. The guide warns that a small-looking excavation is not automatically exempt in South Australia, and recommends checking with the relevant council or PlanSA where earthworks could affect neighbouring land, retaining walls, stormwater, protected features or form part of building work.

Licence checks should match the contracted work. There is no single generic earthmover licence covering every task, while pool excavation can involve regulated building work, engineering, approvals and licensed trades. For contracted building work, the source directs readers to verify any required building-work contractor licence through Consumer and Business Services SA.

Who this checklist suits

This checklist suits property owners, builders and project organisers preparing a residential earthworks brief in Adelaide: site cuts, foundations, drainage excavation, driveway bases, shed pads, pool digs, clearing and cartage. It is most valuable before supplier contact, while the brief can still change without variation disputes downstream.

  1. Classify the job. Match your task to one or more of the six work types: site cuts, trenching, clearing, cartage, driveway or shed pads, pool or tight-access excavation.
  2. Record the finished result. Write down the intended use, dimensions, levels if known, and photos or sketches showing access and nearby structures.
  3. Walk the access route. Measure entry width, turning space and overhead clearance, note the surface and slope, and photograph the loading position.
  4. Note ground and spoil. Record expected soil such as sand, reactive clay, fill or rock, and decide whether spoil and rubble must leave site.
  5. Confirm services, approvals and licences. Arrange asset information and locating, check council or PlanSA where required, and verify any building-work contractor licence through Consumer and Business Services SA.
