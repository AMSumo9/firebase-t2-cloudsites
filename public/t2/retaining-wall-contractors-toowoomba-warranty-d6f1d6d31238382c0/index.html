Quote Inputs That Influence Scope

To ensure accurate comparisons between different retaining wall contractors, it is essential that quotes detail the same inclusions. Key elements to compare typically include excavation, foundation or post installation, materials used, drainage systems, backfill processes, handling of corners and curves, spoil removal, engineering requirements, approval costs, and site reinstatement. Drainage is a critical aspect that should be discussed early in the process. Homeowners should note any observed runoff, ponding, washout issues, and potential drainage outlets, allowing the contractor to confirm what can be accommodated during the inspection.

Site access can significantly impact the construction method and cost. Factors such as gate width, the presence of steps, overhead clearances, and the route for removing excavated material (spoil) will affect how materials and equipment are brought to and from the site. If a quote does not explicitly mention access considerations, it is advisable to inquire whether the price may be subject to change following a site visit. For walls featuring corners, curves, or changes in level, confirm whether these elements are included in the quoted price or are considered provisional. Providing detailed photos and measurements helps prevent discrepancies between written proposals that might be pricing different scopes of work.

Approval and Engineering Questions

The Toowoomba guide highlights that approval requirements for retaining walls extend beyond a simple height measurement. Specific triggers for potential approval checks include retained fill exceeding one metre in height, proximity within 1.5 metres of a building or another existing wall, and the presence of surcharge loads. Additionally, infrastructure and fences situated above the wall can introduce further checks. It is important to note that these points do not constitute a final approval decision for a property. The practical next step is to confirm the specific address with a licensed certifier before commencing work, as height, nearby structures, loads, and site constraints are unique to each project.

For walls that are higher, subject to significant loads, or have restrictive site conditions, it is crucial to ask whether engineering, approval processes, and certification steps are included in the quote, excluded, or will be confirmed after a detailed site inspection. A clear exclusion is generally easier to compare than an unstated assumption within a proposal.

  1. Prepare Site Information. Document retained height, surcharge loads, water flow paths, and site access limitations. Take clear photos from multiple angles, showing the wall line, slope, nearby structures, and any existing damage or drainage issues.
  2. Gather Initial Measurements. Estimate the retained height and measure any relevant distances to buildings or other structures. Note down approximate dimensions, but label them as estimates until a professional site visit.
  3. Request Detailed Quotes. Ensure quotes itemise excavation, foundations, materials, drainage, backfill, spoil removal, engineering, approvals, and reinstatement. Ask for clarification on any unclear items.
  4. Inquire About Approvals and Engineering. Ask contractors about potential approval triggers based on height, proximity, and loads. Clarify if engineering, approvals, and certification are included or require separate confirmation.
  5. Confirm Site Visit and Final Pricing. Understand how site access and specific site conditions might affect the final price after an on-site inspection. Discuss any assumptions made in the initial quote.
