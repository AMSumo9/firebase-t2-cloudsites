Settle Scope Before Requesting Any Price

The biggest budget lever is decided before a tradesperson visits: whether the existing layout stays or changes. Keeping the footprint confines spending to visible elements such as joinery fronts, work surfaces, a new splashback and storage upgrades. Reconfiguring the room pulls plumbing and electrical points into play, shifts appliance positions and changes the order in which trades attend.

Four broad scopes cover most local projects. A cosmetic update suits rooms where the plan already works. A full strip-out adds demolition, service rough-in, new joinery, benchtops, splashback and appliance installation. Storage-focused work centres on custom drawers, tall pantries or a butler's pantry designed from the measured plan. Older homes in suburbs such as Camberwell, Kew and Balwyn need a scope that respects original character while delivering a workable modern space. Settle which of these describes the project before comparing prices, because quotes written to different scopes cannot be compared line by line.

Paperwork to Settle Before Demolition Day

Victorian law shapes what should be signed before any strip-out begins. A major domestic building contract over $10,000 requires a written contract under the Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995, and the published service information states that projects run under a written, fixed-scope agreement with appropriate permits plus registered plumbing and electrical trades.

Insurance deserves equal attention. Domestic building insurance applies to residential building work above a threshold commonly cited at $16,000, and the sensible move is confirming the current figure with Victorian authorities against the actual contract value. Before signing, paperwork should show the scope, the price, start and finish dates, staged payments and the process for variations, because variations are where budgets most often drift.

  • Written scope listing every inclusion and exclusion
  • Fixed price with payment stages tied to progress
  • Permits identified where structural or service work is involved
  • Registered plumbers and electricians named for regulated work
  • A variation process agreed in writing

Benchtop Rules That Changed in 2024

Material selection now carries a legal dimension that did not exist a few years ago. Engineered stone containing 1% or more crystalline silica has been banned for manufacture, supply, processing and installation across Australia since 1 July 2024 under the model WHS Regulations administered by Safe Work Australia.

That single change reshapes pre-start planning. Households that assumed a reconstituted stone surface would be available must choose from compliant alternatives: porcelain, sintered stone, natural granite or marble, solid timber, stainless steel, laminate, or low-silica engineered stone under 1% crystalline silica. Because the benchtop feeds directly into the fabrication window, settling the material before contracts are signed protects the whole schedule. Swapping a surface after joinery is under way can stall installation and trigger variation costs.
