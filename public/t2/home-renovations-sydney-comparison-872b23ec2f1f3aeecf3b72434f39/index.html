Structural Change and Approvals

Structural change and approvals are the hidden drivers of renovation cost. The source page explains that approval pathways, structural work and waterproofing should be raised before pricing so the brief is not silently missing work that later becomes variation territory. A straightforward same-layout renovation is usually the most affordable path, while moving plumbing, enlarging the room or using premium tiling and tapware increases the figure. Waterproofing to Australian Standard AS 3740 is a non-negotiable part of any compliant bathroom. A licensed renovator will price the work in writing after the scope is clear. If the house no longer works at all, the source page points toward whole-home renovation, including layout reconfiguration, modern services and finishes, rather than treating each room as a separate patch job. This approach ensures that the written contract before work begins covers all necessary structural and service upgrades.

Comparing Quotes

Comparing renovation quotes requires a clear understanding of what is included. The source page advises that the quoting contractor sets out the scope, price, programme and exclusions in writing so you can decide with full information. One quote may look lower simply because it assumes less structural work, fewer service changes or a narrower finish schedule. Another may be more complete because it has dealt with those issues earlier. To compare fairly, define the project type. State whether the job is whole-home, kitchen, bathroom, extension, granny flat, or heritage or terrace related. List the known constraints. Note access, likely structural change, approval issues and anything unusual about the site. Then ask which items must be resolved before a fixed-price quote can be issued, what sits inside the written scope, and what is left as an exclusion or later decision. This keeps the comparison fair and prevents unexpected costs later.

  1. Define the project type. Determine if you need a whole-home renovation, a specific room update, an extension or a granny flat. This helps identify the likely constraints early.
  2. List known constraints. Note access issues, heritage overlays, structural change requirements and approval pathways. These factors significantly impact the final price.
  3. Request a written quote. Ask for a fixed-price quote that details the scope, price, programme and exclusions. Ensure the builder is licensed and insured.

Common questions

How much does a home renovation cost in Sydney? Costs depend heavily on scope, size and finish level. A cosmetic refresh across a few rooms can sit in the tens of thousands, while a full whole-home renovation with structural change commonly runs from around $150,000 into the several hundred thousands.

What is included in a renovation quote? A comprehensive quote should include the scope of work, the total price, the programme or timeline, and any exclusions. It should also confirm that waterproofing and structural work meet Australian standards.
