Accreditation and Best Interests Duty

Every broker in the network is accredited with the Mortgage and Finance Association of Australia (MFAA) or the Finance Brokers Association of Australia (FBAA). They operate under an Australian Credit Licence. Since 1 January 2021, mortgage brokers must act in the best interests of consumers under the statutory best interests duty, guided by ASIC RG 273. This means the recommendation must be in your interests rather than the lender's. Credit licensees must also belong to the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA) for independent dispute resolution if something goes wrong. This framework provides a safety net and ensures that the advice you receive is professional, unbiased and regulated by national standards.

Key Features to Review

When you compare home loans, do not focus solely on the headline rate. The published page highlights the importance of the comparison rate, which includes fees and charges. Other features to test include an offset account, redraw facility and cashback offers. An offset account can reduce your interest payable by offsetting your loan balance against your savings. A redraw facility allows you to access extra repayments you have made. For refinancers, ask about discharge and valuation costs that should be identified before you switch. Investors should discuss interest-only periods and a tax-aware setup. A good broker will separate feature benefits from the actual cash flow outcome and explain which parts of the recommendation depend on lender policy rather than your longer-term plan.

Pre-Approval and Construction Loans

Timing is critical when bidding or signing. Pre-approval should be secured before you bid or sign. It is typically valid for around 3 months and remains conditional on final checks. This gives you confidence when making an offer on a property. For construction and renovation finance, look for progress-payment construction loans. This is relevant for buyers in Alfredton, Delacombe and Mount Clear. A useful conversation here is less about slogans and more about the build contract and whether the lender is comfortable with the project as described. You need to ensure the lender understands the construction timeline and the specific requirements for progress payments to avoid delays or funding shortfalls during the build.

Who This Applies To

This guide is most useful if your situation falls into one of four buckets: first home buyer, refinancer, investor or construction borrower. A first home buyer may need to ask about low deposit options and the Regional First Home Buyer Guarantee. A refinancer is more likely to test whether a lower rate is actually better once offset, redraw and switching costs are included. An investor should ask how interest-only periods affect the decision. A construction borrower needs to know what must be in place before signing the build contract. If your case sits across more than one bucket, for example a first purchase plus family guarantor support or a refinance before an investment move, that is usually the point where a structured comparison becomes more useful than a quick rate check.
