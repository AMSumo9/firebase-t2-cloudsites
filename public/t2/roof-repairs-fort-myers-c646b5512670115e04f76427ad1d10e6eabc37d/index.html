Repair Versus Replacement

The roof type changes the decision path. Asphalt shingle is described as the most common roof in Fort Myers, and the source page ties shingle work to ventilation plus the heat and storm exposure that shorten service life in Southwest Florida. Tile jobs need a different read, especially where cracked concrete or clay tiles sit within a broader barrel-tile roof system. Metal and low-slope areas also need their own line item. Standing-seam and metal panel roofs are presented as longer-life options with strong wind performance under the Florida Building Code. For additions, lanais and other low-slope sections, check that the quote is talking about TPO, EPDM or modified bitumen rather than treating every surface like shingles. This ensures you are comparing like with like.

Storm Response and Tarping

Keep storm response and permanent work separate after storms. The source page describes emergency tarping and leak control as the first step to limit water entry, then a documented assessment for the permanent repair and any homeowner insurance claim. That separation matters when you compare scope and price. A tarp is not the same decision as a tear-off, and a post-storm quote should say what was found in the shingles, flashing, decking and ventilation once the roof was assessed. If you need inspection paperwork for insurance, ask whether the inspection can also support a wind mitigation report. This documentation helps streamline the claims process with your insurer.

Licensing and Code Checks

Use licence and code checks early. Florida roofing has a paperwork layer as well as a materials layer. The source page says homeowners can verify a contractor's licence at MyFloridaLicense.com before work begins, and it says Lee County jobs have to meet the Florida Building Code high-wind requirements. Use that as a screening step before you talk about finishes or colours. Ask who is licensed for the job, who will provide the written scope, price and terms, and how the quote describes the work from decking and underlayment up to the finished covering when replacement is on the table. If permits are mentioned, ask the contractor to state in writing who is checking that requirement and who is handling it. This protects you from unlicensed work and ensures compliance with local regulations.
