Conveyancing Services Sydney Explained

The published home page for Sydney Conveyancers describes work from contract review to settlement day. It names licensed conveyancers and solicitors, contract checks, searches, transfer duty calculation and electronic settlement through PEXA. Use those items as the baseline when comparing quotes, because a fixed professional fee can still leave searches, disbursements and government charges outside the headline amount.

Buyers usually need the contract reviewed before commitment, especially where an auction date or off-the-plan contract is involved. Sellers need the contract for sale prepared and prescribed disclosure documents attached. A useful first enquiry states the transaction type, whether a contract is available, whether timing is urgent, and whether any grant, lease, GST or family transfer issue is already known.

Who This Applies To

This guide is relevant to the transaction types named in the published material: ordinary buyers, sellers, first home buyers, off-the-plan buyers, auction bidders, family title transfer clients and commercial property clients. Each group should ask different questions rather than using a generic checklist.

  • First home buyers: ask how the First Home Buyers Assistance Scheme and First Home Owner (New Homes) Grant will be checked for the specific purchase.
  • Auction bidders: arrange contract review before bidding, because the published material states NSW auction purchases do not have the usual cooling-off period.
  • Off-the-plan buyers: ask about sunset clauses and disclosure rules, both named in the material.
  • Commercial parties: raise leases, GST, due diligence and commercial settlement at the start.
  • Family transfer clients: ask what title transfer work is included and which charges sit outside the professional fee.

Fixed Fees And Separate Charges

The published wording says routine matters may be quoted on a fixed professional fee, with disbursements extra. Read that distinction carefully. The professional fee is not the whole property cost if searches, registration charges, transfer duty or other government charges are listed separately.

Before engaging anyone for conveyancing services sydney, ask for the quote in writing and check whether it names the included work. The clearest quote separates professional work, expected disbursements and government charges. If the matter changes after review, ask how extra work will be approved before it is charged.

The quote form described in the material asks for suburb or postcode, project details and preferred call time. Those details are useful because a purchase, sale, transfer, off-the-plan contract or commercial matter can involve different documents and questions.

NSW Timing Before Exchange

The published material states that most residential contracts bought by private treaty in NSW have a 5 business day cooling-off period under the Conveyancing Act 1919. It says the period runs from exchange and ends at 5pm on the fifth business day. It also states the cooling-off period does not apply to property bought at auction.

For private treaty buyers, ask what can be reviewed before exchange and what can still be checked during any cooling-off period. For auction buyers, send the contract before bidding. For sellers, contract preparation and disclosure documents need attention before sale steps progress.

PEXA is named as the digital platform used to lodge documents and transfer ownership. Electronic settlement still depends on correct names, title details, lender requirements and settlement instructions before the agreed settlement day.

Conveyancer Or Solicitor

The published material says an NSW matter can be handled by a licensed conveyancer regulated by NSW Fair Trading or by a solicitor. It also says legal work and advice are provided by the independent professional engaged. Choose by transaction needs, not by title alone.

A licensed conveyancer may suit a routine transfer involving contract review, searches, settlement preparation and lodgement. A solicitor may be appropriate where related legal issues sit beside the transfer. The material does not set a hard boundary, so ask whether your facts require broader legal advice.

Put complex features in the first enquiry. Off-the-plan terms, commercial premises, GST, leases and family title transfer can affect who should review the documents and what should be quoted.

What To Prepare Before Asking For Help

A concise brief helps the professional identify scope. Gather the contract if available, the property address or suburb, the transaction type, any auction or settlement date, and any lender, grant, lease or family transfer issue already known.

Buyers should ask about contract review, searches, transfer duty and settlement steps. Sellers should ask about disclosure documents and the contract for sale. Off-the-plan buyers should include sunset clause and disclosure questions in the first brief. Auction bidders should not leave contract review until after bidding, because the published material states NSW auction purchases do not have the usual cooling-off period.

  1. Identify the transaction type. State whether you are buying, selling, transferring, bidding at auction, buying off-the-plan or dealing with commercial premises.
  2. Send the contract early. Provide the contract before exchange or auction where possible so review is not compressed into the settlement period.
  3. Ask for fee separation. Request the professional fee, searches, disbursements, government charges and transfer duty to be shown separately in writing.
  4. Confirm the adviser. Ask whether a licensed conveyancer or solicitor will provide the advice and manage the settlement steps.
  5. Check settlement handling. Confirm how PEXA lodgement, ownership transfer and settlement communication will be coordinated.

Common questions

What does a fixed professional fee leave out? The published material says fixed professional fees can be quoted for routine matters, with government charges, searches, disbursements and transfer duty set out separately. Ask for those items in writing before engaging anyone.

Should an auction contract be reviewed before bidding? Yes. The published material says there is no cooling-off period at auction in NSW, so practical review should happen before the bid.

Do I need a solicitor instead of a conveyancer? The published material says NSW matters can be handled by a licensed conveyancer or a solicitor. Ask which is appropriate if the transaction involves broader legal issues, commercial terms, leases, GST or family title transfer.

What should first home buyers ask about? The published material names the First Home Buyers Assistance Scheme and First Home Owner (New Homes) Grant. Ask how eligibility and settlement steps will be checked for your purchase.
