Key takeaway

Farmers generally choose between three main finance structures: chattel mortgage, commercial hire purchase and finance lease. Chattel mortgages offer ownership from day one and allow GST-registered businesses to claim the input tax credit upfront. Commercial hire purchases spread GST claims over the term while offering eventual ownership, whereas finance leases retain ownership with the lender. Agricultural Machinery Finance Comparison Explained

When conducting an agricultural machinery finance, the structure of the loan determines who owns the asset and how tax is handled. The chattel mortgage allows the business to own the tractor immediately. For those looking for agricultural machinery finance, this structure is common because it allows the GST input tax credit to be claimed on the purchase price upfront. The lender holds a mortgage over the equipment until the loan is repaid.

Commercial hire purchase differs in that the lender buys the tractor and hires it to the business. Ownership transfers only after the final payment. Under this structure, GST is typically claimed over the life of the agreement rather than upfront. A finance lease is distinct because the lender retains ownership throughout the term and leases it to the farmer. At the end of the term, the business may have the option to buy the equipment, extend the lease or return it.

Their appetite varies by asset type, meaning the profile of the machinery, such as a new harvester versus a used utility tractor, affects the available terms. Comparison platforms can assist by matching farm profiles with lenders most likely to approve the application.

Tax Outcomes and Cash Flow

Tax implications are a critical factor when making an agricultural machinery finance. With a chattel mortgage, a GST-registered business claims the GST input tax credit on the purchase price upfront and can depreciate the asset over its effective life. In a commercial hire purchase, GST is claimed across the life of the loan, and depreciation is claimed once ownership transfers. Interest and charges under a hire purchase may be deductible during the agreement.

Finance leases offer different benefits, often resulting in lower regular repayments. While the business does not own the asset during the term, the lease payments are generally tax-deductible. The decision often comes down to cash flow management versus the desire for asset ownership. The structure that appears cheapest monthly might not provide the best after-tax result over the asset's life. This makes a thorough comparison essential. The right agricultural machinery finance option depends on whether you value immediate tax deductions or lower monthly repayments.

Common questions

Which finance structure offers the lowest monthly repayments? Finance leases and structures with a balloon payment at the end of the term typically offer lower regular monthly repayments. A balloon payment defers a portion of the cost to the end of the loan, reducing the ongoing commitment.

Can I finance used machinery through these structures? Yes, lenders fund both new and quality used agricultural machinery. However, the loan term for used equipment is often shorter, reflecting the remaining useful life of the asset.

How does farm trading history affect the interest rate? Lenders heavily weigh the trading history of the farm. Established businesses with two or more years of financials generally secure rates between 6.5 and 9 per cent, whereas newer or specialist operations may face rates exceeding 12 per cent.
