Understanding the loan process and timing

The published process is simple: submit your details, speak with a broker, then review suitable loan options. The harder part is managing the timeline around that process. Bendigo Home Loan Broker notes that pre-approval is typically valid for around 90 days, depending on the lender. This timing is useful as a planning marker. If you are buying now, ask what documents should be ready before pre approval starts, so the lender is assessing a complete picture rather than chasing paperwork later.

Application through to settlement typically takes around four to six weeks, depending on the lender and your circumstances. This window matters if you are refinancing and the timing is driven by an expiring fixed period, a renovation plan, or the need to release equity for another purpose. A short list becomes more valuable when you also know what could delay formal approval, what information the lender still needs, and whether the indicative timing fits your property search or settlement goal.

Costs, fees and broker accreditation

Cost and accountability deserve the same attention as the loan itself. Bendigo Home Loan Broker states that most Australian residential brokers are typically paid commission by the lender rather than by the borrower, and that any exceptions must be disclosed upfront under ASIC rules. This means one of the first questions should be how remuneration is disclosed for your case.

The page also notes that partner brokers hold an Australian Credit Licence or operate as Credit Representatives under a licensee, are commonly accredited with MFAA or FBAA, and are members of AFCA for external dispute resolution. It further notes that mortgage brokers must act in the consumer's best interests, with that duty applying from 1 January 2021. These are not box ticking details. They help you test whether the recommendation process is transparent, whether there is a complaints pathway if something goes wrong, and whether the broker can explain why one option fits your circumstances better than another.

Loan types and features

Once you have a shortlist, keep the comparison tied to the job the loan needs to do. The Bendigo page mentions variable and fixed options, offset accounts where they fit, and for investors, the choice between interest only and principal and interest. Those are the useful discussion points, not a broad promise that every borrower should pick the lowest advertised rate.

Ask each time: why is this lender on the list, what features are doing the work, and what assumptions were used about income, deposit, equity or future plans. A refinancer may care more about consolidation or future access to equity than a marginal change in rate. An investor may care more about structure and portfolio fit than a single feature. A buyer seeking pre approval may care most about whether the lender can move cleanly from conditional approval to formal approval.

First home buyer considerations

Buying your first home in Bendigo can be overwhelming. A broker helps you understand the Victorian First Home Owner Grant and the federal First Home Guarantee administered by Housing Australia. Eligible first home buyers may purchase with as little as a 5 percent deposit without LMI, subject to price caps that are reviewed annually. This pathway is a key feature for first home buyers who do not have a 20 percent deposit.

The focus should be on which lender setup, timing window and borrower protections fit your purchase plan. A broker can model scenarios against each lender's policy before you sign anything. They can also help you understand the difference between a standard variable rate loan and a loan with an offset account, which can reduce the interest you pay over time. This scenario first approach is what makes a local conversation useful. It turns the brief into something a broker can actually assess against lender policy instead of giving you a broad list of products with no clear fit.
