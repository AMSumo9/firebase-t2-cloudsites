Key takeaway

Most Adelaide kitchen renovations run for 8 to 14 weeks once the project is underway, with quotes for the work commonly sitting between $8,000 and $80,000 plus depending on scope. A facelift that keeps the existing layout sits at the shorter and cheaper end, while a remodel with design work, custom cabinetry, stone benchtops, splashbacks and appliance installation takes longer. Cabinetry dimensions, benchtop templating, splashback installation and the order of licensed trades all shape the calendar, so the schedule is worth planning as carefully as the budget.

Kitchen Renovations Adelaide Timeline Explained

The published range for a complete kitchen project is 8 to 14 weeks, and that window can cover far more than demolition and installation. Design discussion, quoting and ordering all consume time before any tradesperson arrives, so ask any renovator to separate pre-site work from on-site work when they propose dates. A realistic phase plan usually looks like this:

  • Design and quoting: measuring the room, settling the layout, selecting cabinetry, benchtops, splashbacks and appliances.
  • Ordering: cabinetry and benchtop materials booked, with lead times locked before demolition starts.
  • Rough-in: demolition, then any electrical and plumbing repositioning needed for the new plan.
  • Cabinetry install: cabinets positioned and levelled, since accurate cabinet dimensions anchor everything that follows.
  • Benchtops: stone surfaces templated only after cabinets are in place, then fabricated and fitted.
  • Splashbacks and finishes: glass or tile splashbacks follow benchtops, then tiling and final detailing.
  • Appliance fit-off: coordinated electrical and plumbing connections so the kitchen is usable.

Who this schedule applies to

Timeline pressure looks different depending on the property. Owners of compact kitchens often want the job finished quickly, and better drawers, pantry runs, splashbacks and benchtops may deliver that without touching the layout. Families planning an open plan conversion need more patience, because services, structural work and adjoining living areas must all be managed together. Owners of character homes in inner suburbs or the Adelaide Hills should allow time before design is finalised, since heritage context can affect approval checks and the trades involved. Anyone weighing up a full remodel needs to know who designs the plan, who builds the joinery, and who coordinates each licensed trade, because gaps between those parties are where weeks disappear.

What stretches or shortens your schedule

The wide quote range of $8,000 to $80,000 plus exists because a finish refresh and a bespoke joinery remodel are very different purchases. Scope level is the biggest driver of both cost and duration:

  • Facelift: retained layout, replacement doors, benchtops, splashbacks and handles, so trades and lead times stay modest.
  • Same layout renovation: some services stay put, but cabinetry, surfaces and appliances change, adding ordering and fit-off time.
  • Full remodel or open plan change: design work, custom joinery, stone surfaces and several trades sequenced in order, which pushes the calendar toward the longer end.

Itemised inclusions also protect the timeline. Ask whether demolition, rubbish removal, tiling, plumbing, electrical work and finishing sit inside the quote, which selections are provisional, and when benchtop measurement happens, because each allowance that stays vague is a delay waiting to surface.

Approvals and licensing checks before work starts

Some kitchen work needs approval, handled where required in line with PlanSA guidance and the Planning and Design Code. Before committing to a final design, ask whether the proposed work triggers approval, who checks that requirement, and whether structural changes or a heritage listing affect the sequence. Before engagement, homeowners can verify SA building work contractor licensing and supervisor registration through Consumer and Business Services. Industry signals also matter: the Housing Industry Association publishes standards and Master Builders South Australia offers accreditation, so it is reasonable to ask how those benchmarks apply to design, manufacture and installation, and which licensed trade covers each part of the job. Protecting the timeline during ordering and install

Sequence is where kitchen schedules most often slip. Cabinetry needs final dimensions. Benchtops cannot be templated until cabinets are positioned. Splashbacks follow benchtops. The electrician, plumber, tiler and stonemason each need a defined order and clear access. Before accepting a start date, confirm exactly what must be finalised before ordering begins. Late changes are the other common culprit: switching sink size, cooktop type, splashback choice or pantry configuration after ordering can force rework to cabinetry, new benchtop cut outs and rebooked trades. Photograph the room from every corner and list points of friction such as poor lighting, awkward appliance clearances, limited bench space, wasted corner storage, no landing zone near the cooktop, or a pantry that cannot hold a normal grocery shop. Those notes give the designer and cabinet maker concrete evidence to quote against, which keeps decisions front-loaded and the schedule intact.

  1. Define the scope level. Decide whether the job is a facelift, a same layout renovation or a remodel, since that choice sets the likely duration and quote range.
  2. Record the current kitchen. Photograph every corner and note friction points so design decisions happen before ordering, not after.
  3. Check approvals and licensing. Confirm whether PlanSA approval guidance applies and verify contractor licensing and supervisor registration with Consumer and Business Services.
  4. Lock selections before ordering. Finalise sink, cooktop, splashback, pantry and appliance choices so cabinetry and benchtop orders are accurate the first time.
  5. Confirm the trade sequence. Agree the order of cabinetry, benchtop templating, splashbacks and licensed trades before a start date is accepted.

Common questions

Can an Adelaide kitchen renovation be finished faster than eight weeks? The published typical window is 8 to 14 weeks, and shorter results are more realistic for a facelift on a retained layout with few trades. A remodel involving design, custom joinery, stone benchtops and coordinated trades needs the longer end, because ordering and sequencing cannot be safely compressed.

What causes the most common timeline delays? Late changes are the biggest risk. Altering sink size, cooktop type, splashback choice or pantry configuration after cabinetry is ordered can require new benchtop cut outs and rebooked trades. Vague quote allowances and unresolved approval questions can also hold up the start of on-site work.
