Bathroom Renovation Camberwell Common Mistakes Explained

The useful starting point is not the cheapest number. It is whether the quote treats the room, the house and the approvals as one connected job. For a broader overview, see bathroom renovation camberwell guidance, then return to the risk points below.

Camberwell has many Federation and Edwardian homes, so a bathroom that looks fine in isolation can still feel wrong against original cornices, narrow halls, timber floors or older service routes. A proposal should say whether it is refreshing finishes, improving storage, shifting the layout, or building a more private ensuite. If those aims are blurred, two builders can price very different jobs while using similar words.

The common mistake is comparing headline totals before the brief is stable. A vanity can be a simple swap in one home and a character decision in another. A shower can be cosmetic where plumbing stays put, but structural where walls, drainage or ventilation move. The fix is a short written brief that names the room type, the desired finish level and any period-home constraint.

Mistake 1: treating the budget band as the job description

Three bands help, but only after inclusions are aligned. A small bathroom or ensuite refresh is listed at $10,000 to $20,000. A standard full renovation is listed at $15,000 to $35,000. A luxury upgrade is listed at $50,000 to $80,000 or more. Those figures are planning markers, not a promise that similar totals mean similar work.

  • Refresh: best where layout change is limited and storage or finish quality is the real problem.
  • Full renovation: better where demolition, waterproofing, plumbing, tiling, fixtures, vanities and finishes must be coordinated as one build.
  • Luxury brief: suited to freestanding baths, heated flooring, rainfall showerheads, designer tapware and custom joinery from the outset.

Labour is listed at 40 to 60 per cent of total cost. That is why a low total can mislead when demolition, waterproofing or fit-off items are only partly described. Ask each builder to separate materials, labour, demolition, waterproofing and final fit-off in the same format.

Mistake 2: scheduling only the trades, not the approvals

Onsite work for a standard renovation is listed at 2 to 4 weeks. Planning and permit approvals can add another 2 to 6 weeks where required. Luxury bathrooms with structural changes can take 6 to 8 weeks. The planning error is to ask for one date when the job really has two clocks.

Request the expected pre-start period and the expected onsite period as separate lines. The first covers selections, permits, structural or plumbing approvals and site readiness. The second covers demolition through fit-off. This matters for family routines, especially where the only bathroom is being rebuilt or where period-home details slow decisions.

Where walls, services or drainage change, permits should be treated as part of the programme rather than paperwork at the end. A builder who prices the room but not the approval path may look faster on paper while leaving the homeowner exposed to delay once work begins.

Mistake 3: assuming compliance is implied

Waterproofing should be named in the written scope as AS 3740:2021, because it is concealed once the room is finished. The same discipline applies to people and insurance. Plumbing is to be handled by VBA-registered plumbers, and electrical work by Energy Safe Victoria licensed electricians.

Domestic Building Insurance is listed for work over $16,000. Domestic building work over $10,000 in Victoria requires Registered Building Practitioners. These are threshold checks before a deposit, not details to chase after tiles are chosen. A contract pack should show a fixed price, a detailed written contract and warranty language that matches the scope, including workmanship, waterproofing and manufacturer cover for fixtures and fittings.

If a proposal is strong on mood images but vague on who carries compliance responsibility, keep asking. The safest quote is not the one with the longest brochure. It is the one that states the standard, the licence pathway and the insurance point without prompting.

Who this checklist suits

This angle fits four briefs: a compact ensuite, a standard family bathroom, a luxury retreat, or an update inside a Federation or Edwardian home. Each can be sensible, but they should not be priced as if interchangeable. A space-efficiency job is judged by storage, clearances and durable finishes. A full rebuild is judged by coordination. A period-home job is also judged by how comfortably the new room sits beside older detail.

Take a one-page brief into every quote request. List the room type, whether the layout must change, whether storage is failing, whether the design must respect period fabric, and whether the finish level is standard or premium. Then ask for like-for-like inclusions, two dates and compliance evidence. If one quote cannot answer those points clearly, treat that as information too.

  1. Write the brief. Name the room, layout needs, storage problem, period-home constraints and finish level before seeking prices.
  2. Classify the job. Decide whether the work is mainly refresh, full strip-out and rebuild, or a premium brief with custom joinery.
  3. Demand comparable quotes. Ask for fixed pricing with inclusions split into labour, demolition, waterproofing, plumbing, electrical, tiling and fit-off.
  4. Check two dates. Separate the pre-start approval and selection period from the onsite build window.
  5. Verify the safety net. Confirm AS 3740:2021 waterproofing, VBA-registered plumbing, licensed electrical work and the correct insurance threshold.

Common questions

Can two quotes with similar totals still describe different jobs? Yes. One may price a refresh with minor upgrades, while another includes demolition, new plumbing, waterproofing, tiling and final fit-off. Labour is listed at 40 to 60 per cent of total cost, so compare inclusions before comparing totals.

What should be confirmed before any deposit is paid? Confirm that waterproofing is stated as AS 3740:2021, plumbing is by VBA-registered plumbers, electrical work is by Energy Safe Victoria licensed electricians, and the correct insurance and registration thresholds are addressed for the project value.

Why ask for separate pre-start and onsite periods? A standard build phase is listed at 2 to 4 weeks, while planning or permits can add 2 to 6 weeks where required. Structural luxury work can run 6 to 8 weeks, so one blended date hides the part the household feels most.
