Faults, Hazards And Stop Points

The source page sets a firm boundary: describe the symptom and leave fixed wiring alone. A switch that will not reset, a hot outlet or unexpected power loss deserves a safe assessment.

For an immediate hazard, contact emergency services or the electricity distributor. The enquiry page says it cannot dispatch help, and submitting details does not confirm availability, timing or acceptance of work.

Useful notes are simple and factual. Write down when the issue started, what was being used at the time, whether any recent appliance or building change happened, and whether the issue affects one room, one circuit or a wider area. Do not remove covers, open a board or interfere with fixed wiring to gather information.

Switchboards, Safety Switches And Alarms

The published FAQ distinguishes safety switches from circuit breakers. A safety switch monitors leakage of electrical current and cuts power when that fault is detected. A circuit breaker primarily protects wiring and appliances. Some devices combine both functions, so unclear board labels are a question for a licensed electrician.

For switchboard assessment, frame the enquiry around capacity, circuit protection and upgrade decisions. Mention nuisance tripping, added loads, old labels or a planned renovation if any of those facts apply.

For smoke alarms, the source page says compliant alarms are photoelectric, interconnected and meet AS 3786-2014. It also says power supply and replacement choice depend on the dwelling and existing alarm arrangement. Property type, current alarm locations and safe photos are more useful than guessing the compliance answer.

Planned Installations And Quote Inputs

Power points, fixed lighting, ceiling fans, outdoor lighting, split system air conditioning and EV charger installation all need clear quote inputs. The enquiry page asks for suburb, job type, safe description, access and nearby structures. Photos can be provided later if a provider requests them.

For residential work, describe the room, wall or ceiling location, what is already installed and whether building work is happening nearby. For commercial work, include the type of space and any access constraint for trading areas.

If the enquiry involves air conditioning or an EV charger, ask the responding provider what licensing applies to each part of the job. Keep the request factual: what is planned, where it will go, what is already there and what outcome is needed.
