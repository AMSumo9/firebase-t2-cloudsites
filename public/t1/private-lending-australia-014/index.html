Private Lending Australia Explained

Bottom Line Finance describes its role as arranging private lending and construction development funding through third party non-bank and private lenders for developers and asset backed borrowers. The key question is narrower than a home loan comparison: is the funding commercial or business purpose, what asset supports it, and how will the facility be repaid?

The published page says the provider does not arrange consumer credit or owner occupied home loans. A household borrower comparing a home loan should start with ASIC Moneysmart home loan guidance. A developer, commercial borrower or asset backed borrower can use this guide to prepare a concise funding brief before speaking with a broker or lender.

  • State whether funds are for acquisition, construction, bridging, refinance or release of equity.
  • Describe the security, such as residential, commercial, industrial, specialised, childcare, farm or agribusiness related property.
  • Set out the amount, location and timeline without assuming lender acceptance.
  • Explain the exit, such as sale, refinance, completion or repayment from another event.

Who this applies to

This applies to deals outside ordinary consumer mortgage territory. The published page points to developers, commercial borrowers and asset backed borrowers who do not tick the bank boxes. It also names residential, commercial, industrial, specialised assets, childcare, farm and agribusiness as property or asset categories where structured funding may be explored.

For readers comparing broader private lending options for asset backed borrowers, the same discipline applies. Avoid treating private capital as a vague fallback. Prepare the project numbers, current debt position, approval status if relevant, and the event expected to repay the loan.

Match the structure to the pressure point

The funding label should follow the problem being solved. Construction loans are described as funding a build in progress, with draws released as work is completed. Residual stock loans refinance unsold completed dwellings after practical completion to release equity tied up in finished stock. Bridging finance is short term capital for a timing gap, including settlement before a sale completes.

Mezzanine finance sits behind a senior lender and ahead of equity in the capital stack. Development finance funds the land, build and cost to completion. Private lending is framed more broadly as commercial, business purpose capital from non-bank and private lenders, secured against property or other assets.

A useful brief should say which issue needs funding: finishing works, carrying completed stock, settling before an asset sale, filling a capital stack, refinancing existing debt or acquiring a commercial asset. That purpose shapes the questions a broker can ask about cost, risk and exit.

Questions before accepting terms

The published page uses a value over price lens, saying the cheapest rate is rarely the best outcome and that cost should be weighed against return on equity, time, risk and future deal relationships. Borrowers can turn that into a deal checklist.

  • What is the total funding need, including land, build, costs to complete and interest if those items apply?
  • What security is offered, and how may the lender assess value and saleability?
  • Where does each debt layer sit if mezzanine finance or preferred equity is involved?
  • What event repays the facility, and what is the alternative if that event shifts?
  • Does the structure protect equity, or only solve an immediate settlement issue?
  • What documents are needed for an honest view on fundability?

Location and asset notes

The published page lists Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth, Adelaide, Gold Coast, Sunshine Coast and regional NSW, QLD, VIC and WA as location pages or service areas. Location should be treated as a deal input, not a proof of approval. Asset type, borrower purpose, project stage and exit route still do the heavy lifting.

A townhouse project, apartment tower, land subdivision, commercial acquisition, industrial asset, hotel or short stay asset will raise different lender questions. Childcare and agribusiness are named separately, so the brief may need to explain operator income, rural security or specialised property characteristics rather than relying on a standard residential security summary.

Information to prepare before an enquiry

The source asks borrowers to send the project, numbers and timeline. That is the right level of preparation for private lending australia enquiries because the answer depends on the deal rather than a single rate request.

  1. Project summary: asset, location, current stage and reason funds are needed.
  2. Security summary: ownership, existing debt, valuation status if available and any senior lender position.
  3. Funding request: amount sought, use of funds and likely structure.
  4. Timeline: settlement, build, completion, sale, refinance or other repayment events.
  5. Exit plan: expected repayment path and the alternative if the preferred exit changes.
  1. Define the purpose. Write one sentence explaining why funds are needed and whether the purpose is commercial or business related.
  2. Map the security. List the property or asset offered, existing debt and any senior lender position.
  3. Choose the likely structure. Match the need to construction, residual stock, bridging, mezzanine, development or another named funding line.
  4. Document the exit. Identify the sale, refinance, completion or other event expected to repay the facility.
  5. Test value against cost. Compare the funding cost with time, equity protection, risk and the ability to complete the deal.

Who may be a fit for private capital? The published material points to developers and asset backed borrowers with commercial or business purpose funding needs who do not fit standard bank requirements.
